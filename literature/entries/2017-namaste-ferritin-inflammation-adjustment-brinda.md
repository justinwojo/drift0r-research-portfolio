# Summary

- Ferritin is both an iron-storage marker and a positive acute-phase reactant—so inflammation raises ferritin and can mask iron deficiency.
- BRINDA analyzes how to adjust ferritin using inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, AGP) across populations.
- Shows that unadjusted ferritin underestimates iron deficiency prevalence where inflammation is common.
- Directly supports reading “high-normal ferritin + iron replete labs” cautiously when infection/inflammation is on the differential.

# Key data points

- Ferritin increases with inflammation independent of iron stores.
- Concurrent CRP (± AGP) measurement recommended when interpreting ferritin in inflamed populations.
- Regression/correction approaches can adjust ferritin; alternative is higher deficiency cutoffs during inflammation (as later reflected in WHO guidance).
- Even mild inflammatory biomarker elevations shift ferritin distributions.
- Iron-replete appearance on ferritin alone can be false reassurance if inflammation is present.

# Caveats

- BRINDA is population nutrition science, not a diagnostic algorithm for individual multi-system disease.
- Normal CRP does not fully exclude localized or low-grade inflammation; AGP/other markers not described in the patient capture.
- High-normal ferritin has many causes (metabolic syndrome, liver, alcohol, hemochromatosis spectrum, etc.).
- Not medical advice.
