# Summary

- Narrative review of **biologic complications** of cylindrical-lead SCS implants (as opposed to pure hardware failures).
- Reported overall SCS complication rates historically **high (often cited ~30–40%)**, though many events are device-related (lead migration) rather than life-threatening biologic events.
- Infection is a leading **biologic** reason for explant (surgical site/pocket infection; rare deeper neuraxial infection). MarketScan-type data cited infection ≈3% within 12 months in one large cohort; other series 2–10%.
- Other biologic events: hematoma (pooled incidence low, ~0.8% in some syntheses), wound issues, rare neurologic injury.
- **Does not identify** a recognized syndrome of delayed multi-joint polyarthralgia / systemic “joint crash” 4 weeks after implant as a standard SCS complication—important for differential reasoning.

# Key data points

- Complication taxonomy: hardware vs biologic.
- Infection: ~2–10% across literature; pocket infections most common; can progress to epidural abscess/meningitis/osteomyelitis rarely.
- Lead migration remains the dominant overall complication class in many reviews (often >10–20%).
- Explant drivers: loss of efficacy, infection, hardware failure.
- Timing: many infectious complications present in early postoperative window; loss of efficacy can be later.

# Caveats

- Narrative review, not a systematic meta-analysis of all adverse-event databases.
- Under-reporting of rare events possible; absence of evidence ≠ proof of impossibility.
- Does not deeply cover immune, endocrine, or infectious multi-system disease unmasked by surgery/anesthesia.
- Open-access license details should be confirmed on publisher page if redistributing full text.
- Not medical advice.
