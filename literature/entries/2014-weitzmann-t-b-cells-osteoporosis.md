# Summary

- Reviews osteoimmunology evidence that **activated T and B lymphocytes** promote bone loss via RANKL and related pathways in multiple disease contexts.
- Positions immune dysfunction—not only classical endocrine deficiency—as a contributor to osteoporosis and fracture risk.
- Discusses implications for HIV-associated bone loss, inflammatory disease, and future immunomodulatory therapeutic angles.

# Key data points

- RANKL from immune cells increases osteoclastogenesis; OPG balance modulates net resorption.
- Chronic immune activation (infection, autoimmunity, ART-related immune reconstitution in HIV literature) can uncouple bone remodeling.
- Estrogen deficiency itself has immune-mediated components in experimental models.
- Supports a mechanistic bridge from **persistent infection/inflammation hypotheses → low BMD**, independent of proving any one pathogen.
