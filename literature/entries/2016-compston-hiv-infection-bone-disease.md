# Summary

- Reviews osteoporosis and fracture risk as major comorbidities in people living with HIV.
- Pathophysiology is multifactorial: traditional risks + direct viral/immune-activation effects + antiretroviral therapy contributions.
- Demonstrates a real-world model in which **chronic infection and immune activation produce clinically important systemic bone loss**.
- Useful analogy paper for “low-grade / chronic infection osteoporosis” hypotheses outside HIV.

# Key data points

- Higher rates of low BMD and fractures reported in HIV populations vs controls in multiple studies.
- Chronic immune activation and viral proteins implicated in osteoclast/osteoblast imbalance.
- ART (especially some older regimens) can accelerate BMD decline after initiation.
- Screening and prevention strategies recommended as HIV population ages.
- Separates infection-related from drug-related bone toxicity—both matter.
