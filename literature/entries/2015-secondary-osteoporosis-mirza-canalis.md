# Summary

- Comprehensive review of **secondary osteoporosis**: mechanisms, diagnostic approach, and treatment of major endocrine and non-endocrine causes.
- Covers glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis, hypogonadism, hyperthyroidism, hyperparathyroidism, diabetes, GH deficiency, GI malabsorption, idiopathic hypercalciuria, and other common contributors.
- Emphasizes that secondary causes are present in a large fraction of men with osteoporosis and premenopausal women—mandatory systematic evaluation.
- Practical bridge between densitometry findings and the disease-specific treatments that actually stop bone loss (e.g., fix hypogonadism, reduce calciuria, treat celiac disease).

# Key data points

- Men with osteoporosis have secondary causes in a majority of series—do not stop at “idiopathic.”
- Hypogonadism reduces bone formation and increases resorption; replacing T in true deficiency improves BMD (not a substitute for antiresorptives/anabolics when fracture risk is extreme).
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria associates with negative calcium balance and low BMD; thiazides often reduce urine Ca and may help bone.
- Always evaluate 25-OH D, PTH, calcium, phosphate, TSH, sex steroids, 24-h urine calcium, and targeted tests (celiac, SPEP, cortisol excess) based on clues.
- Treat the underlying disease **and** use osteoporosis pharmacotherapy when fracture risk remains high.

# Caveats

- 2015—does not include romosozumab sequencing or 2024 goal-directed ASBMR positions.
- Not a formal society guideline; evidence grading is narrative.
- Does not address infection, mast cell, or IgG4 contributions to MSK pain.
