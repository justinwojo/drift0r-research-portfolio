# Summary

- Three cases of zinc-induced copper deficiency recognized initially on bone marrow morphology rather than clinical suspicion.
- Marrow findings—while not pathognomonic—are characteristic enough (vacuolization of erythroid/myeloid precursors, sideroblastic changes) to prompt copper/zinc testing.
- Excess zinc ingestion is a key etiology of acquired copper deficiency leading to sideroblastic anemia and neutropenia.
- Message to hematologists and internists: think copper/zinc before committing to a primary MDS diagnosis in the right context.

# Key data points

- Clinical copper deficiency often not suspected until cytopenias trigger marrow exam.
- Confirm with low serum copper (± low ceruloplasmin) and elevated zinc when zinc-induced.
- Hematologic abnormalities reverse with copper repletion and zinc cessation.
- Complements neurologic literature (lit-0242, lit-0244, lit-0245) with a hematopathology entry point.
