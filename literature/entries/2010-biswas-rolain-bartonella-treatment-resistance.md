# Summary

- Narrative review of clinical syndromes caused by *Bartonella* spp. and the corresponding **treatment strategies and drug-resistance issues**.
- Emphasizes that *Bartonella* are highly host-adapted intracellular pathogens; **in vitro MICs do not fully predict clinical cure**, and combination/prolonged therapy is used in severe disease.
- Distinguishes self-limited CSD from life-threatening endocarditis, bacillary angiomatosis, and other disseminated forms requiring specialist antimicrobial plans.

# Key data points

- First-line choices differ by syndrome: e.g., supportive/short azithromycin for uncomplicated CSD vs doxycycline + gentamicin (or other combinations) for endocarditis in historical recommendations.
- Aminoglycosides show bactericidal activity important in severe endovascular infection.
- Intrinsic protection inside erythrocytes/endothelial cells contributes to **treatment failure and need for prolonged courses** more than classical plasmid-mediated resistance in many reports.
- No single “IV multi-month protocol” is universally validated for chronic arthralgia without a defined focus.
