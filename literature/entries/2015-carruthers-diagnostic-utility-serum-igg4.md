# Summary

- Evaluates real-world sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV of elevated serum IgG4 for diagnosing IgG4-RD (190 elevated + 190 normal controls reviewed).
- Elevated IgG4 is sensitive but **poorly specific**; many non-IgG4-RD conditions produce elevations in the same range as mild true disease.
- Doubling the cutoff improves specificity at a large cost to sensitivity; IgG4/total IgG ratio does not meaningfully improve performance.

# Key data points

- Sensitivity of elevated IgG4 for IgG4-RD: **~90%** (65/72 cases elevated; mean in cases 405 mg/dL, range 140–2000).
- Among 308 non-IgG4-RD: 125 elevated (mean **234 mg/dL**, range 135–1180) → specificity **~60%**.
- **PPV ~34%**; **NPV ~96%**.
- Doubling the cutoff: specificity ~91%, sensitivity falls to ~35%.
- Non-RD elevations common; test is better at **ruling out** when normal than ruling in when mildly high.

# Caveats

- Single-center tertiary referral population.
- “Elevated” depends on local ULN (here ~135–140 mg/dL class of assays); patient’s lab ULN ~86 mg/dL yields ~2× ULN but absolute values still modest vs multi-organ RD.
- Not clinical advice.
