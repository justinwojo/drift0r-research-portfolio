# Summary

- Pregnancy-associated osteoporosis (PAO) **case series** (n=7 consecutive women) using mutational analysis in severe bone loss around pregnancy/lactation.
- Uncovered monogenic bone disorders—including novel *LRP5*, *COL1A1*, and *COL1A2* mutations—in **3 of 7** women (series yield, not a transferable population rate).
- Pregnancy can act as a **skeletal stress test** that reveals previously unrecognized genetic bone fragility in a subset of PAO cases.
- Therapeutic emphasis on restoring calcium homeostasis to enable partial recovery; long-term management depends on underlying genotype.

# Key data points

- PAO is rare but often vertebral-fracture–dominant with abrupt BMD decline.
- Genetic findings in this **small female series** support not stopping at “idiopathic” once secondary labs are only partially explanatory — by **analogy** for men.
- Same genes appear in male early-onset OP literature (*LRP5*, *COL1*)—sex-specific trigger, shared monogenic architecture candidates.
- Design badge is **case-series**, not a large cohort.

# Caveats

- **n=7, women only**; absolute genetic frequencies and yield may differ in men.
- Finding a monogenic variant does not negate treatable secondary factors (hypercalciuria, hypogonadism, vitamin D).
- Abstract-only access for some readers; 3/7 yield is series-specific.
