# Summary

- Quantitative meta-analysis of published studies linking residential dampness or mold with respiratory infections and bronchitis.
- Random-effects summary ORs ~1.38–1.50 across outcomes, with CIs excluding the null; results robust after restricting to studies controlling age, sex, smoking, and SES.
- Estimated attributable risk proportions ~8–20% for studied outcomes.
- Supports dampness as a preventable public-health exposure for **respiratory** disease—not a validation of systemic mycotoxin/CIRS constructs.

# Key data points

- Bronchitis OR 1.45 (95% CI 1.32–1.59) in confounder-adjusted models.
- Respiratory infections OR 1.44 (1.31–1.59); excluding nonspecific URI OR 1.50 (1.32–1.70).
- Pediatric/infant respiratory infections OR 1.48 (1.33–1.65).
- Little evidence of strong publication bias on funnel plots.

# Caveats

- Observational epidemiology—dampness is a proxy for mixed exposures; does not prove mycotoxin causation.
- Does not address musculoskeletal, bone, or neurocognitive endpoints central to CIRS claims.
- Research catalog only.
