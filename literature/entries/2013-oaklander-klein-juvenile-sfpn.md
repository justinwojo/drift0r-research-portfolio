# Summary

- Tests whether acquired small-fiber polyneuropathy (SFPN) contributes to unexplained pediatric/juvenile widespread pain syndromes.
- Retrospective analysis of 41 consecutive patients with unexplained widespread pain beginning before age 21, using objective SFPN tests (skin biopsy, nerve biopsy, AFT) plus clinical data.
- Objective testing: definite SFPN 59%, probable 17%, possible 22%; only 1/41 entirely normal on SFPN testing.
- Many had multi-system dysautonomia symptoms; among treated subset, corticosteroids and/or IVIG subjectively/objectively helped 80% (12/15).

# Key data points

- Mean onset age 12.3 ± 5.7 years; 73% female; 68% chronically disabled; 68% hospitalized.
- Neurologic exam: reduced sensation 68%; vasomotor abnormalities 55% (erythromelalgia 23%).
- Other somatic complaints consistent with SFPN dysautonomia: cardiovascular 90%, GI 82%, urologic 34%; chronic fatigue 83%; chronic headache 63%.
- Exhaustive causality workup: autoimmune history 33%; serologic markers of disordered immunity 89%.
- Extends recognized age range of acquired SFPN into early childhood/adolescence.

**Why:** Reinforces that widespread noninflammatory pain can be organic small-fiber disease and should not default to primary psychiatric/centralized labels—aligned with case history (SSD reversed; objective beriberi neuropathy). # Caveats

- Pediatric/young-adult onset cohort; adult applicability is by mechanism, not identical epidemiology.
- Patient’s dominant current disability is joint mechanical failure + spine disease, not only neuropathic pain quality.
- Immunotherapy response data are uncontrolled and from a treated subset.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
