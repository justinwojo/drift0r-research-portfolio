# Summary

- Retrospective case series of men presenting to rheumatology with persistent nonspecific diffuse MSK pain who were found to have low testosterone (BMJ Case Reports).
- Notes scarcity of prior published correlations between diffuse MSK pain and male hypogonadism.
- In n=45 hypogonadal men with diffuse pain, longer pain duration correlated with lower baseline total testosterone (Spearman −0.32, p=0.05); free T correlation not significant.
- Authors conclude generalized screening is not yet indicated and call for prospective study of T replacement effects on pain.

# Key data points

- Source: UIHC rheumatology clinic, March 2000–September 2004; 45 men with baseline total T included (mean total T ~191 ng/dL; mean age ~48).
- Mean pain duration ~4.7 years (range 1–20).
- Negative correlation: pain years vs baseline total T; age not independently associated with T in this series.
- Limited post-TRT pain-score data (n=8 completers) showed no significant pain change—underpowered follow-up.
- Clinical implication: consider androgen status in men with unexplained diffuse MSK pain plus hypogonadism clues, without over-claiming causality.

Not complete: does not cover migratory 5-year joint fragility, osteoporosis severity, beriberi, or Ig patterns.

# Caveats

- Case series / observational; cannot prove T deficiency *causes* the pain syndrome.
- Underpowered for TRT pain outcomes; many lost to follow-up.
- Patient’s paradoxical low-T behavioral phenotype and multi-disease stack exceed this series’ scope.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
