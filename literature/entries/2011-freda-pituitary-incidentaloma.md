# Summary

- Practice guideline for endocrine evaluation and management of pituitary incidentalomas (lesions found on imaging done for other reasons).
- Recommend history/exam, labs for hormone **hypersecretion** and **hypopituitarism**, and visual fields if lesion abuts optic apparatus.
- Microincidentalomas not meeting surgical criteria: MRI follow-up at ~1 year, then less often if stable; macroincidentalomas earlier (e.g., 6 months).
- Most microadenomas are nonfunctioning and indolent; surgery reserved for mass effect, visual compromise, or selected secreting tumors.

# Key data points

- Distinguishes micro- vs macro-incidentaloma pathways.
- Screen for hyperprolactinemia and other hypersecretory states; assess corticotroph/thyrotroph/gonadotroph axes as indicated.
- Many small foci may be artifacts, Rathke remnants, or true adenomas below resolution on later scans.
- No automatic need for surgery for asymptomatic ~3 mm lesions without hormone excess or mass effect.

# Caveats

- 2011 document; imaging tech and consensus have evolved (see newer Pituitary Society statements).
- Incidentaloma guidelines do not establish that a 3 mm focus caused profound hypogonadism.
- Disappearance on follow-up MRI reduces but does not zero the probability of subtle central pathology.
