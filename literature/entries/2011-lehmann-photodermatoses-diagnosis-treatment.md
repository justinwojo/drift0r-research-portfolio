# Summary

- Clinical review of sunlight-induced dermatoses with modern nosology, diagnosis, and treatment.
- Divides photodermatoses into: (1) phototoxic and photoallergic reactions to known photosensitizers; (2) idiopathic photodermatoses (photosensitizer unknown).
- Spectrum from very rare (hydroa vacciniforme, prevalence ~0.34/100,000) to very common (polymorphic light eruption, prevalence ~10–20%).
- Emphasizes that photodermatoses are usually not life-threatening but can cause major morbidity; prevention equals treatment importance.
- Directly supports a structured **non-lupus** differential when photosensitivity appears with negative ANA/ENA.

# Key data points

- Workup hinges on history (latency, drug/exogenous photosensitizers), morphology, phototesting, and selective photopatch testing.
- Idiopathic group includes polymorphic light eruption (PMLE), actinic prurigo, chronic actinic dermatitis, solar urticaria, hydroa vacciniforme.
- Connective-tissue disease (cutaneous lupus) is a major differential but not the default when serologies are repeatedly negative.
- Phototoxic drug reactions are common and often overlooked (antibiotics, diuretics, etc.).

# Caveats

- 2011 general review—does not replace dermatology phototesting when symptoms are significant.
- “Photosensitivity” in patient narrative may be subjective sun intolerance without a primary photodermatosis.
- Some porphyrias and metabolic photodermatoses need separate labs if clinically suspected.
- Research catalog only.
