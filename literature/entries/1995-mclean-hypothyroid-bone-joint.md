# Summary

- Reviews musculoskeletal manifestations of hypothyroidism ranging from myalgias and arthralgias to true myopathy and arthritis.
- Includes a case vignette of isolated hip arthropathy as the presenting feature of severe hypothyroidism in a young man.
- Adult hypothyroid arthropathy most often involves knees and hands; pediatric disease more often involves hip/epiphysis of femoral head.
- Describes highly viscous, noninflammatory joint effusions (knees, wrists, hands) and associations with epiphyseal dysgenesis, aseptic necrosis, and possible crystal disease.

# Key data points

- Hypothyroid state can alter connective tissue/cartilage/bone biology enough to produce clinical arthropathy.
- Effusions may be noninflammatory (low WBC) despite joint swelling—important for “inflammatory vs noninflammatory” exams.
- Spectrum includes arthralgia without frank arthritis as well as more destructive processes.
- Correction of thyroid hormone deficiency is the disease-specific intervention when hypothyroidism is causal.

# Caveats

- Older review (1995); imaging and endocrine diagnostics have evolved.
- Case vignette is hip-focused monarthropathy—not multi-year migratory polyarthralgia.
- Do not equate “endocrine possible” with “thyroid proven” without labs.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
