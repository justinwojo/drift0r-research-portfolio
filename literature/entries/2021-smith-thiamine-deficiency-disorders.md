# Summary

- Broad clinical review of thiamine deficiency disorders (TDDs) spanning metabolic, neurologic, cardiovascular, respiratory, GI, and musculoskeletal systems.
- Frames dry beriberi (peripheral neuropathy), wet beriberi, Wernicke encephalopathy, and infantile forms as a continuous spectrum rather than siloed diagnoses.
- Stresses under-recognition outside classic alcoholism, variable presentations, and the need for empiric treatment when clinical suspicion is high.
- Discusses risk from low intake, increased metabolic demand, losses (e.g., diuretics, vomiting), and malabsorption.

# Key data points

- Body stores of thiamine are limited (~2–3 weeks of reserve with zero intake in many contexts).
- Dry beriberi: symmetrical sensory-motor polyneuropathy, distal predominance, reflex loss, pain/paresthesia.
- Wernicke: classic triad incomplete in many patients; do not wait for full triad.
- Biomarkers imperfect; treat on clinical grounds while labs are pending.
- Increased carbohydrate/energy flux raises thiamine cofactor demand (illness, refeeding, surgery).

# Caveats

- Technical/clinical review, not a primary interventional study.
- Global and pediatric content dilutes focus on adult non-alcoholic neuropathy cases.
- Laboratory cutoffs and assay methods vary by section and source literature.
