# Summary

- Systematic review of recreational cannabis exposure and adult **BMD** outcomes (MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane, Web of Science through March 2023).
- From 2620 records, only **four** studies (n≈4032; ages ~18–60) met inclusion: three cross-sectional and one RCT—evidence base still thin relative to preclinical CB1/CB2 bone biology.
- Split results: **two studies** associated cannabis with decreased BMD; **two** found no BMD alteration. Risk of bias rated low by JBI tools despite heterogeneous exposure definitions.
- Authors call for longitudinal work with better product/route/dose capture, especially in vulnerable groups (elderly, low BMI, multi-hit secondary osteoporosis).

# Key data points

- Eligible human studies: 4 (after screening 2620).
- Heterogeneity: cannabis products, routes, and exposure ascertainment differ widely.
- Direction of effect inconsistent across included studies (↓BMD vs null).
- Preclinical endocannabinoid–osteoblast/osteoclast literature stronger/more mechanistic than clinical BMD consensus.
- Complements Sophocleous 2017 (heavy-use signal) without elevating it to settled causality.

# Caveats

- Few studies; no robust dose–response meta-analysis of BMD.
- Recreational vs medical cannabinoid products not interchangeable.
- Does not address testosterone axis outcomes (see Belladelli 2021).
- Not medical advice.
