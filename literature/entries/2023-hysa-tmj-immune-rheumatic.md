# Summary

- Systematic review of temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) in adult immune-mediated rheumatic diseases (IMRDs).
- Of 3259 screened abstracts, 56 papers included; quality scored with Newcastle-Ottawa scale.
- TMJ/TMD signs and symptoms are common but variable: RA 8–70%, SpA 12–80%, SSc 20–93%, SLE 18–85%, primary Sjögren 24–54%, myopathies 4–26%.
- Risk factors often track higher disease activity/duration; early multidisciplinary recognition is urged because TMJ is frequently underdiagnosed in rheumatology practice.

# Key data points

- RA papers dominate the literature (~77% of included studies).
- RA TMD risk associations: female sex, younger age, ACPA positivity, higher disease activity, cervical spine involvement, CV/neuropsychiatric comorbidities.
- SpA TMD more common with radiographic spine involvement, psoriasis, HLA-DRB1*01.
- In SSc/SLE, TMD correlates with activity/duration; skin fibrosis extent (SSc) and renal involvement (SLE) noted as correlates in synthesis.
- Clinical implication: jaw pain in multi-joint disease may be systemic joint involvement, not isolated dental TMD.

# Caveats

- Prevalence ranges are wide; heterogeneity and diagnostic definitions vary across studies.
- Review concerns immune-mediated rheumatic diseases with inflammatory features often present—case lacks clinical synovitis and acute-phase elevation.
- Does not establish mechanism of noninflammatory TMJ arthralgia.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
