# Summary

- Multidisciplinary AASLD practice guidance for diagnosis and management of **Wilson disease (copper overload)** due to *ATP7B* dysfunction.
- Diagnostic approach integrates low ceruloplasmin, serum/urine copper interpretation, hepatic copper quantification, slit-lamp exam for Kayser–Fleischer rings, neurology assessment, and molecular testing.
- Treatment pillars: chelators and/or zinc under specialist care; transplant for selected hepatic failure; family screening mandatory.
- Catalogued here primarily for **differential contrast**: Wilson is copper overload, not the acquired copper deficiency myeloneuropathy hypothesized in community discussion.

# Key data points

- Ceruloplasmin is usually low in neurologic Wilson but is **not specific**—also falls in copper deficiency, aceruloplasminemia, severe liver failure, and some heterozygotes.
- Non-ceruloplasmin (free) copper concepts and 24-hour urinary copper help, but require careful assay interpretation; genetics increasingly front-line.
- Phenotype: hepatic disease ± movement disorders, dysarthria, psychiatric presentations—not isolated small-fiber burning pain or thiamine-responsive beriberi.
- Zinc used therapeutically in Wilson (blocks copper absorption) is the **same mechanistic pathway** that, in excess without WD, can *cause* copper deficiency—context is everything.

# Caveats

- Full guidance is long; executive summary also published (PMID 36152019).
- Local labs differ in free-copper methodology—follow specialist protocols.
- Existing EASL 2012 card (lit-0198) remains useful historical EU counterpart.
