# Summary

- Comprehensive review of **drug-induced photosensitivity (DIP)**—phototoxic and photoallergic reactions from systemic/topical drugs plus UV/visible light.
- Catalogs a very large set of implicated agents (hundreds of drugs/compounds with varying evidence strength) across antibiotic, cardiovascular, psychotropic, NSAID, and other classes.
- Distinguishes **phototoxicity** (direct cellular injury; often UVA; sunburn-like; minutes–hours) from **photoallergy** (T-cell–mediated; delayed eczematous; 24–72 h), and discusses chronic photoaging/photocarcinogenic concerns for some culprits.
- Clinical consequence: photosensitivity is often **exogenous/drug-related** and reversible with drug cessation + photoprotection—must be separated from primary autoimmune photodermatoses (e.g., cutaneous lupus, dermatomyositis) and metabolic photodermatoses (porphyria).

# Key data points

- Authors reference on the order of **~393** drugs/compounds with reported photosensitizing potential (evidence quality highly variable).
- Mechanisms: drug chromophore absorbs light → ROS/direct damage (phototoxic) vs photoantigen formation and immune response (photoallergic).
- Epidemiology and public-health angle: common, under-recognized adverse drug reaction.
- Long-term issues: photoaging; possible photocarcinogenesis signals for selected agents.
- Management pillars: identify/stop culprit when feasible, strict photoprotection, symptomatic care; photopatch testing for photoallergy workups.

# Caveats

- Many culprit associations are case-level; challenge-rechallenge data uneven.
- Overlap phenotypes can still represent autoimmune disease unmasked by sun or drugs.
- Does not replace rheumatology/dermatology evaluation when systemic autoimmune features dominate.
- Not medical advice.
