# Summary

- Multidisciplinary AUA evidence-based guideline on diagnosing and managing adult male **testosterone deficiency**.
- Diagnosis requires compatible signs/symptoms **plus** low morning total testosterone, confirmed on **repeat** testing (AUA operational cutoff commonly cited as total T <300 ng/dL supporting deficiency—interpret with free T/SHBG when indicated).
- Emphasizes on-treatment monitoring for therapeutic levels and symptom response; addresses CV disease, prostate, and fertility-preserving pathways.
- **Exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis**—men desiring fertility should not receive TRT as first-line; consider alternative strategies (e.g., SERMs/hCG in appropriate candidates).

# Key data points

- Measure total T in the morning; repeat to confirm; investigate secondary causes and comorbidities rather than treating a single lab value.
- Goal of therapy: ameliorate symptoms and achieve mid-normal therapeutic testosterone range with safety monitoring (Hct, PSA as age-appropriate, symptom review).
- Bone health: hypogonadism is a recognized secondary osteoporosis risk—DXA considerations overlap Endocrine Society male osteoporosis guidance.
- 2024 AUA review confirmed ongoing validity of the 2018 statements pending major new evidence.

# Caveats

- AUA target audience is largely urology/men’s health practice—endocrine nuance (pituitary MRI thresholds, etc.) may need Endocrine Society co-reading.
- Cutoffs and assays vary; free T by equilibrium dialysis preferred when SHBG abnormal.
- Not medical advice.
