# Summary

- Joint IDSA / AAN / ACR evidence-based guideline on prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of Lyme disease (*Borrelia burgdorferi* s.l. in North America/Europe context).
- Endorses **two-tier serology** (or modified two-tier with EIA/EIA) as standard for extracutaneous disease; clinical diagnosis for classic erythema migrans.
- Does **not** support unvalidated “specialty” multi-pathogen panels as substitutes for CDC/IDSA-aligned testing; recommends against long-term antibiotic therapy for persistent symptoms after standard treatment (PTLDS).
- Essential contrast card when commercial labs are negative but specialty labs report positive tick-borne signals.

# Key data points

- Early localized EM: clinical diagnosis; serology often negative in first 2 weeks—do not delay treatment for EM.
- Neurologic, cardiac, and arthritis Lyme: two-tier serology is cornerstone; CSF antibody index in selected neuroborreliosis cases.
- PCR on blood has poor sensitivity for Lyme (unlike Babesia); synovial fluid PCR may help in Lyme arthritis.
- Recommended antibiotic durations are finite (typically 10–28 days by syndrome); extended courses not recommended for subjective persistent symptoms alone.
- Coinfection testing (Anaplasma, Babesia) when epidemiology and cytopenias/hemolysis warrant—not shotgun specialty panels without pretest probability.

# Caveats

- Does not cover *Bartonella* chronic MSK syndromes (different organism, weaker evidence base).
- Highly contested by some patient advocacy groups; analysts should state IDSA/AAN/ACR vs ILADS frameworks explicitly.
- Epidemiology matters: Texas exposure patterns differ from Northeast endemic areas.
- PubMed lists dual DOIs (CID and ACR journals); use 10.1093/cid/ciaa1215 for free full text.
