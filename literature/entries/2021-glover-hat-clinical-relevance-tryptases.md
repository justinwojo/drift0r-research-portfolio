# Summary

- Positions HαT as the **most common genetic cause of elevated basal serum tryptase** (~1 in 20 White individuals).
- Reviews evolutionary genetics of human tryptases, how extra α copies change tryptase composition (including α/β heterotetramers), and clinical impact across clonal and nonclonal mast-cell disorders.
- Many HαT carriers lack major symptoms; enrichment and severity modification appear in anaphylaxis and mastocytosis populations.

# Key data points

- HαT: autosomal dominant *TPSAB1* α copy-number gain.
- Increased mature α/β-tryptase heterotetramers proposed as a mechanistic bridge to anaphylaxis severity.
- Clinical landscape is moving toward genotype-informed counseling rather than treating every elevated tryptase as SM/MCAS.
