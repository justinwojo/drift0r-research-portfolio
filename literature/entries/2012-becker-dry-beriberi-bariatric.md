# Summary

- Case of a 25-year-old woman who developed subacute progressive weakness and areflexia followed by confusion, ophthalmoplegia, and nystagmus after gastric lap-band (bariatric) surgery.
- Multifocal neurologic dysfunction attributed to dry beriberi (neuropathy/myopathy features) plus Wernicke encephalopathy from vitamin B1 deficiency.
- Emphasizes rising neurologic complications of bariatric surgery as obesity surgery volume grows.
- Argues vitamin deficiency should be routinely suspected after bariatric procedures to prevent permanent neurologic injury.

# Key data points

- Sequence: neuropathy signs first, then classic WE triad.
- Post-bariatric thiamine deficiency can appear with vomiting, reduced intake, and/or malabsorption.
- Differential of generalized weakness + altered mental status is broad; B1 deficiency is treatable and time-sensitive.
- Prompt recognition can prevent irreversible injury.
