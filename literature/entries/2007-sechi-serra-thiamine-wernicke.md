# Summary

- Authoritative clinical review of **Wernicke’s encephalopathy (WE)** as the acute neuropsychiatric face of thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency, emphasizing underdiagnosis on autopsy series.
- Expands classic “alcoholic” framing to bariatric surgery, hyperemesis, malignancy, prolonged vomiting/diarrhea, TPN without vitamins, starvation, and other non-alcoholic settings.
- Classifies common, rare, and late symptoms; stresses that the classic triad (ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion) is often incomplete.
- Details parenteral thiamine dosing for treatment and prophylaxis and prevention of Korsakoff psychosis—framework transferable to dry beriberi neuropathy management principles (urgent repletion, do not wait for assays if suspicion high).

# Key data points

- WE remains under-recognized; many cases lack the full triad at presentation.
- MRI (mamillary body, periaqueductal, medial thalami FLAIR/DWI changes) supports diagnosis but normal MRI does not exclude WE.
- Empiric high-dose **parenteral** thiamine should not be delayed for laboratory confirmation when WE is suspected.
- Glucose administration without thiamine can precipitate or worsen WE in deficient patients.
- Dry beriberi (peripheral neuropathy) and wet beriberi (high-output cardiac failure) are related thiamine-deficiency phenotypes on the same spectrum.

# Caveats

- Focus is WE/Korsakoff more than chronic dry beriberi or outpatient benfotiamine regimens.
- 2007—dosing protocols and bariatric pathways have been refined since; core principles hold.
- Paywalled full text common; abstract free on PubMed.
- Does not explain multi-year non-thiamine features after repletion.
