# Summary

- Analytical/clinical laboratory evaluation of a commercial **Babesia genus-specific FISH** assay on blood smears.
- FISH detected *B. microti*, *B. duncani*, *B. divergens*, and veterinary *Babesia* spp. in <2 hours via fluorescent probes to parasite rRNA.
- Positioned as a **supplement** to smear, serology, and PCR—especially claimed useful where PCR inhibitors or low resource settings matter.
- Directly relevant to Drift0r’s **IGeneX-type FISH+ ×2** results.

# Key data points

- Method: fluorescent DNA probes hybridize to *Babesia* 18S rRNA in fixed blood smears; visualized under fluorescence-capable microscopy.
- Detects multiple human-relevant species (*microti*, *duncani*, *divergens*)—genus-level, not automatically species-resolved by FISH alone.
- Turnaround claimed <2 hours after smear preparation.
- Authors report utility alongside existing methods rather than as sole gold standard.
- **Conflicts of interest stated:** lead author owns stock / CEO of IGeneX and scientific director of ID-FISH; coauthors employees/affiliates.

# Caveats

- Heavy industry conflict of interest; independent multi-center validation limited.
- Paper does not fully resolve **FISH+/PCR− discordance** clinical meaning (false positive FISH vs low-level viable parasites vs other Babesia/piroplasms vs artifact).
- Not FDA-cleared diagnostic standard equivalent to clinical PCR used by Quest/LabCorp/hospital labs.
- Genus-level positivity does not identify species for treatment epidemiology.
