# Summary

- Randomized controlled trial comparing **spinal cord stimulation** vs **repeat lumbosacral spine surgery** for chronic radicular pain after prior surgery.
- Key result: SCS was **more effective** than reoperation for persistent radicular pain; a large majority of successful SCS patients avoided further surgery.
- Frames neuromodulation as a legitimate alternative pathway when the clinical question is “more surgery vs stimulate.”
- Relevant whenever fusion/reoperation is contemplated for refractory lumbar radicular pain—including when structural surgery is **deferred** for medical reasons (e.g., bone density).

# Key data points

- Population: patients with persistent radicular pain and an indication for reoperation.
- Primary outcome construct: ≥50% pain relief and patient satisfaction/crossover patterns.
- Reported significant pain relief more often with SCS than reoperation (classic cited figure: ~39% SCS vs lower reoperation success in the original analysis).
- Crossover design elements: failed SCS patients could proceed to surgery; many surgical failures later sought SCS.
- Supports staged decision-making: trial SCS before committing to higher-morbidity reoperation when appropriate.

# Caveats

- All patients had prior spine surgery (FBSS context).
- Older SCS hardware and selection criteria.
- Does not evaluate osteoporosis-related fusion risk or multi-system post-implant symptom flares.
- Single-center era RCT; external validity limited.
- Not medical advice.
