# Summary

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of incidence of **antidepressant discontinuation (withdrawal) symptoms** after stopping antidepressants vs placebo across RCTs and observational studies.
- Incidence of ≥1 discontinuation symptom ≈ **0.31** after antidepressant stop vs **0.17** after placebo; placebo-controlled difference ≈ **8 percentage points** (~15% incidence after accounting for nonspecific effects; roughly 1 in 6–7 patients).
- Severe discontinuation symptoms uncommon (~2.8% antidepressant vs 0.6% placebo). Higher rates/severity signals for venlafaxine/desvenlafaxine, imipramine, paroxetine, escitalopram in subgroup analyses.
- **Critical interpretation for musculoskeletal claims:** established withdrawal includes flu-like symptoms, dizziness, sensory disturbances, insomnia, irritability—typically days to weeks. This evidence base does **not** establish a chronic inflammatory polyarthritis or progressive multi-system MSK disease entity as “post-SSRI syndrome.” Distinct from **PSSD** (enduring sexual dysfunction literature), which is a different construct with its own sparse evidence.

# Key data points

- 79 studies (44 RCTs + 35 observational); 21,002 patients (72% female; mean age ~45).
- Antidepressant discontinuers n=16,532; placebo discontinuers n=4,470.
- Any discontinuation symptom incidence 0.31 (95% CI 0.27–0.35) vs placebo 0.17 (0.14–0.21); RCT difference 0.08 (0.04–0.12).
- Severe symptoms incidence 0.028 vs 0.006.
- Substantial heterogeneity; residual/re-emerging psychopathology must be considered.
- Does not meta-analyze long-term MSK disease incidence after remote SSRI exposure.

# Caveats

- Withdrawal ≠ disease relapse ≠ PSSD ≠ chronic autoimmune arthritis.
- Heterogeneity and placebo rates show large nonspecific symptom reporting.
- Observational studies inflate incidence relative to RCTs.
- Supportive PSSD literature (Healy and colleagues) concerns sexual dysfunction, not validated chronic polyarthritis.
- Not medical advice.
