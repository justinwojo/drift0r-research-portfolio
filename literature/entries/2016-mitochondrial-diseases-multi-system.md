# Summary

- Mitochondrial diseases are genetic OXPHOS disorders (mtDNA or nuclear DNA) with extreme phenotypic heterogeneity.
- Adult disease is often progressive and multi-system: myopathy, neuropathy, migraine/stroke-like episodes, hearing loss, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, GI dysmotility, and endocrine failure.
- “One lesion, many systems” over time is a clinical clue; maternal inheritance patterns help only for mtDNA disease.
- Diagnosis uses phenotype-guided genetics, muscle biopsy (ragged-red fibers, COX-neg fibers) in selected cases, and metabolic labs (lactate, etc.—insensitive).

# Key data points

- Prevalence of adult mitochondrial disease is non-trivial in population studies (nuclear + mtDNA combined).
- Exercise intolerance, ptosis/ophthalmoplegia, and sensorineural hearing loss are classic red flags—not always present.
- Secondary mitochondrial dysfunction can mimic primary disease (drugs, aging)—interpret genetics carefully.
- No single disease-modifying cure for most genotypes; management is supportive/complication-focused (specialist centers).

- Secondary causes of low energy (sleep, depression, deconditioning) can mimic.
