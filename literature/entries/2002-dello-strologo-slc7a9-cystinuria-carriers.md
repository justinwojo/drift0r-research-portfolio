# Summary

- Large ICC clinical–genetic comparison of *SLC3A1* vs *SLC7A9* cystinuria patients and obligate carriers, arguing for a genetics-based classification (type A/B) over classic urinary phenotypes I/II/III alone.
- Shows that **heterozygous SLC7A9 carriers** frequently have abnormal urinary cystine/dibasic amino acids yet much lower lifetime stone risk than biallelic patients.
- Documents incomplete dominance of many *SLC7A9* alleles—carrier biochemistry ≠ classic recurrent cystine stone disease.
- Foundational for counseling patients with incidental or modest *SLC7A9* heterozygous findings.

# Key data points

- 224 patients; full genotype in 125; amino acid excretion patterns in 189 heterozygotes.
- Only two genes (*SLC3A1*, *SLC7A9*) account for classic cystinuria types in this framework.
- *SLC7A9* heterozygotes: often elevated urinary cystine; stone formation in a minority (literature range roughly low-double-digit % over lifetime, far below biallelic disease).
- Clinical severity and age at first stone differ substantially between patients and carriers.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4–5

**Why:** Heterozygous *SLC7A9* with modest carrier-pattern biochemistry and limited stone history matches the ICC carrier phenotype—not classic severe cystinuria. Supports treating *SLC7A9* het as a **mild risk modifier**, while idiopathic calcium hypercalciuria and other OP drivers remain the main bone story.

# Caveats

- Does not cause or explain T-score −4.3 osteoporosis.
- Variant-specific effects differ; not all *SLC7A9* hets have identical urine phenotypes.
- Qualitative cyanide-nitroprusside tests can be negative in mild carriers—quantitative amino acids preferred.
- Stone composition matters; a single non-cystine stone should not be attributed to *SLC7A9* alone.
