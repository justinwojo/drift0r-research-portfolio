# Summary

- Uncontrolled case series of hydrochlorothiazide in hypercalciuric men with low BMD.
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria long associated with low BMD in men.
- Source conclusion: hydrochlorothiazide treatment **was associated with** a rapid rebound increase in bone mineral density (associational, not causal proof).
- Often cited in secondary-osteoporosis and stone-bone discussions.
- Complements modern hypercalciuria reviews (lit-0013).

# Key data points

- Design: case series (not RCT).
- Population: five male patients, ages 42–66 years.
- Intervention: hydrochlorothiazide.
- Outcome: reduced calciuria and rapid BMD improvement reported; no fracture outcomes.
- The present research subject (~age 38) is younger than every enrolled subject.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Male elevated urine calcium + markedly low BMD for age is the research phenotype of interest.
- Associational evidence only that addressing calciuria can coincide with densitometric recovery.
- Does not establish that ongoing calciuria explains spine T −4.3 magnitude, and is not a treatment recommendation.

# Caveats

- Uncontrolled case series, n=5, ages 42–66; no control group; no fracture outcomes.
- Patient younger than every enrolled subject.
- 1999 densitometry and evaluation standards differ from current practice.
- Thiazide literature context only — not a prescription or multi-modal treatment plan.
