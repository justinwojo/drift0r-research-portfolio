# Summary

- Identifies *WNT1* as a human skeletal disease gene linking the Wnt pathway to early-onset osteoporosis and osteogenesis imperfecta (OI).
- Heterozygous *WNT1* missense (p.Cys218Gly) segregated with **dominantly inherited early-onset osteoporosis** and vertebral compression fractures across a large pedigree.
- Separate family: homozygous *WNT1* nonsense → recessive severe OI phenotype with osteopenia and long-bone deformity.
- Mutant WNT1 proteins showed impaired induction of canonical Wnt signaling, target genes, and mineralization in vitro.

# Key data points

- Dominant early-onset OP kindred: multiple adults with severe vertebral fractures and low BMD without classic childhood deforming OI.
- Functional assays: WNT1C218G and WNT1S295* failed to accumulate active β-catenin comparably to wild-type WNT1.
- Mouse data: *Wnt1* expressed in bone marrow (notably B-lineage) and a subset of osteocytes—suggests hematopoietic–osteoblast Wnt crosstalk.
- Frames the clinical concept that **non-COL1 monogenic OP** can present as “idiopathic” adult osteoporosis.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 3–4

**Why:**
- **Matches:** Markedly low BMD for age (spine T to −4.3), male sex compatible with dominant WNT1 OP, multi-year diagnostic odyssey where historical low testosterone is judged **insufficient** as sole cause, fusion deferred until bone is rebuilt.
- **Does not fully match as sole disease:** Documented hypercalciuria, heterozygous *SLC7A9*, HαT, thiamine-responsive neuropathy, IgG4 pattern, and migratory noninflammatory polyarthralgia sit outside pure WNT1 skeletal disease.
- **Research value:** *WNT1* belongs on any early-onset OP / bone-fragility gene panel even when secondary factors (hypogonadism, hypercalciuria) are present—monogenic and secondary causes can coexist.

# Caveats

- Not every early OP is monogenic; secondary workup remains mandatory first-line.
- Heterozygous WNT1 OP phenotype is variable; family history may be subtle or de novo-looking.
- Does not dictate drug choice alone (anabolic vs antiresorptive) without specialist input.
