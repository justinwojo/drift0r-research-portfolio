# Summary

- Cochrane systematic review of interventions that remediate damp/mould-damaged buildings to reduce respiratory symptoms, infections, and asthma.
- Included 12 studies (8028 participants): 2 RCTs, 1 cluster-RCT, 9 controlled before-after studies; interventions ranged from thorough renovation to cleaning only.
- Moderate-quality evidence that repairing houses decreased adult asthma-related symptoms (e.g., wheezing OR 0.64) and respiratory infections (e.g., rhinitis OR 0.57).
- Children’s results less consistent; evidence quality limited by study designs.

# Key data points

- N = 8028 across 12 studies.
- Adult wheezing OR 0.64 (95% CI 0.55–0.75); rhinitis OR 0.57 (0.49–0.66) after house repair (moderate-quality evidence, two studies cited in abstract).
- Interventions heterogeneous: full remediation vs cleaning-only.
- Primary outcomes are respiratory—not multi-system CIRS symptom batteries.

# Caveats

- Many included studies are controlled before-after (higher bias risk than RCTs).
- Does not validate mycotoxin blood tests, binder protocols, or non-respiratory CIRS claims.
- Improvement after remediation ≠ proof of systemic mycotoxicosis.
- Research catalog only.
