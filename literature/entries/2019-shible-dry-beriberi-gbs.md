# Summary

- Case of a 56-year-old woman with prior gallstone pancreatitis and protein-calorie malnutrition (recent TPN) who presented with ascending paresthesias of limbs and neck plus encephalopathy.
- Initially treated as Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) with IVIG based on MRI/CSF findings; encephalopathy worsened and she required intubation/vasopressors.
- Repeat brain MRI showed Wernicke encephalopathy changes; high-dose IV thiamine produced mental-status improvement within 48 hours and extubation by day 3.
- Authors emphasize dry beriberi neuropathy can mimic AIDP/GBS, including albuminocytologic dissociation, and that empiric thiamine can prevent irreversible injury (e.g., Korsakoff).

# Key data points

- Dry beriberi = neuropathy from thiamine (B1) deficiency; wet beriberi = high-output heart failure ± neuropathy.
- Severe deficiency may add Wernicke encephalopathy (ocular signs, ataxia, confusion).
- Thiamine repletion led to rapid clinical improvement after failed IVIG.
- Risk contexts listed: alcohol, malnutrition, bariatric surgery, TPN, malabsorption, increased caloric requirements.

# Caveats

- Single case; coexisting Wernicke makes pure “dry beriberi only” comparison imperfect.
- Cause was overt malnutrition/TPN—not idiopathic low serum B1 on an apparently adequate diet.
- Recovery of mental status was rapid; chronic axonal neuropathy recovery timelines not fully detailed.
