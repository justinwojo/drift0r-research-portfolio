# Summary

- International Study Group (1990) published widely used clinical diagnostic criteria for Behçet’s disease to standardize case definition.
- **Required:** recurrent oral ulceration (minor aphthous, major aphthous, or herpetiform ulcers recurring at least 3 times in one 12-month period).
- **Plus any two of:** recurrent genital ulceration; eye lesions (anterior/posterior uveitis or cells in vitreous, or retinal vasculitis); skin lesions (erythema nodosum, pseudofolliculitis, papulopustular lesions, acneiform nodules in post-adolescent patients not on corticosteroids); positive pathergy test read by a physician at 24–48 h.
- High specificity in classic series; later ICBD criteria (lit-0328) improved sensitivity for some regional phenotypes.

# Key data points

- Oral ulcers are mandatory in ISG—absence essentially excludes ISG-defined Behçet.
- Pathergy is supportive, not standalone.
- Behçet is inflammatory vasculitis with mucocutaneous-ocular core—not a primary metabolic bone disease.
- Classification criteria aid research/clinical consensus but cannot replace exclusion of mimics (HSV, IBD, SLE, infection).

# Caveats

- 1990 criteria predate ICBD and modern imaging/vascular definitions.
- Some true Behçet phenotypes (especially vascular-predominant) were less well captured by ISG—hence ICBD.
