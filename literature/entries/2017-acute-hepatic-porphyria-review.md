# Summary

- Acute hepatic porphyrias (esp. acute intermittent porphyria, AIP) cause life-threatening neurovisceral attacks: severe abdominal pain, autonomic instability, dark urine, hyponatremia, and motor neuropathy that can mimic Guillain–Barré.
- Attacks are precipitated by drugs, fasting, hormones, infection, and stress; urine porphobilinogen (PBG) is markedly elevated during attacks.
- Between attacks, patients may be well or have chronic pain/fatigue; skin disease is absent in pure AIP (present in variegate/hereditary coproporphyria).
- Prevention and attack treatment (heme arginate/hemin, givosiran in selected) are specialty-managed.

# Key data points

- Screening during symptoms: random urine PBG/porphyrins (protect from light); genetics for *HMBS* etc. after biochemical confirmation.
- Chronic symptoms without ever-elevated PBG make AIP unlikely.
- Motor weakness and bulbar involvement define severe attacks—not isolated joint pain.
- Drug safety lists matter for any suspected case.
