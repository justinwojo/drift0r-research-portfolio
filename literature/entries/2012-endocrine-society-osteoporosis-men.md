# Summary

- Endocrine Society clinical practice guideline formulating recommendations for diagnosis and management of osteoporosis in men.
- Recommends DXA testing for men ≥70 and men 50–69 with risk factors (low body weight, prior adult fracture, smoking, etc.).
- Laboratory evaluation is mandatory to detect secondary/contributing causes before attributing disease to primary osteoporosis.
- Pharmacologic treatment recommended for men ≥50 with spine/hip fractures, T-score ≤ −2.5, or high fracture risk by BMD + clinical factors.
- Emphasizes adequate calcium/vitamin D, weight-bearing exercise; avoid smoking and excess alcohol.
- Serial DXA used to monitor treatment response.
- Notes significant morbidity and mortality from male osteoporotic fracture (higher post-fracture mortality than women).
- Hypogonadism and other secondary causes (including hypercalciuria in related workups) require detection and management.

# Key data points

- Testing threshold: age ≥70, or 50–69 with risk factors.
- Treatment T-score threshold: ≤ −2.5 at spine or hip (in appropriate age groups).
- Secondary workup expected in all men with osteoporosis, especially younger men.
- Pharmacologic options discussed: bisphosphonates, denosumab (context), teriparatide for high risk.
- Central DXA (spine, hip); 33% radius when spine/hip unusable.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Historically framed secondary-cause evaluation for young men with extreme T-scores; guideline is retired and not current society guidance.
- Hypogonadism flagged as a major secondary contributor (historical T 34 ng/dL).
- Fracture risk + low BMD pathway supports aggressive evaluation even when age is below typical screening cutoffs.
- Guideline is less explicit on men <50; ISCD Z-score rules and clinical judgment still needed (see lit-0015).

# Caveats

- **FORMALLY RETIRED** by the Endocrine Society on **2025-10-16** (PMID 41313253; DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgaf645). The issuing society no longer endorses this guideline; historical context only.
- Primary focus is men ≥50; recommendations for men under 50 are less detailed.
- 2012 document; drug landscape and densitometry database debates have evolved further since retirement.
- Does not replace individualized secondary-cause evaluation panels (hypercalciuria, turnover markers, etc.).
- Prefer lit-0015 (ISCD densitometry positions) for age-appropriate BMD reporting language in men <50.
