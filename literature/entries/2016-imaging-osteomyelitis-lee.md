# Summary

- Practical multimodality review of **osteomyelitis imaging**, with MRI as the preferred early diagnostic tool.
- Covers acute vs chronic findings, periosteal reaction, soft-tissue extension, and common mimics of marrow edema.
- Useful baseline for interpreting “mild periosteal edema” and for understanding what would constitute stronger imaging evidence of bone infection.

# Key data points

- MRI can detect osteomyelitis-related marrow signal change within **~1–2 days** of symptom onset in acute disease (sensitivity often cited ~82–100% in literature syntheses).
- Supportive MRI features: confluent T1 marrow hypointensity, fluid-sensitive marrow hyperintensity, periosteal reaction/elevation, adjacent soft-tissue edema/phlegmon/abscess, cortical destruction, sinus tracts.
- Radiographs lag days–weeks; normal early films do not exclude osteomyelitis.
- Specificity is imperfect: contusion, neuropathic bone, inflammatory arthropathy, and reactive marrow change can mimic infection.
- Contrast helps characterize abscess/sinus tracts but diagnosis is primarily marrow + clinical concordance.

# Caveats

- High MRI sensitivity applies best to clinically acute osteomyelitis, not chronic low-grade or culture-negative periostitis alone.
- “Mild periosteal edema” without aggressive marrow pattern is **not diagnostic** of osteomyelitis.
- Does not cover Bartonella-specific imaging nuances.
