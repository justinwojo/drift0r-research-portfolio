# Summary

- Case report of a 41-year-old man with subacute diffuse weakness and distal sensory loss 4 months after **gastric bypass**, with recent emesis and weekend binge drinking.
- Exam showed severe lower-extremity and hand weakness; MRI revealed T6–T7 central disc protrusion indenting cord (**spinal stenosis**), creating a dual diagnostic problem (structural myelopathy vs nutritional neuropathy).
- Clinical pattern and context supported **dry beriberi** (thiamine-deficiency neuropathy); neurosurgical stenosis finding was concurrent but not the sole explanation for the polyneuropathy picture.
- Teaching points: postoperative/post-bariatric states and reduced intake/emesis precipitate thiamine deficiency; structural spine disease can coexist and distract; repletion must not wait for perfect localization.

# Key data points

- Age 41; gastric bypass ~4 months prior (Tijuana) without follow-up.
- 3-day progressive leg/hand weakness and sensory loss.
- Risk stack: bariatric malabsorption + emesis + alcohol binges.
- MRI: T6–T7 disc protrusion with cord indentation (stenosis).
- Dry beriberi diagnosis framed by polyneuropathy + thiamine-risk context; management centers on urgent thiamine repletion and nutritional rehabilitation.
- Illustrates recurrence/new-onset risk whenever surgical stress, starvation, or GI losses hit previously vulnerable patients.
