# Summary

- Review dedicated to idiopathic osteoporosis (IOP) in men—osteoporosis without identifiable secondary cause after thorough evaluation.
- IOP often presents in middle-aged men with vertebral fractures or low BMD; can appear earlier.
- Pathophysiology may include low bone formation, reduced IGF-I, genetic susceptibility, and microarchitectural defects.
- Distinguishes age-related primary osteoporosis from earlier-onset idiopathic disease.
- Emphasizes exhaustive secondary-cause exclusion before applying the idiopathic label.
- Discusses histomorphometric heterogeneity and treatment options including anabolic therapy.
- Contextualizes male IOP within peak bone mass failure vs accelerated loss models.
- Core conceptual paper for Drift0r-like presentations once Ca/PTH/VitD and common secondaries are addressed.

# Key data points

- IOP = residual diagnosis after secondary workup.
- Associated features in literature: low formation, lowish IGF-I in subsets, family history sometimes present.
- Fracture presentation may be vertebral-predominant.
- Anabolic agents mechanistically attractive for formation-defective IOP.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Age 38 with extreme low BMD and limited fracture until atraumatic pars fits “younger male IOP” clinical pattern.
- Normal Ca/PTH/VitD matches the post-exclusion phenotype.
- High-impact history with few fractures until low-trauma event echoes quality/density dissociation sometimes seen in IOP.
- Hypercalciuria and past hypogonadism, if confirmed ongoing contributors, would reclassify away from pure idiopathic—review helps draw that line.

# Caveats

- Review article; limited original data.
- “Idiopathic” is a moving target as occult secondaries are found.
- Does not replace quantitative BTM or genetic evaluation when indicated.
