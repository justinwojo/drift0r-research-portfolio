# Summary

- Large retrospective patient/family survey quantifying the **rare-disease diagnostic odyssey** across Europe via EURORDIS Rare Barometer.
- Average **total diagnosis time (TDT) ≈ 4.7 years**; 56% waited >6 months after first medical contact; a substantial minority wait many years.
- Delay determinants include early-life symptom onset, female sex, region, number of professionals consulted, misdiagnosis, genetic disease, family history, and unmet psychosocial/financial support needs.
- Policy/clinical takeaway: prolonged multi-specialty journeys are the norm, not an individual failure—and multi-hit, adult-onset, hard-to-classify phenotypes can sit even further outside standard pathways.

# Key data points

- n = 6507 people living with 1675 rare diseases in 41 countries.
- Mean TDT 4.7 years; majority of delay after first medical contact (health-system phase), not pre-care delay.
- Odds of longer delay elevated for childhood (OR 3.11) and adolescent (OR 4.79) onset vs later onset; female OR 1.22; genetic disease OR 1.33; misdiagnosis OR 2.48; higher number of professionals consulted OR 5.15 (highest-tier comparison).
- Northern/Western Europe also associated with longer reported TDT in models (interpretation complex: detection, expectations, case-mix).
- Informs goals such as IRDiRC “diagnosis within 1 year” and Rare 2030 six-month aspirations—currently unmet for many.

# Caveats

- Self-report retrospective survey; recall and selection bias.
- “Rare disease” cohort after eventual diagnosis—does not measure still-undiagnosed persons.
- Geographic/system differences limit direct U.S. transfer.
- Not medical advice.
