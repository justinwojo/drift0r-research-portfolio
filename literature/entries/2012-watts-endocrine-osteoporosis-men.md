# Summary

- First major society CPG dedicated to osteoporosis **in men**: who to test, how to diagnose, secondary workup, and pharmacologic treatment.
- Recommends DXA for men ≥70 and for younger men with risk factors (prior fracture, hypogonadism, glucocorticoid use, low body weight, etc.).
- Pharmacologic therapy for men ≥50 with hip/spine fragility fracture, T-score ≤ −2.5, or high fracture risk by BMD + clinical factors; calcium/vitamin D and lifestyle are foundational.
- Explicitly flags **hypogonadism, idiopathic hypercalciuria, GI malabsorption, and medications** as secondary drivers—core to any analyst reading a markedly low BMD for age in a young male.

# Key data points

- Central DXA (spine, hip); 33% radius when spine/hip unusable or hyperparathyroidism suspected.
- Laboratory evaluation for contributing causes is recommended in men with osteoporosis (CBC, chemistry, 25-OH D, total T, 24-h urine calcium, and selected additional tests).
- Treat adult men with spine/hip fractures or T ≤ −2.5; high-risk osteopenia may also warrant treatment.
- Bisphosphonates have the strongest evidence in men; teriparatide for high-risk or failed antiresorptive; consider testosterone only if concurrent hypogonadism (bone benefit secondary).
- Monitor with serial DXA; avoid attributing all male bone loss to “aging” without secondary workup.

# Caveats

- **FORMALLY RETIRED** by the Endocrine Society on **2025-10-16** (PMID 41313253; DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgaf645). The issuing society no longer endorses this guideline; historical context only. Alias of lit-0001.
- 2012—predates romosozumab, modern anabolic-first strategies, and some densitometry nuance (pair with ISCD young-adult positions / lit-0015 and 2024 ASBMR goal-directed statement).
- Most pharmacologic trials still extrapolate from postmenopausal women; male-specific fracture endpoint data are thinner.
- Does not address inflammatory vs non-inflammatory joint disease or infection differentials.
