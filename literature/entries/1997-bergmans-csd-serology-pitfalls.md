# Summary

- Systematically evaluated B. henselae IFA and ELISA IgM/IgG assays for CSD diagnosis and documented major performance limitations.
- IgG assay sensitivities were very low in some antigen preparations (e.g., ~41% for IFA with non-cocultivated antigen); IgM performance also highly method-dependent.
- Demonstrates that serologic test characteristics vary dramatically with antigen preparation and assay design—creating false reassurance or false alarms.
- Supports multi-modal diagnosis rather than reliance on a single antibody result.

# Key data points

- IgG IFA sensitivity as low as 40.9% with non-cocultivated antigen in this evaluation.
- IgM sensitivities also variable; assay design critically affects clinical utility.
- Highlights need for standardized antigens and careful cutoff interpretation.
- Pre-dates modern immunoblots/LDTs but principles still apply to specialty serology.

# Caveats

- 1997 assays are not identical to current IGeneX immunoblots.
- Low sensitivity can also cause false negatives—does not prove patient is uninfected.
- Study focused on typical CSD, not chronic multi-system presentations.
- Contradicts over-confident serology-only diagnosis more than the infection hypothesis itself.
