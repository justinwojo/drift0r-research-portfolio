# Summary

- Open-access review of past and present treatment strategies for human babesiosis.
- Current standard combinations—**atovaquone + azithromycin** or **clindamycin + quinine**—were largely repurposed from other apicomplexan diseases rather than purpose-built for *Babesia*.
- Highlights adverse events, **emerging drug resistance**, and need for new *Babesia*-specific therapeutics—especially relevant to relapse/persistent infection scenarios.

# Key data points

- Preferred modern regimen in mild–moderate disease: atovaquone + azithromycin.
- Alternative: clindamycin + quinine (more toxicity).
- Patient-relevant hybrid: atovaquone is often combined with azithromycin; clindamycin appears in severe-disease IV strategies and in some real-world oral combinations (as in this case’s courses).
- Resistance mutations (e.g., cytochrome b / atovaquone-related) reported in relapsing/immunocompromised contexts in the wider literature this review synthesizes.
- Pipeline discussion: need safer, resistance-resilient agents (research stage).

# Caveats

- Treatment review assumes infection is correctly diagnosed.
- Does not resolve FISH+/PCR− diagnostic controversies.
- Resistance data largely from severe/immunocompromised or in vitro contexts—not proven in this patient.
- Non-durable benefit can also be anti-inflammatory antibiotic effect without babesiosis.
