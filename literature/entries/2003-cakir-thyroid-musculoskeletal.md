# Summary

- Evaluated prevalence of adhesive capsulitis, Dupuytren’s contracture, trigger finger, limited joint mobility, and carpal tunnel syndrome across thyroid diseases and functional states (n=137).
- Musculoskeletal disorders commonly accompany thyroid dysfunction—not only hypothyroidism.
- Carpal tunnel syndrome significantly more prevalent in hypothyroid patients (30.4%, P=0.004).
- Adhesive capsulitis highest in subclinical thyrotoxicosis (17.4%); Dupuytren’s, limited joint mobility, and CTS commonest in hypothyroidism.

# Key data points

- Cohort mix: thyrotoxic 30.6%, subclinical thyrotoxic 16.8%, euthyroid 28.5%, subclinical hypo 7.3%, hypothyroid 16.8%.
- Overall rates: adhesive capsulitis 10.9%, Dupuytren’s 8.8%, limited joint mobility 4.4%, trigger finger 2.9%, CTS 9.5%.
- Hypothyroid subgroup: Dupuytren’s 21.7%, limited joint mobility 8.7%, CTS 30.4%.
- Authors conclude MSK disorders accompany both hypo- and hyperfunction, supporting thyroid status assessment in MSK complaints.

# Caveats

- Cross-sectional associations; not proof of causality for each MSK finding.
- Does not address testosterone deficiency arthropathy or thiamine-related neuropathy.
- Thyroid status in case should be checked against endocrine summary, not assumed abnormal.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
