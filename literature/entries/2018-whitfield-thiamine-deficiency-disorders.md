# Summary

- Comprehensive open-access review of **thiamine deficiency disorders (TDD)**: clinical spectra, biomarkers, prevalence, and control strategies.
- Integrates dry beriberi, wet beriberi, infantile beriberi, and Wernicke–Korsakoff spectrum under a unified TDD framework.
- Discusses laboratory assessment (whole-blood thiamine diphosphate preferred over serum in many settings), assay limitations, and clinical response as a diagnostic adjunct.
- Useful for analysts interpreting pre- and post-repletion labs (case: serum 7 nmol/L low → whole blood high after repletion) and for understanding recurrence risk under metabolic stress.

# Key data points

- Thiamine has a short body half-life; continuous intake needed; stores can deplete within weeks of poor intake/increased demand.
- Erythrocyte/whole-blood thiamine diphosphate (ThDP) is generally preferred functional status marker vs serum thiamine alone.
- Clinical syndromes: dry beriberi (sensorimotor neuropathy), wet beriberi (cardiac), WE/Korsakoff (CNS).
- Risk settings: polished-rice diets, alcoholism, bariatric surgery, critical illness, hypermetabolic states, recurrent vomiting.
- Treatment response can be rapid for acute features; neuropathy recovery may take months—matches case narrative of hours–days symptomatic relief and longer full recovery.

# Caveats

- Global nutrition focus; not a US specialty-society practice guideline.
- Does not address whether residual multi-system disease after repletion shares etiology with TDD.
- Serum vs whole-blood assays are not interchangeable—note method when comparing values.
