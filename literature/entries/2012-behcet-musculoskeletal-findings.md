# Summary

- Behçet’s disease is a multi-system variable-vessel vasculitis defined by recurrent oral ± genital ulceration, uveitis, skin pathergy/lesions, and vascular events.
- Musculoskeletal involvement is common: non-erosive, non-deforming oligo/polyarthritis or arthralgia, often knees/ankles/wrists; usually not spine/sacroiliitis-dominant.
- Arthritis is typically inflammatory during flares; acute-phase reactants often rise with systemic activity.
- Permanent joint destruction is uncommon compared with RA.

# Key data points

- Arthritis/arthralgia reported in roughly 40–60% of BD cohorts depending on definition.
- Diagnosis requires classic mucocutaneous/ocular features (ISG/ICBD criteria)—joint pain alone is never enough.
- Vascular thrombosis and uveitis drive much of the morbidity.
- HLA-B51 association varies by ancestry; not diagnostic alone.

# Caveats

- Incomplete Behçet and neuro-Behçet phenotypes exist but still usually declare cardinal features over years.
- Geographic epidemiology matters for pretest probability (Texas non-Silk Road baseline lower).
