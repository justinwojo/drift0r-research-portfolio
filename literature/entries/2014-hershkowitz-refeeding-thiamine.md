# Summary

- Case report of thiamine deficiency in the setting of self-induced refeeding syndrome after prolonged starvation.
- Refeeding syndrome: life-threatening fluid/electrolyte and metabolic shifts when nutrition is restarted in malnourished patients.
- Carbohydrate reintroduction drives insulin-mediated shifts and sharply increases thiamine utilization as a cofactor in glucose metabolism.
- Authors highlight risk of neurologic and systemic collapse when thiamine is not repleted before or with refeeding.

# Key data points

- RFS typically appears within hours to ~5 days of reintroducing nutrition after deprivation.
- Hallmark electrolyte issues: hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, plus thiamine deficiency.
- Neurologic TDDs in this context include Wernicke encephalopathy and dry beriberi neuropathy.
- Preventive thiamine before/with feeding is standard in high-risk refeeding protocols.
- Condition can be lethal if unrecognized.
