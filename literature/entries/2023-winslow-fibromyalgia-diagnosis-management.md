# Summary

- Fibromyalgia is framed as a chronic centralized pain syndrome with disordered nociceptive processing.
- Core features: diffuse chronic pain, poor sleep, fatigue, cognitive dysfunction, mood disturbance; more frequent in women (~2% US prevalence cited).
- Diagnosis uses clinical criteria (ACR or AAPT); establishing the diagnosis can reduce unnecessary testing when presentation is classic.
- Management is multidisciplinary: education, exercise, CBT; duloxetine, milnacipran, pregabalin, amitriptyline may help; NSAIDs/opioids lack benefit evidence for fibromyalgia.

# Key data points

- FM is a clinical diagnosis; routine extensive autoimmune panels not required in typical presentations without rheumatic red flags.
- Comorbid functional somatic syndromes, psychiatric diagnoses, and true rheumatologic disease can coexist—FM does not exclude organic disease.
- Screening tools (e.g., Fibromyalgia Rapid Screening Tool) exist for diffuse chronic pain.
- Nonpharmacologic therapies are foundational; drug choices target symptom clusters, not inflammation.

# Caveats

- FM is a diagnosis of pattern and exclusion of competing explanations; applying it early can freeze workup.
- Does not explain structural spine disease, DXA-confirmed osteoporosis, or treatment-responsive neuropathic beriberi history.
- Centralized pain can be *secondary* to organic drivers; coexistence ≠ causation.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
