# Summary

- Perspective piece arguing that the traditional label **“chronic Q fever”** should be retired in favor of **persistent focalized *Coxiella burnetii* infection** (endocarditis, vascular graft/aneurysm infection, osteoarticular foci, etc.).
- Modern diagnostics (imaging, serology with phase I IgG, PCR) allow identification of a **specific infected focus** rather than a free-floating “chronic” syndrome.
- Goal: improve prognosis by defining and treating the focus rather than treating a serologic label alone.

# Key data points

- Phase I IgG titers and clinical/imaging evidence of a persistent focus define disease needing prolonged combination therapy in specialist practice.
- Osteoarticular *Coxiella* infection (osteomyelitis, tenosynovitis, spondylodiscitis) is a recognized focalized form.
- Serology without a focus can mislead; cross-reactivity and cutoff interpretation are specialist-level.
- Nomenclature shift aligns with Eldin et al. 2017 paradigm change review.
