# Summary

- Open-access review of magnesium’s role in bone health and osteoporosis pathogenesis.
- Mg deficiency contributes to osteoporosis **directly** (hydroxyapatite crystal formation, osteoblast/osteoclast balance, bone stiffness) and **indirectly** (PTH secretion/activity, low-grade inflammation, vitamin D metabolism interactions).
- Both chronically low and excessively high magnesium can be adverse for bone—homeostasis matters more than “more is better.”
- Epidemiologic and experimental data link low Mg status to lower BMD; interventional evidence remains incomplete.

# Key data points

- ~50–60% of body magnesium resides in bone; surface bone Mg buffers serum levels—**normal serum Mg does not guarantee replete tissue stores**.
- Hypomagnesemia mobilizes bone-surface Mg; prolonged deficiency impairs mineralization quality.
- Mg is a cofactor for hundreds of enzymes; related nutrient pathways (including thiamine-dependent metabolism) can be secondarily affected when Mg is low.
- Clinical implication for secondary OP workups: consider dietary Mg, serum Mg, and—when available—more informative status markers, not serum alone.

# Caveats

- Review synthesizing heterogeneous human and animal data; supplementation trials mixed.
- High-dose Mg is not a stand-alone osteoporosis drug.
- Serum Mg cutoffs miss intracellular depletion (see also lit-0250).
