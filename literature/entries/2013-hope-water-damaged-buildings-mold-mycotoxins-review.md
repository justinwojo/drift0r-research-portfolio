# Summary

- Narrative review arguing that illness from water-damaged buildings (WDBs) reflects mixed exposures: mold spores/hyphal fragments, mycotoxins, bacteria/endotoxins, cell-wall components, and other factors.
- Proposed mechanisms: inflammation, oxidative stress, toxicity, infection, allergy, and irritant effects.
- Reviews treatment approaches used in environmental-medicine practice: exposure avoidance, glutathione/antioxidants, antifungals, and sequestering agents (cholestyramine, charcoal, clay, chlorella), probiotics, induced sweating.
- Functions as a primary-care-facing synthesis of the **pro-causation clinical literature** rather than a controlled evidence grade.

# Key data points

- Emphasizes avoidance of ongoing WDB exposure and contaminated belongings as foundational.
- Treatment discussion is largely based on case series, mechanistic speculation, and practice experience—not large RCTs.
- Bundles mycotoxins with non-mycotoxin microbial products as co-exposures.
- OA full text allows direct inspection of citation quality.

# Caveats

- Not a systematic review; high risk of selection bias toward positive mechanistic claims.
- Treatments listed (binders, empiric antifungals) lack robust controlled evidence for “mycotoxin illness.”
- Must be read against AAAAI 2006 and Cochrane remediation evidence.
- Research catalog only.
