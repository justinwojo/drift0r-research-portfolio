# Summary

- Retrospective comparison of **91 biopsy-confirmed** chronic diffuse lacrimal gland enlargements: nonspecific dacryoadenitis (DA, n=42), **IgG4-related dacryoadenitis** (n=33), and lymphoma (n=16).
- Discriminators: age (lymphoma older), **unilaterality** (more common in lymphoma), gland size, “wedge sign,” and **extra-orbital head/neck involvement** (infraorbital nerve, sinuses, salivary glands—more frequent in IgG4-RD).
- Reinforces that unilateral lacrimal swelling is a high-value finding that should trigger structured DDx (infection, IgG4-ROD, sarcoid, Sjögren, GPA, lymphoma) rather than assumed allergic eyelid disease.
- Imaging can prioritize but does not replace biopsy when malignancy or IgG4-RD must be confirmed/excluded.

# Key data points

- Lymphoma: older (mean ~60 y) and more often unilateral (62.5% vs 31% DA, 15% IgG4-RD).
- DA: smaller glands; lower wedge-sign frequency.
- IgG4-RD: more extra-orbital involvement—infraorbital nerve 36%, paranasal sinus 73%, salivary gland 59%.
- Modalities: CT n=73; MRI n=43 with ADC/enhancement patterns assessed.
- Biopsy remains reference standard across all three entities.

**Score:** 4

**Why:**
- Documented lacrimal swelling event + persistent **serum IgG4↑** without classic fibrotic multi-organ IgG4-RD makes IgG4-related dacryoadenitis vs nonspecific DA vs lymphoma a live differential.
- Unilateral emphasis matches acute asymmetric presentations better than classic bilateral sicca patterns.
- Supports imaging + serology + (when indicated) biopsy pathway rather than dismissing as isolated ENT noise.

# Caveats

- Tertiary biopsy series—selection toward more severe/uncertain lesions.
- Does not deeply analyze sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, or bacterial dacryoadenitis (must keep in clinical DDx).
- Serum IgG4 elevation alone ≠ IgG4-ROD (known false positives elsewhere in corpus).
- Not medical advice.
