# Summary

- Review of neurologic and neuropsychiatric syndromes linked to Bartonella spp., including peripheral neuropathies, neuroretinitis, encephalitis/encephalopathy, cerebral vasculitis, and emerging neuropsychiatric associations.
- Documents multiple species beyond B. henselae (B. quintana, B. koehlerae, rodent-associated species, etc.) and notes cases without documented animal/vector exposure.
- Highlights molecular studies finding Bartonella DNA more often in blood of patients with schizophrenia/psychosis than controls (citing Lashnits and Delaney work).
- Argues for greater clinical awareness and One Health framing of neurobartonellosis as under-recognized.

# Key data points

- Spectrum includes neuralgic amyotrophy, CRPS, CIDP, cranial neuropathies, GBS-like presentations, transverse myelopathy, neuroretinitis, encephalopathy, vasculitis/aneurysm, neuropsychiatric disorders.
- Recent ddPCR studies: higher Bartonella DNA detection in psychosis vs controls (details in Delaney 2024).
- Diagnostic basis for inclusion generally required serology, molecular testing, and/or pathology.
- Authors (Maggi, Breitschwerdt) have commercial diagnostic COI (Galaxy Diagnostics).

# Caveats

- Review of heterogeneous case literature; association ≠ causation for neuropsychiatric disease.
- Heavy representation of specialized diagnostic methods and author group with commercial interest.
- Drift0r’s formal psych evaluations reversed primary SSD; neurobartonellosis remains hypothesis-level for this case.
- Does not establish treatment algorithms for chronic neuropsychiatric presentations.
