# Summary

- Wilson disease (*ATP7B*) causes **copper overload** with hepatic disease, neuropsychiatric movement disorders, Kayser–Fleischer rings, and low ceruloplasmin in most patients.
- Neurologic Wilson: tremor, dystonia, parkinsonism, dysarthria—not primarily small-fiber burning pain.
- Bone/joint: osteopenia/arthritis can occur secondary to chronic liver disease and renal tubular effects, but are not the usual presenting isolated syndrome.
- Diagnosis uses ceruloplasmin, 24h urine copper, liver copper, slit-lamp exam, and *ATP7B* genetics; treatment is chelation/zinc under specialists.

# Key data points

- Age of onset often childhood–young adult; untreated hepatic failure or progressive neurology.
- Ceruloplasmin is low in most neurologic Wilson cases but false positives/negatives exist.
- **Opposite copper problem** from acquired copper deficiency myeloneuropathy.
- Family screening is mandatory after proband confirmation.

# Caveats

- Low ceruloplasmin also occurs in copper deficiency—interpret with serum copper and clinical context.
- Guidelines date to 2012; local protocols may update chelator choices.
