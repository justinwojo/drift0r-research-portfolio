# Summary

- Case of a 61-year-old man with chronic alcoholism and treated Wernicke encephalopathy who developed late-onset hypothermia (35.0°C) after glycerin enema, 3 months after WE diagnosis.
- Brain MRI showed mammillary body and hypothalamic atrophy (thermoregulatory center).
- Autonomic testing: absent sympathetic skin responses in hands and abnormal orthostatic hypotension.
- Hypothermia improved with rewarming; constipation management avoided recurrent hypothermic episodes.
- Literature reviewed: hypothermia is uncommon in WE (~1–4% in classic series) and usually described in the acute phase; late-onset hypothermia is rare.

# Key data points

- Proposed mechanism: posterior hypothalamic dysfunction + autonomic failure.
- Acute WE hypothermia can reverse with thiamine; untreated historical cases had high mortality.
- This case showed late hypothermia despite ongoing oral thiamine 300 mg/day and normal measured thiamine at evaluation.
- Autonomic neuropathy signs accompanied thermoregulatory failure.
- Temperature dysregulation is a recognized atypical WE feature.

# Caveats

- Alcoholic WE with structural hypothalamic atrophy—not pure dry beriberi.
- Late hypothermia may reflect residual structural injury rather than ongoing low circulating B1.
- Axillary temperature measurement limitation noted by authors.
