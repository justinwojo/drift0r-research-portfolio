# Summary

- Clinicopathologic series of **25 skeletal coccidioidomycosis** cases (bones, joints, or both) from University of Arizona and Mayo Clinic Arizona (2000–2012).
- Infections often involved **distal extremities (68%)**, especially the **wrist (32%)**; many patients had prior cocci, autoimmune disease, diabetes, malignancy, or iatrogenic immunosuppression.
- Cultures and serology were positive in the large majority of tested patients; histopathology shows granulomatous inflammation with spherules when sampled.

# Key data points

- Median age ~40; male predominance in this series.
- Symptom duration median ~3 months (pain/arthralgia, swelling).
- Combined osteomyelitis + septic arthritis common.
- Reminder that chronic mono/oligoarticular or bony lesions in SW residents may be fungal, not only bacterial or autoimmune.

# Caveats

- Surgical/pathology referral bias (more severe/local disease).
- Arizona-centric; Texas risk is real but uneven by region.
- Abstract-only for some readers.
