# Summary

- Laboratory series applying **Babesia genus FISH** plus separate **IFA IgM/IgG for *B. microti* and *B. duncani*** to clinical samples referred for tick-borne disease testing (USA, Europe, Australia, elsewhere).
- Authors conclude parallel FISH + dual-species IFA provides more diagnostic information than either alone and that *B. duncani* infections may be more widespread than traditionally recognized.
- Important for interpreting specialty panels that report FISH and species serologies separately from commercial microti PCR.

# Key data points

- Sample context: EDTA blood + serum from patients submitted to IGeneX for routine TBD testing (n=390 in methods description of companion workstream).
- FISH = active organism RNA in erythrocytes (claimed); IFA = humoral response to species antigens.
- Discordant patterns expected: serology+/FISH− (past or early/late), FISH+/serology− (early, impaired antibody, non-cross-reactive species), dual positive.
- Authors emphasize *B. duncani* seroreactivity outside classic West Coast narrative.
- Same commercial ecosystem as lit-0062; interpret with COI awareness.

**Why:**
Matches the specialty-lab multi-test philosophy applied to the patient (FISH, IB/PCR options for microti/duncani). Helps frame how a lab might report FISH+ with negative microti/duncani PCR/IB—possible early infection, non-microti/non-duncani piroplasm, low antibody, or assay error. Patient’s dual time-point FISH+ without seroconversion/PCR is a high-stakes discordance this paper’s framework tries (imperfectly) to address.

# Caveats

- Referral population is highly selected (patients already seeking specialty TBD testing)—positive rates not generalizable.
- No gold-standard adjudicated PCR-negative FISH+ false-positive rate against microscopy/culture/sequencing for all cases.
- Industry COI (IGeneX/ID-FISH).
- Does not prove chronic multi-system disease causation from isolated FISH+.
