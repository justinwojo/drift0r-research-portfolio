# Summary

- SAD = recurrent infections with **normal** total Igs (and often normal subclasses) but impaired antibody responses to polysaccharide antigens.
- Diagnosis typically hinges on inadequate response to 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV23), with contested cutoffs for “adequate.”
- Management options include antibiotic prophylaxis and, in selected patients, IgG replacement—evidence base is limited/expert-opinion-heavy.
- Emphasizes individualized interpretation: age, serotype, prior conjugate vaccines, and assay method all matter.

# Key data points

- Classic SAD definition: normal IgG/A/M (± subclasses) + poor polysaccharide vaccine response + clinical infections.
- Protective titer concepts (e.g., ~1.3 µg/mL post-PPSV23 for many serotypes) are guideline-derived and debated.
- Conjugate vaccine (PCV) responses may be preserved when pure polysaccharide responses fail—does not exclude SAD.
- Therapy ladder: infection prevention/prophylaxis first; Ig replacement considered when infections severe/recurrent despite prophylaxis.

# Caveats

- Baseline low titers alone ≠ SAD; diagnosis requires documented inadequate *vaccine response* under standardized criteria.
- Patient has IgG4↑/IgG1↓ — subclass abnormality may place him outside pure classic SAD definitions (some classify separately).
- Controversies in cutoffs mean over- and under-diagnosis are common.
- Does not explain osteoporosis by itself; relevance is infection susceptibility / immune dysregulation context.
- Published corrigendum exists (Front Immunol 2018 doi 10.3389/fimmu.2018.00450 / PMID 29576764) — check publisher record.
- Not medical advice; immunology specialist confirmation required for any clinical use.
