# Summary

- Randomized trial in men with idiopathic hypercalciuria and recurrent calcium oxalate stones comparing (1) a traditional low-calcium diet vs (2) a normal-calcium, low-animal-protein, low-salt diet.
- Over 5 years, the normal-calcium / low-Na / low-protein diet produced fewer stone recurrences and a greater reduction in urine calcium (and oxalate) than calcium restriction.
- Underpins modern advice: do **not** restrict dietary calcium in hypercalciuric stone formers; target sodium and animal protein instead—directly aligned with urology recommendations already noted in this case.

# Key data points

- Population: men with idiopathic hypercalciuria and recurrent calcium oxalate stones.
- Intervention: normal calcium + low animal protein + low salt vs low-calcium diet; both with fluid advice.
- 5-year follow-up: fewer recurrences on the normal-Ca / low-Na / low-protein diet.
- Urine calcium fell more on the low-salt / low-protein arm than on low-calcium alone (as cited across secondary literature).
- Low-calcium diets can raise oxalate absorption and worsen negative calcium balance—relevant when osteoporosis coexists.

# Caveats

- Recurrent stone formers; absolute recurrence rates higher than a single-stone patient.
- Diet RCT, not a thiazide or anabolic-bone trial.
- Adherence and 24h urine Na monitoring needed to operationalize “low salt.”
