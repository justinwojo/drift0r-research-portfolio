# Summary

- *COL1A1*/*COL1A2*-related osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) causes fractures with minimal trauma, variable dentinogenesis imperfecta, adult-onset hearing loss, and a severity spectrum from perinatal lethal to mild non-deforming disease.
- Four classical clinical groupings (non-deforming with blue sclerae; perinatally lethal; progressively deforming; common variable with normal sclerae) aid prognosis but do not replace molecular diagnosis.
- Diagnosis: clinical/radiographic OI features plus heterozygous pathogenic *COL1A1* or *COL1A2* variant on molecular testing.
- Management is multidisciplinary (ortho, PT/OT, bone specialists); bisphosphonates used selectively (vertebral fractures, frequent long-bone fractures, severe phenotypes).

# Key data points

- Inheritance is autosomal dominant; de novo variants and parental mosaicism (up to ~16% in some parental cohorts) matter for recurrence risk.
- Mild OI can present as “early-onset osteoporosis” or recurrent fractures without classic deformity—important monogenic DDx in young adults.
- Extra-skeletal issues: DI, hearing loss, basilar impression, pulmonary morbidity in severe disease.
- Agents/circumstances to avoid in severe disease include high-impact collision sports and unnecessary prolonged immobilization after fracture.

**Why:**
- **Partial:** Markedly low BMD for age / fragility is exactly why mild OI remains on the monogenic bone DDx.
- **Mismatches:** Public case narrative emphasizes multi-system infection/immune/endocrine/nutrient threads (*SLC7A9*, HαT, thiamine response, hypogonadism history) not classic OI (blue sclerae, DI, childhood fracture cascade, progressive deformity).
- Genetic testing for collagen genes (or broader skeletal dysplasia panels) is a **rule-out** step when secondary workup is incomplete—not a high prior without OI red flags.
- Does not explain polyarthralgia-without-synovitis, tryptase genetics, or beriberi physiology.

# Caveats

- Non-*COL1A1/2* OI genes exist; negative COL1 sequencing does not exclude all OI if phenotype is strong.
- Bisphosphonate strategy differs from postmenopausal osteoporosis algorithms.
- Not medical advice.
