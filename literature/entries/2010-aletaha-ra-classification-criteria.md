# Summary

- Replaces 1987 criteria with a score-based system designed to classify earlier RA for intervention studies and clinical research.
- Definite RA classification requires confirmed synovitis in ≥1 joint, absence of a better alternative diagnosis, and total score ≥6/10 across four domains.
- Domains: joint involvement, serology (RF/ACPA), symptom duration (<6 vs ≥6 weeks), acute-phase reactants (CRP/ESR).
- Seronegative patients can still classify as RA if joint involvement and acute-phase domains score high enough—but **synovitis remains obligatory**.

# Key data points

- Score range 0–10; ≥6 = definite RA for classification purposes.
- Serology high-positive RF or ACPA contributes substantially; double-negative serology scores 0 in that domain.
- Normal CRP and ESR score 0 in acute-phase domain.
- Classification ≠ clinical diagnosis in every edge case, but operationalizes what “RA-like” requires: synovitis first.

# Caveats

- Classification criteria are for research standardization, not a complete clinical differential.
- Seronegative RA exists but still requires synovitis; this case lacks that anchor.
- Does not evaluate non-RA inflammatory diseases (SpA, crystal, CTD) in depth.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
