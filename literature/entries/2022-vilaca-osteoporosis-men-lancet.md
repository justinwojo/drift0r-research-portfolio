# Summary

- Major 2022 review of osteoporosis in men in Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology.
- Diagnostic densitometric criterion analogous to women: T-score ≤ −2.5 at hip or lumbar spine (young-adult reference), with clinical caveats by age.
- Sex steroids critical for male bone; estrogen (via aromatization) plays a key role as in women.
- Men generally have larger bones and less lifetime bone loss, fracture less often, but higher mortality after fracture.
- Secondary osteoporosis is more common in men than women—central clinical message.
- Management: lifestyle, calcium/vitamin D, exercise; bisphosphonates, denosumab, and teriparatide increase BMD and are used pharmacologically.
- Discusses persistent controversies (who to screen, which normative database, testosterone’s therapeutic role).
- Best single high-level 2020s overview for the corpus.

# Key data points

- T-score ≤ −2.5 densitometric threshold described for men (context: typically ≥50 for WHO-style diagnosis).
- Secondary osteoporosis more frequent in men.
- Estrogen key mediator of male bone health.
- Pharmacologic options with BMD efficacy: bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide.
