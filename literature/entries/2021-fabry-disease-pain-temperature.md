# Summary

- Fabry disease (*GLA*, X-linked α-Gal A deficiency) causes glycosphingolipid storage with early **small-fiber neuropathic pain** (acroparesthesias), often triggered by heat, fever, exercise, or stress.
- Autonomic features include **hypohidrosis → heat intolerance**, GI dysmotility, and orthostasis; later kidney, heart, and stroke risk dominate morbidity.
- Males with classic disease present in childhood/adolescence; late-onset variants present with cardiac/renal disease and milder pain.
- Diagnosis in males: enzyme assay + *GLA* sequencing; females need genetics (enzyme may be normal).

# Key data points

- Pain crises can be disabling and misdiagnosed as growing pains, erythromelalgia, or functional pain.
- Both heat **and** cold can trigger symptoms in some patients, but **heat intolerance from hypohidrosis is the textbook autonomic signature**.
- Angiokeratomas, cornea verticillata, and proteinuria are diagnostic clues when present.
- Enzyme replacement / chaperone therapy exist for confirmed disease (specialist care).

- **Test threshold:** α-Gal A enzyme (male) is inexpensive relative to missing a treatable storage disease—reasonable “don’t miss” if SFN workup expands.

# Caveats

- Late-onset cardiac variants may lack childhood pain history.
- Do not equate cold intolerance alone with Fabry.
- Variant of uncertain significance requires biochemical correlation.
