# Summary

- Among 296 patients seen by a rheumatologist, anti-Bartonella antibodies (B. henselae, B. koehlerae, or B. vinsonii subsp. berkhoffii) were found in 185 (62%) and Bartonella spp. bacteremia by PCR methods in 122 (41.1%).
- Pre-referral diagnoses included Lyme disease (46.6%), arthralgia/arthritis (20.6%), chronic fatigue (19.6%), and fibromyalgia (6.1%).
- B. henselae bacteremia associated with prior neurology referral (blurred vision, numbness, subcortical deficits); authors explicitly state causality for joint pain cannot be established.
- PCR positivity often required testing blood, serum, and enrichment culture (BAPGM) components—single-compartment PCR missed many positives.

# Key data points

- Seroreactivity 62%; bacteremia 41.1% in this selected cohort (not population prevalence).
- Figure-level finding: few patients positive in blood + serum + enrichment simultaneously; enrichment added yield.
- B. henselae PCR+ associated with blurred vision (aOR 2.37), numbness (aOR 2.74), neurology consult (aOR 2.76).
- No self-reported animal/insect exposure associations with species-specific PCR positivity in adjusted models.
- Authors caution against inferring that Bartonella causes the rheumatic syndrome from this design alone.

# Caveats

- Highly selected rheumatology population in Lyme-endemic area—detection rates not generalizable.
- Cross-sectional; no untreated controls matched for symptoms; CDC and Raoult commentaries raised methodologic concerns.
- BAPGM enrichment PCR is specialized (Galaxy/Breitschwerdt ecosystem); not equivalent to commercial PCR/FISH negative results.
- Does not validate IGeneX IgM immunoblot specifically.
