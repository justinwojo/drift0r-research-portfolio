# Summary

- Idiopathic juvenile osteoporosis (IJO) is a **primary** rare bone demineralization disorder presenting in childhood/adolescence with back and extremity pain, walking difficulty, vertebral compression, and metaphyseal/long-bone fractures.
- Diagnosis is clinical after **exclusion of secondary causes** and of defined monogenic osteoporoses (OI spectrum, *LRP5*, *WNT1*, *PLS3*, etc. as appropriate).
- Orphanet catalogs IJO as a rare disease entity (ORPHA:85193) with highly frequent features of bone pain, osteoporosis, and recurrent fractures.
- Some susceptibility associations (e.g., *WNT1* listed in Orphanet gene resources as a major susceptibility factor in related nosology) blur boundaries with monogenic early-onset osteoporosis.

# Key data points

- Onset typically prepubertal to adolescence; many cases improve after puberty, but residual deformity/low peak bone mass can persist into adulthood.
- Workup mandate: exclude endocrine, GI malabsorption, inflammatory, renal phosphate wasting, immobilization, and medication causes before labeling “idiopathic.”
- Adult presentation of “idiopathic osteoporosis in young men” is a related but not identical clinical problem—IJO is the pediatric-onset rare-disease label.
- Management is supportive/specialty metabolic bone care; no single FDA-labeled IJO-specific drug.

# Caveats

- Orphanet pages are curated summaries, not peer-reviewed trials; depth varies by entry update cycle.
- “Idiopathic” shrinks as genetic testing expands.
