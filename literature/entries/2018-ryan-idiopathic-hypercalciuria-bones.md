# Summary

- Clinical review of idiopathic hypercalciuria (IH) as a shared risk factor for kidney stones and osteoporosis.
- IH defined by excess urinary calcium without hypercalcemia or other obvious drivers of calciuria.
- Present in a substantial fraction of stone formers and also in osteoporotic patients without stone history.
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria noted in 10–19% of otherwise healthy men with low bone mass.
- Directly implicated in trabecular bone loss, particularly in men.
- Normal serum calcium does not exclude clinically important renal calcium wasting.
- Management aims: reduce stone risk and protect skeleton (fluids, sodium restriction, thiazides when appropriate, dietary calcium not severely restricted).
- Critical secondary-cause paper for Drift0r’s hypercalciuria anchor.

# Key data points

- IH prevalence ~10–19% among men with low bone mass.
- Up to ~20% of postmenopausal osteoporotic women without stones may have hypercalciuria.
- Mechanism: intestinal hyperabsorption and/or renal calcium leak ± increased bone resorption.
- Thiazide diuretics can reduce calciuria and may help BMD in selected patients.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Hypercalciuria with normal Ca/PTH/VitD matches classic idiopathic hypercalciuria pattern.
- Male sex specifically associated with trabecular bone loss from IH.
- Provides a reversible/modifiable contributor alongside historical hypogonadism.
- Supports 24-h urine calcium as essential in young male markedly low BMD for age workup.

# Caveats

- PMID verification: primary access via CCJM open full text (doi 10.3949/ccjm.85a.16090).
- Not an RCT of fracture outcomes with thiazides in young men.
- Must exclude primary hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D excess, and dietary extremes before labeling IH.
- Dietary calcium restriction can worsen bone loss—avoid simplistic low-Ca diets.
