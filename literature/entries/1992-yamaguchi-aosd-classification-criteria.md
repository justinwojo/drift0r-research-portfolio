# Summary

- Multicenter Japanese derivation of **preliminary classification criteria** for adult Still’s disease (AOSD) from 90 cases vs 267 controls.
- **Major criteria:** fever ≥39 °C lasting ≥1 week; arthralgia ≥2 weeks; typical salmon-colored macular/maculopapular rash; leukocytosis ≥10,000/µL with ≥80% granulocytes.
- **Minor criteria:** sore throat; lymphadenopathy and/or splenomegaly; liver dysfunction; negative RF and ANA.
- Classification requires **≥5 criteria including ≥2 major**, plus an **exclusion process** (infection, malignancy, other rheumatic disease)—sensitivity 96.2%, specificity 92.1% in the derivation analysis.

# Key data points

- AOSD is a systemic autoinflammatory disease with quotidian high spiking fevers, evanescent rash, and arthritis/arthralgia.
- Modern practice heavily weights **extreme hyperferritinemia** and high **CRP**/inflammatory markers, though these are **not** formal Yamaguchi majors/minors.
- Yamaguchi is classification, not a standalone diagnostic blood test—exclusions are mandatory.
- Fautrel and other criteria sets exist; Yamaguchi remains most cited clinically.

# Caveats

- 1992 preliminary criteria; rare disease mimics (infection, lymphoma, other AIDs) remain critical exclusions.
- Ferritin glycosylation fraction and IL-18 used in research settings—not substitutes for clinical criteria.
