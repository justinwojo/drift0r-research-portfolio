# Summary

- ACR imaging appropriateness guidance for adults with **chronic extremity joint pain** when inflammatory arthritis, crystalline disease, or erosive OA is suspected.
- Radiographs remain first-line in most variants; advanced imaging (MRI without IV contrast, US, dual-energy CT for urate) is tiered by clinical suspicion and radiograph findings.
- Provides a structured imaging pathway that rheumatologists and MSK radiologists use when CRP/ESR are normal but symptoms persist—key for distinguishing “imaging-negative” pain from occult erosive or crystalline disease.
- Anchor card for analysts: negative inflammatory serologies + normal acute-phase reactants still need a deliberate imaging strategy, not assumption of primary psychiatric pain.

# Key data points

- Initial imaging for suspected inflammatory/crystalline/erosive OA: radiography of the symptomatic joint(s) is usually appropriate.
- After non-diagnostic radiographs, MRI without IV contrast or US often next for soft-tissue, synovitis, and marrow evaluation.
- Dual-energy CT usually appropriate when gout is suspected and radiographs/US are inconclusive.
- Contrast-enhanced MRI is not routinely first-line for this indication.
- Criteria assume a clinical pretest for inflammatory disease; pure mechanical or neuropathic pain follows other pathways.

# Caveats

- Appropriateness criteria ≠ full rheumatologic differential diagnosis or treatment guideline.
- Written for *suspected* inflammatory/crystalline disease; pure non-inflammatory pain may still be correctly radiograph-first then stop.
- Does not cover spine, SI joints, or whole-body PET pathways.
- Abstract may be free; full JACR text often institutional access.
