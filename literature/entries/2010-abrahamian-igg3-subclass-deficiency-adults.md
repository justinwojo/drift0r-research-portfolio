# Summary

- Retrospective chart review of 17 adults with selective IgG3 subclass deficiency attending a specialty immunology clinic.
- Immunologic profile: mostly normal lymphocyte subsets; decreased mitogen responses in ~33% and antigen responses in ~40%; tetanus antibody responses preserved; pneumococcal serotype responses impaired in a subset.
- Clinical phenotype dominated by recurrent upper respiratory infections; many concurrent atopic diseases (allergic rhinitis/asthma).
- IVIG associated with decreased infection frequency/severity in treated patients.

# Key data points

- Selective IgG3 deficiency can present in adulthood with recurrent URIs even when other immune numbers look broadly normal.
- Specific antibody testing (pneumococcal serotypes) is more clinically informative than subclass level alone.
- Tetanus protein antigen responses may remain intact when polysaccharide responses fail—parallel to SAD logic.
- IVIG considered when infections are recurrent despite other measures (small uncontrolled series).

# Caveats

- Small single-center retrospective series; IgG3-focused.
- IVIG outcomes uncontrolled—confounding by indication and regression to mean.
- Subclass reference ranges assay-dependent.
- Research catalog only.
