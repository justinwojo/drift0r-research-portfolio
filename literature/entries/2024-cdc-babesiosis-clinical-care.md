# Summary

- CDC clinician guidance on treating human babesiosis: most asymptomatic persons do **not** require therapy.
- Ill immunocompetent adults: typically **≥7–10 days** of dual therapy—**preferred atovaquone + azithromycin**; alternative clindamycin + quinine.
- Severe/hospitalized disease uses higher-intensity regimens (often IV azithromycin or IV clindamycin components) then step-down to oral completion of 7–10 days once improving.
- Highly immunocompromised patients may need **≥6 weeks**, smear monitoring until parasites absent for consecutive weeks, and specialist-directed multi-drug salvage if relapse.

# Key data points

- Mild–moderate outpatient (adults): atovaquone 750 mg PO BID + azithromycin 500 mg day 1 then 250 mg daily (higher azithromycin used in immunocompromise/severe disease).
- Alternative: clindamycin 600 mg PO TID + quinine 650 mg PO TID.
- Supportive care may include transfusion, exchange transfusion for high-grade parasitemia (>10%) or organ dysfunction, vasopressors, ventilation, dialysis.
- Fatigue and PCR positivity can persist weeks after clinical recovery; relapse uncommon in immunocompetent hosts after standard courses.

# Caveats

- Web page adapts IDSA 2020—check IDSA full text for grading and special populations.
- Pregnancy: clindamycin + quinine often preferred over atovaquone+azithromycin per CDC notes.
- Not medical advice; not a substitute for ID specialist care.
