# Summary

- Assesses **nerve T2 signal** and **caliber** on MR neurography as diagnostic signs of **ulnar neuropathy at the elbow (UNE)**.
- Moves beyond qualitative “bright nerve” reads toward measurable criteria that improve specificity.
- Reinforces that signal intensity alone is a weak standalone classifier.

# Key data points

- Both elevated T2 signal and nerve enlargement can occur in UNE, but **caliber change** and quantitative T2 assessment improve diagnostic confidence versus subjective signal alone.
- Because asymptomatic ulnar hyperintensity is common (Husarik and subsequent literature up to ~60%), reliable UNE imaging usually needs **enlargement**, side-to-side comparison, and clinical/EMG concordance.
- Focal caliber increase within the cubital tunnel with corresponding clinical deficit is more persuasive than mild diffuse hyperintensity.
- MR neurography is adjunctive; electrodiagnostics remain central in many pathways.

# Caveats

- Does not explain multi-nodule forearm exam findings or periosteal humeral edema.
- Protocol-dependent; not all clinical elbow MRIs are true MR neurography.
- Left index stiffness is not classic isolated ulnar neuropathy distribution alone.
