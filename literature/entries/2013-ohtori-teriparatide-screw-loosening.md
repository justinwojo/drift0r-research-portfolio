# Summary

- Prospective comparison of **teriparatide**, **risedronate**, and **no osteoporosis medication** after instrumented lumbar posterolateral fusion in postmenopausal osteoporotic women.
- Primary mechanical endpoint: **pedicle screw loosening** at 12 months—major failure mode when fusing osteoporotic bone.
- Teriparatide group had the **lowest screw-loosening rates** and improved pedicle bone quality markers vs bisphosphonate and untreated controls.
- Complements the 2012 Ohtori fusion-union study: anabolics may improve both **union** and **implant fixation**.

# Key data points

- n ≈ 62 women with osteoporosis + degenerative spondylolisthesis phenotypes undergoing 1–2 level instrumented PLF.
- Arms: teriparatide 20 µg daily SC; risedronate 2.5 mg oral daily; control without anti-osteoporosis drug.
- 12-month screw loosening incidence (study ranges): **~7–13%** teriparatide vs **~13–26%** risedronate vs **~15–25%** control.
- Authors interpret improved pedicle cortex/bone marrow quality as mechanism for better screw purchase.
- Clinical takeaway: untreated or only-antiresorptive osteoporosis still carries substantial loosening risk after instrumentation.

**Why:** Quantifies why fusion is deferred with **extreme osteoporosis**—high risk of **screw loosening/pseudarthrosis**—and why anabolic therapy is under discussion before any L5–S1 construct.

# Caveats

- Postmenopausal women; not young male secondary osteoporosis.
- Degenerative spondylolisthesis cohort, not pure isthmic L5–S1—but implant-bone mechanics still apply.
- Modest sample size; radiographic loosening definitions vary across literature.
- Not medical advice.
