# Summary

- Consensus update refining diagnosis/classification of **cutaneous and systemic mastocytosis**, bone marrow mastocytosis (BMM), and **MCAS**.
- Reiterates SM diagnosis: **major criterion** (multifocal dense MC infiltrates ≥15 in aggregates in BM/extracutaneous organ) **plus 1 minor**, **or 3 minor criteria**.
- Minor criteria include atypical MC morphology, **KIT codon 816 (or related activating) mutation**, aberrant CD25/CD2/CD30, and **basal tryptase >20 ng/mL** (with caveats when HαT or associated myeloid neoplasm is present).
- MCAS requires typical episodic systemic symptoms + event-related mediator rise (classically tryptase: baseline + 20% + 2 ng/mL) + response to anti-mediator therapy.

# Key data points

- Tryptase >20 is only a **minor** SM criterion and is **not diagnostic alone**.
- HαT should be considered when interpreting basal tryptase (adjustment concepts appear in later WHO/ICC-aligned discussions).
- KIT D816V is highly prevalent in adult SM but not 100%; sensitive assays and tissue testing matter.

**Why:** Provides the formal yardstick for the patient’s workup: tryptase <20, KIT neg, no MCAS episodes, urine mediators normal → does **not** meet MCAS and does **not** meet SM by blood markers alone. Marrow remains the only way to assess the major histologic criterion if clinical suspicion persists (e.g., severe unexplained osteoporosis).

# Caveats

- Consensus document, not a trial of biopsy strategies in HαT+ osteoporosis.
- “When to biopsy” is still clinician judgment integrating skin findings, anaphylaxis, cytoses/cytopenias, organopathy, and tryptase trajectory.
