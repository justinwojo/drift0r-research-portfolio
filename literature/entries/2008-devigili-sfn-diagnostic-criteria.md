# Summary

- Prospective diagnostic study that defined practical criteria for pure small-fibre neuropathy (SFN) when routine nerve conduction studies are normal.
- Screened 486 referrals; among 124 sensory neuropathy cases identified 67 pure SFN using a rule requiring **≥2 abnormal domains** among clinical exam, quantitative sensory testing (QST), and skin-biopsy intraepidermal nerve fibre density (IENFD).
- Skin biopsy had the highest diagnostic efficiency (88.4%) versus clinical exam (54.6%) and QST (46.9%).
- Autonomic clinical signs (peripheral vascular/sudomotor) were common (~70%); spontaneous pain dominated the phenotype.

# Key data points

- Diagnostic combinations among pure SFN: abnormal clinical + IENFD 43.3%; abnormal IENFD + QST 37.3%; abnormal clinical + QST 11.9%; all three abnormal 7.5%.
- Significant inverse correlation between distal-leg IENFD and cold/warm thresholds.
- Pinprick and thermal hypoesthesia present in ~50%; autonomic vascular signs ~70%.
- Emphasizes that electrodiagnostic large-fiber studies are typically normal in pure SFN—diagnosis needs specialized small-fiber testing.

# Caveats

- 2008 criteria later refined by the same group (2019 reappraisal).
- Nutritional neuropathy (thiamine) is mixed-fiber in many cases—pure SFN criteria may under-capture mixed disease.
- Research catalog only; not diagnostic advice.
