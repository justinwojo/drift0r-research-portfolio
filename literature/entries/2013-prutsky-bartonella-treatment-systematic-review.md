# Summary

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of treatment outcomes for the three dominant human *Bartonella* species (*B. henselae*, *B. quintana*, *B. bacilliformis*).
- Summarizes heterogeneous observational data across cat-scratch disease, endocarditis, bacteremia, and Oroya fever/verruga—highlighting how **syndrome and species drive regimen choice**, not a single “chronic Bartonella protocol.”
- Concludes that high-quality randomized evidence is sparse for many clinical scenarios; recommendations rest largely on observational series and expert guidance.

# Key data points

- Mild CSD often self-limited; azithromycin can speed lymph-node resolution in selected cases (prior RCT context).
- Severe syndromes (endocarditis, bacillary angiomatosis, neuroretinitis, osteomyelitis) typically use **combination and/or prolonged** therapy (e.g., doxycycline ± rifampin; aminoglycoside-containing regimens in endocarditis).
- Treatment failure/relapse reported; duration varies from days (mild CSD) to weeks–months (endovascular/complex disease).
- Intravenous agents appear mainly in severe/endovascular or hospitalized contexts—not as default outpatient “chronic MSK” care in mainstream literature.

# Caveats

- Mostly nonrandomized data; confounding by indication is large.
- Does not validate specialty LDT-only diagnoses.
- Cannot be read as a prescription for any individual regimen (not medical advice).
