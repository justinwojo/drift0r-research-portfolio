# Summary

- Contemporary review of immunoglobulin G subclass deficiency (IGGSCD) in adults, emphasizing chronic respiratory disease cohorts and management uncertainty.
- Summarizes epidemiology of adult PID phenotypes where subclass deficits (especially IgG1/IgG3) appear with normal or near-normal total IgG.
- Discusses infection risk, antibody vaccine responses, and when replacement therapy is considered.

# Key data points

- IgG1 is the largest subclass (~60–70% of IgG); low IgG1 often lowers total IgG, but **relative IgG1 deficiency with total IgG still in range** occurs and warrants functional assessment.
- Adult IGGSCD presentations commonly include recurrent sinopulmonary infection; clinical significance hinges on specific antibody responses, not subclass numbers alone.
- IgG2 deficiency classically linked to polysaccharide response failure; IgG1/IgG3 more protein/viral response associations—but mixed phenotypes are common.
- Overlap with SAD is frequent; evaluate pneumococcal/Hib responses before attributing symptoms to a low subclass number.
- Management individualized: vaccination optimization, antibiotics, selective IgG replacement in severe phenotypes.

# Caveats

- Review focused partly on chronic respiratory disease adults—not multi-system non-inflammatory arthralgia phenotypes.
- Subclass reference ranges are assay- and age-dependent; single-lab persistence matters more than one-off values (patient has repeated lows).
- Research catalog only.
