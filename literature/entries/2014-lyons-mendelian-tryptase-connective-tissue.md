# Summary

- Clinical/family description of **autosomal-dominant elevated basal serum tryptase** with multisystem features **before** *TPSAB1* copy-number genotyping was published.
- Linked inherited hypertryptasemia with **atopy** and **connective tissue abnormalities** (including joint hypermobility phenotypes in the original clinical description).
- Established that high tryptase can segregate in families **without** requiring a diagnosis of systemic mastocytosis.

# Key data points

- Dominant inheritance pattern of elevated tryptase across kindreds.
- Clinical clustering: atopic disease features + connective-tissue findings + other mediator-like symptoms in some members.
- Important historical step: elevated BST is not pathognomonic for clonal mast cell disease.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4

**Why:** Matches the concept that inherited hypertryptasemia + connective-tissue/joint features can co-occur without proven SM. Patient has confirmed HαT (downstream of this clinical entity) and noninflammatory joint disease; he lacks classic atopy/MCAS, so phenotype overlap is partial.

# Caveats

- Small familial series; not a population prevalence study.
- Molecular mechanism (*TPSAB1* CNV) not yet identified in this paper.
- Connective-tissue findings are heterogeneous and do not equal EDS or osteoporotic fracture risk.
