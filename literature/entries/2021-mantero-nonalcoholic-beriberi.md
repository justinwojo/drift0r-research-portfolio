# Summary

- Case of a woman with depression and chronic eating disorder who developed non-alcoholic Wernicke encephalopathy and beriberi.
- Sural nerve and muscle biopsy demonstrated severe axonal neuropathy.
- Thiamine supplementation produced rapid improvement of pulmonary and cardiac involvement; peripheral neuropathy recovery was incomplete.
- Mini-review stresses that beriberi remains an important cause of acute flaccid paralysis and is easily misdiagnosed when alcohol history is absent.

# Key data points

- Wernicke may coexist in ~25% of beriberi presentations (authors’ framing).
- Severe axonal neuropathy documented histologically.
- Cardiac/pulmonary features responded quickly to thiamine.
- Neurologic sequelae can persist despite repletion—early treatment critical.
- Non-alcoholic risk via chronic inadequate intake / eating disorder.
