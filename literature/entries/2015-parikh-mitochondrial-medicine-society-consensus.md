# Summary

- Consensus recommendations from the Mitochondrial Medicine Society for physicians evaluating and treating suspected mitochondrial disease.
- Emphasizes multi-organ pattern recognition (neuro, muscle, cardiac, hepatic, renal, endocrine, GI, sensory) rather than any single lab.
- Diagnostic approach historically combined clinical phenotype, blood/CSF biomarkers (lactate, pyruvate, amino acids), imaging, tissue biochemistry, and dual-genome genetics (mtDNA + nuclear genes); field has shifted toward earlier broad NGS.
- Management is largely supportive/organ-specific with selected cofactor therapies; avoid toxins and metabolic stressors; care coordination is essential.

# Key data points

- Delphi method + literature review produced standardized recommendations to reduce practice variation.
- Red flags: multi-system involvement, maternal lineage clues (for mtDNA), stroke-like episodes, refractory epilepsy, progressive external ophthalmoplegia, unexplained lactic acidemia—none required in isolation.
- Negative single-gene or limited panel testing does not exclude disease; tissue heteroplasmy and nuclear gene breadth matter.
- Treatable mimics (B12, copper, thiamine, etc.) must remain in the differential—mitochondrial label is not a wastebasket.

# Caveats

- 2015 consensus—NGS first-line strategies and gene lists have expanded substantially.
- Elevated lactate is neither sensitive nor specific.
- Not medical advice.
