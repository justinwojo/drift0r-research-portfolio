# Summary

- Open-access review of **persistent *B. microti* infection** in humans, spanning asymptomatic immunocompetent carriage (transfusion risk) and relapsing disease in immunocompromised hosts.
- Summarizes diagnostic modalities: smear, nucleic acid tests (PCR/TMA), antigen, antibody.
- Clarifies that persistence is real and clinically important—but measured with conventional parasitologic/NAAT tools, not specialty FISH alone.

# Key data points

- Immunocompetent persons can remain asymptomatically infected for **up to ~2 years**.
- Chronically infected blood donors can transmit via transfusion; TTB has substantial morbidity/mortality (~1/5 severe outcomes historically cited in this literature stream).
- Immunocompromised (asplenia, HIV/AIDS, malignancy, immunosuppressants) may have severe disease and relapse **up to ~2 years** despite therapy.
- Persistence promoted by parasite immune evasion + host immune defects.
- Laboratory detection methods differ in what they prove (DNA/RNA vs antibody memory vs visualized parasites).

# Caveats

- Asymptomatic persistence ≠ chronic multi-system “air hunger/night sweats/joint fragility” syndrome without lab confirmation.
- Does not validate FISH+/PCR− as chronic active infection.
- Transfusion-medicine emphasis differs from community chronic Lyme clinic populations.
