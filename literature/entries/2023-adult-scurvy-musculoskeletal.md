# Summary

- Scurvy is clinical vitamin C deficiency: impaired collagen cross-linking → bleeding, poor wound healing, follicular hyperkeratosis, gingival disease, and prominent **musculoskeletal pain**.
- Adult cases still occur with restricted diets, alcohol use disorder, psychiatric disease, malabsorption, or food insecurity—not only historical sailors.
- MSK features: arthralgia (knees/ankles/wrists), myalgia, hemarthrosis, muscle hematomas, subperiosteal hemorrhage; bone resorption/defective osteoid can lower bone quality.
- Diagnosis is clinical + low plasma vitamin C (or empiric response); levels must be drawn before supplementation when possible.

# Key data points

- Musculoskeletal symptoms appear in a large majority of scurvy series (~80% in classic reviews).
- Imaging may show periosteal elevation from hemorrhage—another periosteal-reaction differential.
- Often coexists with other micronutrient deficiencies.
- Rapid improvement of systemic features after ascorbate is characteristic.

- **Does not match:** Public case notes **normal vitamin C later** (research brief)—active scurvy is then excluded as ongoing driver. No classic perifollicular hemorrhage/gingival bleeding pattern described. Years-long progressive osteoporosis + hypogonadism + HαT + IgG pattern not explained by intermittent scurvy alone. Normal CRP is compatible (scurvy is not a classic CRP-high autoinflammatory disease), but pathognomonic skin/oral findings are absent.
- **Role:** Historical scurvy could have contributed to a past pain flare; it is unlikely the chronic unifier if ascorbate has been normal.

# Caveats

- Plasma vitamin C falls quickly with recent intake—timing matters.
- Do not attribute multi-year steroid-sparing, abx-responsive systemic symptoms to scurvy without levels and exam correlates.
