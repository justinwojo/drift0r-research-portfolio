# Summary

- Formal taxonomic description of ***Babesia duncani* n.sp.** from human isolates WA1 (Washington) and CA5 (California).
- Intraerythrocytic morphology is **indistinguishable from *B. microti*** on routine smears (rings, Maltese cross tetrads possible).
- 18S rRNA phylogeny places *B. duncani* in a distinct clade from *B. microti*.
- Underpins species-specific serology/PCR panels (microti vs duncani) used by specialty and some commercial labs.

# Key data points

- Morphologic forms: round/oval trophozoites; piriform, ring, ameboid; tetrad (Maltese cross) merozoites.
- Ultrastructure similar to *B. microti*/Theileria-like organisms; no Theileria-type intralymphocytic schizonts observed in humans.
- Species differentiation requires molecular (and/or specific serologic) methods—not morphology alone.
- Geographic origin of type isolates: Pacific Northwest / California human cases.

**Why:**
Patient had specialty testing that explicitly included microti/duncani PCR targets (negative) alongside genus FISH (positive). This paper explains why dual-species testing exists and why a genus-level signal could theoretically reflect a non-microti/non-duncani agent—or cross-detection—rather than classic microti disease.

# Caveats

- Does not describe chronic FISH-only syndromes.
- Geographic range of *B. duncani* has been debated and possibly expanding in later literature; 2006 paper is the taxonomic foundation, not latest epi.
- Negative species PCR weakens (does not fully exclude) duncani specifically if assay performance/timing imperfect.
