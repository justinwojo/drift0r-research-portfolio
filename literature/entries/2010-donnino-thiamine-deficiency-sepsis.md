# Summary

- Prospective observational study measuring thiamine levels in critically ill adults with **sepsis**.
- Found a substantial prevalence of **absolute thiamine deficiency** and examined relationships with **lactic acidosis**.
- Supports the concept that severe infection/critical illness can unmask or cause clinically important B1 depletion via increased metabolic demand and inadequate intake/stores.

# Key data points

- Setting: urban tertiary center ED/ICU population with sepsis.
- Absolute TD was common enough to warrant clinical awareness in septic shock pathways.
- Thiamine is a cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase; deficiency promotes lactate generation.
- Laid groundwork for later interventional trials of IV thiamine in septic shock (e.g., Donnino 2016 pilot RCT).

**Why:** Mechanistic support that **infection and systemic stress increase thiamine consumption**, relevant to recurrent B1 problems in a patient with suspected chronic/relapsing infectious or inflammatory burden. ICU sepsis ≠ outpatient chronic MSK disease, but the metabolic principle transfers.

# Caveats

- Critical illness cohort, not ambulatory complex illness.
- Does not prove that Bartonella/Babesia specifically deplete B1 in this patient.
- Assay methods and reference ranges vary (ETKAC vs direct thiamine).
- Not medical advice.
