# Summary

- Prospective clinical study (n=127) examining thiamine and magnesium status in patients presenting with alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS), relating biochemistry to clinical features and 1-year mortality.
- Frames Mg as a critical cofactor for thiamine diphosphate (TDP)–dependent enzymes in intermediary metabolism—**Mg deficiency can impair functional thiamine utilization even when some thiamine is present**.
- Demonstrates real-world variability in circulating thiamine/Mg measures and their imperfect mapping onto outcomes.
- Open-access methods discussion is useful beyond alcohol populations whenever B1 deficiency is treated or suspected.

# Key data points

- Thiamine diphosphate (TDP) and magnesium jointly support pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and transketolase.
- Clinical practice often repletes B1 while ignoring Mg; persistent symptoms after thiamine can reflect ongoing Mg (or other) cofactor deficits.
- **Measurement pitfalls:** serum/plasma Mg represents <1% of total body Mg and can be normal despite intracellular depletion; erythrocyte/RBC Mg and functional enzyme assays (e.g., ETKAC for thiamine) better reflect tissue status but are less widely available and require careful pre-analytics.
- Acute illness and systemic inflammation alter circulating micronutrient concentrations—interpret levels in clinical context (see also Maguire 2021 SIR/TDP/Mg, PMID 34050237).

# Caveats

- Population is alcohol withdrawal—confounding from malnutrition, liver disease, and illness severity.
- Not a randomized trial of combined thiamine+Mg repletion in nonalcoholic beriberi.
- RBC Mg assays are method-sensitive (direct vs calculated; see Schutten et al., PMID 32919943) and not universally standardized.
