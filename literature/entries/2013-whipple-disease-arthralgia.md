# Summary

- Classic *Tropheryma whipplei* infection is rare (~1–3 per million) but demographically biased toward middle-aged White men.
- Nondeforming, often migratory arthralgia/arthritis is frequently the **first** manifestation and may precede GI disease by years (mean prodrome ~6–8 years in many series).
- Full picture later includes diarrhea, malabsorption, weight loss, fever, lymphadenopathy, and sometimes CNS disease; diagnosis hinges on duodenal histology (PAS+ macrophages) ± tissue/fluid PCR.
- Joint phase is often mislabeled seronegative inflammatory arthritis and may worsen under TNF inhibitors or other immunosuppression.

# Key data points

- Arthralgia/arthritis in roughly 2/3–4/5 of classical Whipple cases; large joints preferred; usually non-erosive.
- GI symptoms historically dominate once systemic phase appears; neurologic disease worsens prognosis.
- Inflammatory markers are often elevated in systemic disease, but the early articular phase can be more subtle.
- Confirm with upper endoscopy + PAS histology and *T. whipplei* PCR (duodenum, saliva/stool as adjuncts, CSF if neuro concern).

# Caveats

- Not medical advice; rarity makes pretest probability low without GI/systemic red flags.
- Specialty-lab tick-borne positives do not substitute for Whipple testing.
- Confirm any positive PCR in an appropriate clinical/histologic context (colonization vs invasive disease).
