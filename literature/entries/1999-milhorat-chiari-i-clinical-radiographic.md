# Summary

- Prospective cohort of 364 symptomatic Chiari I malformation (CMI) patients with MRI of head/spine; subset volumetric posterior fossa morphometry and family studies.
- Clinical syndrome clusters: headaches, pseudotumor-like episodes, Ménière-like symptoms, **lower cranial nerve signs**, and spinal cord disturbances even without syrinx.
- Associated findings: syringomyelia 65%, scoliosis 42%, basilar invagination 12%; mean symptom onset age ~25 years; 24% cited trauma as precipitant.
- Imaging hallmarks: obliterated retrocerebellar CSF spaces; tonsillar herniation ≥5 mm in most; reduced posterior fossa volume vs controls.

# Key data points

- 275 female / 89 male; onset 24.9 ± 15.8 years.
- Lower cranial nerve involvement is part of the classic adult CMI phenotype (dysphagia, dysarthria/speech changes, aspiration risk in some series).
- Neck/occipital headache exacerbated by Valsalva is the dominant pain pattern—not generalized polyarthralgia.
- Familial aggregation in 12%; pedigrees suggest AD or AR patterns in some kindreds.
- Posterior fossa volume reduced vs matched controls (Cavalieri method, n=50 pairs).

# Caveats

- Referral neurosurgical cohort—severe/end of spectrum.
- Incidental tonsillar ectopia is common on modern MRI; clinical correlation required.
- Research catalog only.
