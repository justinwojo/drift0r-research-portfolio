# Summary

- AACE/ACE 2020 update on diagnosing, risk-stratifying, evaluating secondary causes, and treating osteoporosis (primarily postmenopausal women; framework widely applied to severe male disease).
- Strong emphasis on **evaluate secondary causes** with a structured laboratory panel when osteoporosis is diagnosed.
- Stratifies very high fracture risk (recent fracture, very low T-score, multiple fractures) and prioritizes **anabolic agents first** in that tier, followed by antiresorptive sequencing.
- Useful anchor for any analyst proposing bone workup completeness, monitoring intervals, and pharmacologic sequencing before elective spine surgery.

# Key data points

- Secondary-cause evaluation (Grade B): CBC, CMP, 25-OH vitamin D, 24-h urine calcium (± creatinine, sodium), PTH as indicated, TSH, celiac serology when suspected, SPEP/UPEP when indicated, testosterone in men, etc.
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria is explicitly among secondary causes linked to bone loss—relevant when 24-h urine Ca is high-normal/high.
- Very high risk → consider abaloparatide, teriparatide, or romosozumab first; then transition to antiresorptive to consolidate gains.
- DXA every 1–2 years until stable; more frequent if clinical change or therapy switch.
- Drug holidays are not appropriate for denosumab (rebound vertebral fracture risk).

# Caveats

- Primary population is postmenopausal women; male pharmacologic evidence is thinner—pair with Watts 2012 men guideline (lit-0216).
- Not a spine-surgery optimization document (pair with Sardar 2022, lit-0227).
- Does not address non-inflammatory polyarthralgia or mast-cell/IgG4 phenotypes.
