# Summary

- Reviews mast cells as active participants in bone remodeling via mediators (histamine, tryptase, cytokines including RANKL-related signals, etc.).
- Covers physiologic roles and pathologic bone disease, especially osteoporosis in systemic mastocytosis (SM).
- Positions mast cells as potential modulators of fracture healing and osteoporotic bone loss in experimental models.
- Provides biological plausibility that mast-cell axis disorders (including tryptase-related traits) could influence BMD.

# Key data points

- Mast-cell–derived factors can stimulate osteoclast activity and influence osteoblasts.
- Clinical anchor: high prevalence of osteopenia/osteoporosis and fractures in SM.
- Preclinical work links mast cells to disturbed bone healing and ovariectomy-related bone loss (expanded in later papers by same group).
- Therapeutic angle in SM bone disease often includes antiresorptives ± mast-cell–directed care.
- Distinguishes clonal mast-cell disease from nonclonal mast-cell activation syndromes.

# Caveats

- Strongest human bone data are in SM, not isolated HαT.
- HαT is common (~5–7% in some populations); most people with HαT do not have markedly low BMD—so HαT alone is unlikely sufficient cause.
- Experimental mouse mast-cell bone findings may not translate 1:1.
- Not medical advice.
