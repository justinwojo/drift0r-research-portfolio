# Summary

- Evaluated whether quantitative sudomotor axon reflex testing (QSART) improves SFN diagnosis beyond QST and skin-biopsy IENFD.
- 101 patients with clinically suspected SFN underwent QSART, QST, and IENFD.
- Adding QSART to existing somatic criteria increased diagnostic yield from 38% to 66%; a combined rule requiring ≥2 abnormal among QSART/QST/IENFD diagnosed 56%.
- Supports assessing **both somatic and postganglionic sympathetic cholinergic (sudomotor) fibers**.

# Key data points

- Current (somatic) criteria diagnosed SFN in 38/101 (38%).
- Adding QSART raised diagnosis to 67/101 (66%).
- ≥2 abnormal among QSART, QST, IENFD → 57/101 (56%).
- Conclusion: dual somatic + autonomic small-fiber assessment strengthens diagnostic criteria.

# Caveats

- Single-center diagnostic series; not a population screening study.
- QSART requires specialized lab conditions; false positives/negatives with anticholinergics, dry skin, technical factors.
- Companion paper (same group, 2014) found autonomic scores poorly correlated with IENFD/QST—autonomic and somatic arms can diverge.
