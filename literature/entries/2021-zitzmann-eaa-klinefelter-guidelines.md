# Summary

- Contemporary European andrology guideline for diagnosis and lifelong management of Klinefelter syndrome across pediatric and adult care.
- Emphasizes that diagnostic rate remains low relative to true prevalence; structured indication lists for when to order karyotype.
- Management spans testosterone replacement, bone and metabolic health, neurocognitive/psychosocial support, and fertility preservation/assisted reproduction pathways.
- Positions KS as a multi-system condition, not solely an infertility diagnosis.

# Key data points

- **Karyotype indicated (core adult scenarios from guideline framing):** men with nonobstructive azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia; **or** primary hypogonadism (low T + elevated LH/FSH) with small testicular volume (often cited <5 mL per testis); pediatric cryptorchidism pathways also specified.
- Standard peripheral blood karyotype is first-line; FISH or tissue studies may detect low-level mosaicism when clinical suspicion remains high after normal karyotype.
- TRT when symptomatic hypogonadism and biochemically confirmed low T; monitor hematocrit, PSA (age-appropriate), BMD, and metabolic parameters.
- Bone disease is common; DXA and vitamin D/lifestyle measures are part of standard care—T alone does not always normalize BMD.

# Caveats

- Guideline, not an RCT; recommendations graded from mixed evidence quality.
- Does not address copper deficiency, thiamine deficiency, or mast-cell genetics—those remain separate workstreams.
- US Endocrine Society hypogonadism guideline (lit-0215) is complementary for general T diagnosis/treatment framing.
