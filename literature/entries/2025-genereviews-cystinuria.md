# Summary

- Cystinuria causes early-onset recurrent **cystine** kidney stones from defective proximal tubular reabsorption of cystine (and dibasic amino acids) via *SLC3A1* (rBAT) and/or *SLC7A9* (b0,+AT).
- Diagnosis: cystine stones and/or elevated 24-h urinary cystine ± pathogenic variants (biallelic, digenic, or heterozygous in suggestive context).
- Treatment pillars: high fluid intake, low-sodium (± moderated methionine/protein) diet, urinary alkalinization (potassium citrate/bicarbonate), thiol drugs (tiopronin, D-penicillamine) when needed, stone procedures as indicated.
- Complications: recurrent UTI, CKD, hypertension; some progress to ESKD.

# Key data points

- **Type B (*SLC7A9*)** often shows incomplete penetrance in heterozygotes; many *SLC7A9* heterozygotes have elevated dibasic aminoaciduria and lifetime stone risk.
- Surveillance includes serial 24-h urine chemistry (cystine, volume, pH, sodium, calcium, citrate), imaging, BP, and kidney function.
- Mixed stone chemistry can coexist; cystinuria does not exclude concurrent idiopathic hypercalciuria or other metabolic stone risk.
- GeneReviews notes bone-health surveillance considerations in adults with additional risk factors (DXA/bone markers as indicated)—not that cystinuria alone equals primary osteoporosis.

# Caveats

- Heterozygous *SLC7A9* ≠ automatic classic recessive cystinuria phenotype; phenotype–genotype correlation needs urine cystine quantification.
- Stone composition analysis remains decisive when available.
