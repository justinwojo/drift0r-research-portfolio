# Summary

- Review of thiazide and thiazide-like diuretics for hypercalciuric stone formers, covering stone-recurrence RCTs, urine chemistry effects, adverse effects, and bone outcomes.
- Notes historical stone-prevention RCTs used relatively high thiazide doses; modern lower doses are common but less rigorously proven.
- Reports institutional experience: HCTZ 25 mg/day or indapamide 2.5 mg/day reduced urinary calcium over ~3 months with acceptable short-term tolerability; mixed BMD signals at 1 year in a small DXA subsample.
- Argues thiazides may protect bone both by reducing calciuria and via direct effects on osteoblast activity.

# Key data points

- Classic stone-prevention RCTs generally used high-dose thiazides (often 50–100 mg HCTZ-equivalent range) and reduced recurrence in most longer trials.
- Authors’ series: significant reduction in urine calcium after ~3 months on HCTZ 25 mg or indapamide 2.5 mg.
- Small DXA subset (n≈28, mean age ~35): HCTZ group showed significant improvement at femoral neck T-score over ~1 year; spine change not significant; wide individual variation.
- Mechanistic notes: anticalciuric effect plus in-vitro osteoblast stimulation / NCC expression and possible osteoclast inhibition.
- Emphasizes lack of large modern RCTs comparing agents, doses, and long-term bone endpoints in hypercalciuric patients.

# Caveats

- Much of the bone data are observational / small / uncontrolled.
- Written before full impact of NOSTONE (2023) was integrated; must be read alongside null modern HCTZ stone RCT.
- Not a substitute for anabolic therapy when T-scores are marked.
