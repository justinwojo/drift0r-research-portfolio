# Summary

- Hypogonadism is a major driver of male osteoporosis; early-onset T deficiency in KS is a particular risk for precocious bone loss.
- Osteoporosis is reported in up to ≈40% of men with KS and has classically been attributed to low testosterone.
- Importantly, reduced bone mass can occur even when T is normal, and testosterone replacement does **not always** restore BMD—pointing to additional KS-specific mechanisms.
- Proposed non-T determinants include androgen receptor (CAG length/inactivation patterns) and low insulin-like factor 3 (INSL3), which has anabolic effects on osteoblasts.

# Key data points

- T acts on bone both via aromatization to estradiol and via osteoblast androgen receptors; puberty-onset deficiency impairs peak bone mass accrual.
- Prevalence figures: substantial osteopenia/osteoporosis burden in KS cohorts (often cited combined rates approaching ~40%).
- Clinical implication: DXA monitoring is warranted in KS regardless of current T level on or off TRT.
- Research implication: treating only the serum T number may be incomplete for skeletal outcomes in KS.

# Caveats

- Mechanistic discussion of INSL3/AR is partly speculative relative to clinical trial evidence.
- Does not compare KS bone disease against other secondary osteoporosis causes present in this case (hypercalciuria, etc.).
