# Summary

- Mechanistic and clinical review of IgG4 beyond IgG4-RD: roles in immune tolerance, chronic antigen exposure, allergic disease, helminth infection, and selected autoantibody-mediated diseases (pemphigus, bullous pemphigoid, membranous nephropathy, etc.).
- Explains why serum IgG4 rises in non-RD contexts—especially Th2/IL-10–skewed chronic stimulation.
- Useful bridge between modest serologic elevation and infection/allergy/parasite/autoimmune differentials.

# Key data points

- IgG4 is the least abundant IgG subclass; often anti-inflammatory / “blocking,” with Fab-arm exchange and poor complement/FcγR activation.
- Chronic antigen exposure (allergens, helminths, immunotherapy) drives large IgG4 increases with IL-10–associated tolerance programs.
- Parasitic infection classically elevates IgG4 (and often IgE); strong IgE responses associate with resistance phenotypes in some helminth models.
- IgG4 can be pathogenic as an autoantibody class in specific diseases unrelated to fibrotic IgG4-RD.
- Elevation in chronic inflammatory or asymptomatic infection states is expected biology—not pathognomonic for IgG4-RD.

- Patient lacks classic atopy/MCAS mediator phenotype; HαT explains tryptase better than allergy-driven IgG4 alone.
