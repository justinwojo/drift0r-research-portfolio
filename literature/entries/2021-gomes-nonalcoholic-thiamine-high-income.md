# Summary

- Literature review of confirmed adult thiamine deficiency unrelated to alcohol in high-income countries (1999–2020).
- 17 reports comprising 81 adult cases (ages 19–75; 69% male), mostly from the United States plus Japan, Europe, Korea, Saudi Arabia.
- Causes: disease-related malnutrition, bariatric surgery, chronic diuretics, vomiting, thiamine-free parenteral nutrition, food insecurity, monotonous/restrictive diets; 6% without major background disease but dietary restriction/insecurity.
- IV thiamine yielded partial or complete recovery of cardiac, neurologic, and metabolic symptoms for most; three deaths reported.
- Key message: in high-income settings, thiamine deficiency is not exclusive to alcohol use disorder.

# Key data points

- 94% had an underlying condition (cancer, GI disease, HF, recent surgery/weight loss, etc.).
- BMI ranged from underweight to obese when reported.
- Blood thiamine low in most tested patients vs lab reference ranges.
- Presentations: weakness, peripheral neuropathy, confusion, edema, tachycardia, dyspnea, metabolic acidosis.
- Initial IV doses in reports ranged widely (~75 mg daily to 500 mg TID).

**Why:** Directly supports the central claim that severe, symptomatic, treatable thiamine deficiency occurs without alcoholism—matching Drift0r’s no-alcohol history. # Caveats

- Heterogeneous case literature; publication bias toward dramatic presentations.
- Few cases truly “healthy diet + no comorbidity” like Drift0r’s self-report.
- Treatment mostly IV thiamine, not benfotiamine 1000 mg oral.
