# Summary

- Clinical educational review of thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency spanning wet beriberi, dry beriberi, and Wernicke–Korsakoff spectrum.
- Dry beriberi: symmetrical peripheral neuropathy with sensory, motor, and reflex deficits, often distal lower-limb predominant; pain and paresthesias common.
- Risk factors extend beyond alcohol: malnutrition, bariatric surgery, TPN without vitamins, hyperemesis, diuretics, hypermetabolic states, refeeding, pregnancy/lactation.
- Stresses empiric parenteral thiamine in at-risk patients and thiamine before/with glucose or nutrition support in refeeding/alcohol-withdrawal prophylaxis.

# Key data points

- Body stores deplete over weeks of inadequate intake; clinical disease follows.
- Dry vs wet vs cerebral (Wernicke) forms may overlap in one patient.
- Neurologic findings include distal sensory loss, weakness, hyporeflexia; severe cases mimic GBS.
- Treatment is thiamine repletion; do not delay for lab confirmation in emergencies.
- Refeeding and high carbohydrate loads increase thiamine demand.
