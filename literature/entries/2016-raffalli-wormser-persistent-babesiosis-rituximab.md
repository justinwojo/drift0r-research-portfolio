# Summary

- Case report: patient on **rituximab** for rheumatoid arthritis developed *B. microti* infection that **persisted 26 months** despite prolonged anti-babesial drug therapy.
- Long-term B-cell impairment from rituximab is the proposed mechanism.
- Patient remained **seronegative for *B. microti* antibodies for >1 year** after onset—demonstrating that antibody tests can fail when humoral immunity is blocked.
- Reinforces that multi-year microbiologically documented persistence is a recognized entity—in **profoundly immunocompromised** hosts.

# Key data points

- Duration of persistence: **>2 years (26 months)**.
- Therapy: multiple/prolonged anti-babesia regimens still failed to eradicate promptly.
- Serology unreliable under rituximab; diagnosis depended on direct detection (smear/PCR context).
- Contrasts with typical immunocompetent recovery after standard courses.

Low phenotypic match (patient not on rituximab; not known B-cell depleted in this way) but high conceptual value: shows what “real” prolonged babesiosis looks like in the literature—ongoing infection with immune failure and direct pathogen detection—not FISH+/PCR− specialty reports alone. Also warns that **negative serology is not always reassuring** when immune response is impaired (less applicable if humoral immunity intact).

# Caveats

- Single case.
- Cannot be generalized to immunocompetent chronic symptom syndromes.
- Does not evaluate FISH assays.
