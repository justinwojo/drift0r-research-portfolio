# Summary

- Comprehensive review integrating genetics, endocrinology, and neuropsychology of KS—framed around persistent diagnostic failure decades after the syndrome’s description.
- As few as ≈25% of individuals with KS are ever correctly diagnosed; most diagnoses occur in adulthood (infertility, hypogonadism workups).
- Classic features (small testes, hypergonadotropic hypogonadism, infertility) coexist with highly variable cognitive, psychiatric, metabolic, and skeletal manifestations.
- Argues for broader clinician vigilance and multi-disciplinary lifelong care once diagnosed.

# Key data points

- Underdiagnosis estimate (~25% lifetime diagnosis rate) is a repeatedly cited epidemiologic anchor.
- Genotype–phenotype: non-mosaic 47,XXY vs mosaic and higher-grade sex chromosome aneuploidies alter severity; parental origin of extra X and skewed X-inactivation are research-level modifiers.
- Endocrine: progressive Leydig/Sertoli cell dysfunction after puberty; T may be low-normal early and decline later.
- Neuropsychology: language-based learning difficulties, social cognition differences, and increased psychiatric comorbidity risk—not uniform intellectual disability.

# Caveats

- Narrative synthesis with heterogeneous source studies; underdiagnosis percentages are estimates from register/epidemiologic work, not a single gold-standard census.
- Full text typically institutional/paywalled.
