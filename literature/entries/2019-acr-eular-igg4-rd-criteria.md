# Summary

- International ACR/EULAR **classification** criteria (not purely diagnostic) for IgG4-related disease, developed and validated across multi-organ cohorts.
- Three-step structure: (1) **entry** — characteristic clinical/radiologic involvement of a typical organ **or** pathologic lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate of uncertain cause in a typical organ; (2) **exclusion** criteria that remove mimics; (3) **inclusion** weighted score ≥20 points from domains (immunostaining, serum IgG4, bilateral lacrimal/salivary, chest, pancreas/biliary, kidney, retroperitoneum).
- Explicitly prevents classifying isolated serum IgG4 elevation without organ phenotype as IgG4-RD.
- Anchor for this case’s “high IgG4, no fibrosis” phenotype: criteria explain why classic IgG4-RD is not currently supported.

# Key data points

- Entry fails if there is no typical organ involvement pattern—serum IgG4 alone is insufficient.
- Exclusion examples include malignancy, infection, Sjögren, ANCA vasculitis, etc., when they better explain the picture.
- Serum IgG4 contributes points in a graded fashion but is neither necessary nor sufficient.
- Pathology (IgG4+ plasma cells, IgG4/IgG ratio, storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis) carries high weight when available.
- Validated sensitivity/specificity were high in derivation/validation cohorts for classification use in research and specialty care.

# Caveats

- Classification criteria ≠ clinical diagnosis in every edge case; some true IgG4-RD may score <20 early.
- Japanese RCD criteria (lit-0223) use a different structure (clinical/radiologic + serology + pathology tiers).
- Does not explain low IgG1, polyarthralgia, or bone disease.
