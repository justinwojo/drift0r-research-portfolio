# Summary

- Endocrine Society clinical practice guideline on who has true hypogonadism, how to confirm low testosterone, and when/how to treat with testosterone.
- Diagnosis requires consistent symptoms/signs **plus** unequivocally and repeatedly low morning total testosterone (or free T when SHBG is altered); screening of unselected populations is discouraged.
- Separates classic organic hypogonadism (primary testicular or secondary pituitary/hypothalamic disease) from age-related or comorbidity-associated declines; treatment thresholds and goals differ.
- Directly relevant to a young/mid-adult male with documented very low T, multi-system illness, and bone fragility—workup must include LH/FSH, prolactin, iron studies, and consideration of secondary causes before lifelong TRT.

# Key data points

- Confirm low T with **≥2 morning fasting total T** measurements; use reliable assay; free T when obesity, aging, or SHBG abnormality confounds total T.
- Organic hypogonadism (pituitary mass, hemochromatosis, Klinefelter, etc.) warrants therapy and disease-specific evaluation; functional hypogonadism may improve if comorbidities reverse.
- TRT aims for mid-normal range total T; monitor hematocrit, PSA (age-appropriate), symptoms, and adherence.
- Men desiring near-term fertility should **not** start exogenous T (suppresses spermatogenesis); SERMs (e.g., clomiphene), hCG, or aromatase inhibitors may be used off-label under specialist care.
- Contraindications include prostate/breast cancer (active), high hematocrit, uncontrolled HF, recent MI/stroke, untreated severe OSA (relative).

# Caveats

- Not medical advice; guideline for clinicians, not self-treatment.
- 2018 document predates some longer-term CV outcome data and newer formulations; still the standard US reference.
- Does not explain multi-system non-endocrine features (polyarthralgia, IgG4, HαT, specialty-lab pathogens).
- “Idiopathic” hypogonadism remains a diagnosis of exclusion after full workup.
