# Summary

- Early illustrative case report of **zinc-induced copper deficiency** producing both pancytopenia and myeloneuropathy.
- Excessive zinc upregulates intestinal metallothionein, which preferentially binds copper and traps it in enterocytes, leading to systemic hypocupremia.
- Stopping zinc and repleting copper can reverse hematologic failure; neurologic recovery is variable and often incomplete if delayed.
- Establishes a concrete, iatrogenic pathway that clinicians should ask about (supplements, “immune” zinc, cold-remedy stacks).

# Key data points

- Presentation pairs progressive gait/sensory ataxia with cytopenias—check copper **and** zinc together.
- History of high-dose zinc supplementation is the causal clue; hyperzincemia supports the mechanism.
- Distinct from dietary copper scarcity alone; removing the zinc source is mandatory therapy.
- Open-access PMC full text facilitates direct review of the clinical narrative.
