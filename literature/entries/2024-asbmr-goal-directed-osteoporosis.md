# Summary

- ASBMR / Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation task force position on **goal-directed** long-term osteoporosis management aimed at fracture prevention.
- Moves beyond “treat for 3–5 years then holiday” toward individualized targets based on fracture history (site, number, recency), BMD at hip/spine, and other major risk factors.
- After secondary-cause evaluation, implement falls prevention and adherence support; choose initial therapy intensity to match risk (including anabolic-first pathways for very high risk).
- Provides the 2024 North American bone-field consensus language analysts should use when proposing monitoring targets and therapy switches for markedly low BMD.

# Key data points

- Goal = prevent fractures; treatment targets tailored to baseline risk profile.
- Assess clinical fracture history, vertebral imaging when appropriate, DXA at total hip/femoral neck/lumbar spine, and major risk factors.
- Secondary causes of osteoporosis should be evaluated as part of goal-directed care before attributing non-response to drug failure alone.
- Higher-risk patients may need more potent initial therapy (including anabolics) and earlier reassessment.
- Evidence base is primarily older postmenopausal women of European ancestry—extrapolate carefully to young men with secondary disease.

# Caveats

- Population evidence ≠ young male multi-system secondary osteoporosis trials.
- Does not replace disease-specific therapy for hypogonadism, hypercalciuria, or malabsorption.
- Pair with Watts 2012 (men), Sardar 2022 (spine pre-op), and ISCD young-adult reporting rules.
