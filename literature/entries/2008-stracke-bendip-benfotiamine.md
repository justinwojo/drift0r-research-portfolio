# Summary

- Double-blind, placebo-controlled phase-III RCT of benfotiamine for symmetrical distal diabetic polyneuropathy (BENDIP).
- 165 patients randomized after screening; analyzed ITT/PP groups for benfotiamine 600 mg/day, 300 mg/day, or placebo over 6 weeks.
- Primary focus on neuropathy symptom scores; higher dose showed greater symptomatic benefit.
- Authors conclude benfotiamine may extend treatment options via effects on impaired glucose metabolism pathways.

# Key data points

- Doses studied: 300 mg/day and 600 mg/day oral benfotiamine vs placebo.
- Duration: 6 weeks (short-term symptomatic endpoint).
- Neuropathy Symptom Score improved more with active treatment, especially higher dose.
- Population: diabetic polyneuropathy—not classic dry beriberi from absolute B1 deficiency.
- Benfotiamine is a lipid-soluble thiamine prodrug with higher bioavailability than water-soluble thiamine salts.

# Caveats

- Not thiamine-deficiency beriberi; mechanistic target is diabetic metabolic neuropathy.
- Symptom scores, not definitive nerve regeneration endpoints.
- Abstract-level access for full numeric tables in some environments; longer RCTs (e.g., Fraser 2012) show more mixed structural outcomes.
