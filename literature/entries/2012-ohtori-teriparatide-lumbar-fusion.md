# Summary

- Prospective clinical study of daily subcutaneous **teriparatide** vs bisphosphonate around instrumented **lumbar posterolateral fusion** in osteoporotic postmenopausal women.
- Found higher and **faster radiographic bone union** with teriparatide than bisphosphonate.
- Foundational clinical evidence that an anabolic osteoporosis drug can favorably modify **fusion biology**, not only systemic BMD.
- Underpins later guideline statements supporting preoperative/perioperative teriparatide for osteoporotic spine instrumentation.

# Key data points

- Intervention: teriparatide 20 µg SC daily (typically started preop and continued postop in this program) vs oral bisphosphonate comparator.
- Bone union rate: **82%** teriparatide vs **68%** bisphosphonate (study-reported).
- Average time to union: **~8 months** teriparatide vs longer (~10 months class) with bisphosphonate.
- Population: postmenopausal women with osteoporosis undergoing 1–2 level PLF with local bone graft.
- Supports “treat bone first / treat bone through fusion” rather than operating on untreated osteoporosis when elective.

# Caveats

- Small, single-program prospective series; not blinded RCT.
- Postmenopausal female population only.
- Local bone graft PLF—may not generalize to multi-level interbody constructs with modern biologics.
- Hypercalciuria/nephrolithiasis risk of teriparatide needs separate assessment in this patient.
- Not medical advice.
