# Summary

- Defines polyarthritis as arthritis in ≥5 joints and stresses that polyarticular *pain* is broader than true polyarthritis.
- Emphasizes difficulty distinguishing true polyarthritis from polyarticular joint pain causes such as fibromyalgia or osteoarthritis.
- Integrates history, physical exam, laboratory findings, and imaging as the diagnostic toolkit.
- Reviews major inflammatory, infectious, crystal, and noninflammatory categories that produce multi-joint presentations.

# Key data points

- Polyarthritis ≠ polyarthralgia: swelling/tenderness/synovitis define arthritis; pain alone may be non-arthritic.
- Fibromyalgia and osteoarthritis are highlighted as common polyarticular pain mimics without true multi-joint synovitis.
- Acute vs chronic and additive vs migratory patterns remain key chronology features.
- Laboratory and imaging findings are supportive tools, not substitutes for clinical classification of inflammation.

# Caveats

- General rheumatology overview; not disease-specific for thiamine deficiency, HαT, or specialty-lab tick-borne findings.
- Open-access but still secondary literature (review, not primary cohort of similar multi-system cases).
- Does not fully cover neuropathic joint pain or small-fiber neuropathy as arthralgia mimics.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
