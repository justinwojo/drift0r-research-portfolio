# Summary

- Large RCT fracture analysis within TRAVERSE: middle-aged/older hypogonadal men (T <300 ng/dL ×2) randomized to T gel vs placebo, median ~3.2 years.
- **Clinical fracture incidence was not lower with T; it was numerically higher** (3.50% vs 2.46%; HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.04–1.97).
- Challenges the simple clinical assumption that because T improves BMD metrics, it necessarily reduces fractures in treated older men with CV risk.
- Critical counterweight to 2017 TTrials bone imaging benefits.

# Key data points

- Full analysis n = 5204 (2601 T, 2603 placebo).
- Age 45–80; preexisting or high CV risk; hypogonadal symptoms + T <300 ng/dL ×2.
- Clinical fracture: 91 (T) vs 64 (placebo).
- Similar directional signal across other fracture endpoints.
- Does not negate BMD gains in other trials; fracture biology includes falls, activity, bone quality, and competing risks.

**Why:**
- Highly relevant caution for a patient with markedly low BMD who is on/transitioning to TRT: BMD improvement ≠ automatic fracture protection.
- Population (older, CV-risk, milder T deficit) differs substantially from young severe idiopathic HG + multi-hit bone disease.
- Still essential literature for counseling and monitoring expectations.

# Caveats

- Event rates low; fractures partly fall-related; activity levels may rise on T.
- Not a dedicated osteoporosis population with very low T-scores like this case.
- Gel formulation; dose/monitoring differ from injectable regimens (e.g., Xyosted).
