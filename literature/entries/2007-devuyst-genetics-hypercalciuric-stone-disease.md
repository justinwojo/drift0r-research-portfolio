# Summary

- Review of genetic architecture of **hypercalciuric nephrolithiasis**, from common idiopathic hypercalciuria to rare monogenic tubular disorders.
- Summarizes heritability data (family and twin studies) showing substantial genetic contribution to urinary calcium excretion and stone risk.
- Catalogs monogenic hypercalciuric syndromes (e.g., Dent disease, familial hypomagnesemia with hypercalciuria, AD hypocalcemia, hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria) that must be distinguished from garden-variety IH.
- Positions common IH as polygenic/complex with candidate loci (including VDR-region associations) rather than a single-gene diagnosis in most patients.

# Key data points

- Lifetime stone risk high; ~80% calcium stones; hypercalciuria in a large fraction of formers.
- Twin heritability for urinary calcium often cited ~50%.
- Family history is among the strongest clinical predictors of stones after diet.
- Rare monogenic diseases teach pathophysiology of renal calcium handling relevant to milder IH.

# Caveats

- 2007; newer GWAS of stones/hypercalciuria exist but core heritability message stands.
- Does not prescribe thiazide vs diet vs potassium citrate for this case—pair with stone-metabolic entries.
- Not a BMD outcomes trial.
