# Summary

- Landmark NEJM review of human babesiosis by Vannier & Krause covering epidemiology, clinical features, diagnosis, and therapy.
- *B. microti* dominates U.S. disease; *B. duncani* and other agents are less common but clinically important where present.
- Clinical spectrum ranges from asymptomatic infection to severe hemolytic, multiorgan disease—especially in asplenic, elderly, or immunocompromised hosts.
- Diagnosis relies on blood smear microscopy, PCR, and serology used appropriately by phase of illness.
- Standard therapy: atovaquone + azithromycin (or clindamycin + quinine).

# Key data points

- Incubation typically days to weeks after tick bite; transfusion-associated cases can present later.
- Common symptoms: fever, fatigue, chills, sweats, headache, myalgia; hemolytic anemia and thrombocytopenia when parasitemia is appreciable.
- Many infections in immunocompetent hosts are mild or subclinical; severe disease concentrates in risk groups (asplenia, advanced age, HIV, malignancy, immunosuppressants).
- Smear sensitivity falls at low parasitemia—PCR is more sensitive for low-level infection.
- Antibodies rise after infection and can persist; serology alone does not prove active disease.
