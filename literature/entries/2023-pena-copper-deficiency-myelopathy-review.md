# Summary

- Acquired copper deficiency causes a treatable **myeloneuropathy** mimicking B12-deficiency subacute combined degeneration: sensory ataxia, spasticity, dorsal-column signs ± axonal neuropathy.
- Hematologic clues (anemia, leukopenia/neutropenia, vacuolated marrow precursors) are helpful but **not required** for neurologic disease.
- Common drivers: prior bariatric/gastric surgery, malabsorption, zinc excess (supplements, some denture adhesives), inadequate parenteral nutrition copper, rarely excessive zinc from other sources.
- Serum copper ± ceruloplasmin and zinc co-testing; copper repletion can stabilize/partially reverse deficits—delayed diagnosis risks permanent cord injury.

# Key data points

- MRI may show dorsal-column T2 hyperintensity; imaging can be normal early.
- Distinguish from Wilson disease (copper overload)—opposite problem, different tests.
- Zinc excess induces intestinal metallothionein that traps copper; stop zinc source while repleting copper.
- 2020+ literature continues to emphasize under-recognition in patients labeled “idiopathic” myeloneuropathy or “B12 nonresponders.”

**Why:**
- Neuropathy/nutrient-deficiency context and B12-mimic physiology keep copper on the workup list (especially with zinc supplementation history).
- Not a high-probability unifier for markedly low BMD, HαT, or stone genetics unless low copper is documented.
- Research action: ensure copper/zinc were measured (and not only once mid-supplementation).

# Caveats

- Case series + review, not an RCT.
- Over-supplementation of copper has risks; repletion should be clinician-directed.
- Not medical advice.
