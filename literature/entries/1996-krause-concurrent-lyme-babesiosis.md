# Summary

- Community serosurvey + clinic cohort (Rhode Island island community; Connecticut clinics, 1990–1994) examining concurrent Lyme disease and babesiosis.
- Coinfection associated with **more symptoms and longer illness** than Lyme disease alone.
- Classic evidence that *Babesia* coinfection worsens and prolongs clinical illness in endemic *Ixodes* regions.

# Key data points

- Serosurvey: 97/1156 (8.4%) Lyme-seroreactive; 14/97 (14%) also babesia-seroreactive.
- Clinic Lyme diagnoses: 26/240 (11%) coinfected with babesiosis.
- Coinfection more often had fatigue, headache, **sweats**, chills, anorexia, emotional lability, nausea, conjunctivitis, splenomegaly vs Lyme alone.
- Illness duration longer in coinfected patients.
- Approximately half of coinfected patients were still symptomatic at 3 months vs fewer with Lyme alone (per study reporting).

# Caveats

- 1990s serologic definitions differ from modern two-tier Lyme algorithms.
- Demonstrates severity of **documented** dual infection—not specialty FISH without PCR confirmation.
- Does not address Bartonella coinfection (patient’s other contested signal).
