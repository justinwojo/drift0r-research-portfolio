# Summary

- Official IDSA 2020 guideline for diagnosis and management of human babesiosis (published 2021 in *CID*).
- Diagnosis of acute disease: **peripheral blood smear examination or PCR** preferred over antibody testing for confirmation.
- The guideline does **not** discuss FISH LDTs; applying the smear/PCR standard as a foil for FISH-only specialty patterns is a **project inference**, not a guideline sentence.
- Treatment: **atovaquone + azithromycin** preferred; **clindamycin + quinine** alternative; longer courses/monitoring for severe or immunocompromised disease.
- Coinfection with Lyme addressed in a separate IDSA/AAN/ACR guideline.

# Key data points

- Target audience: primary care, ID, EM, intensivists, hematology, transfusion medicine.
- Antibody testing supports epidemiologic/serologic context but is **not** the confirmatory test for acute infection.
- Mild–moderate outpatient disease typically treated 7–10 days with atovaquone + azithromycin.
- Highly immunocompromised patients may need prolonged therapy and close follow-up for relapse.
- Exchange transfusion reserved for high-parasitemia/severe organ compromise cases (per guideline framework).

# Caveats

- Guideline does not validate commercial LDT FISH as a standalone confirmatory assay.
- Written for acute/subacute and known immunocompromised relapse patterns—not for FISH+/PCR− chronic multisystem illness without documented parasitemia.
- Lyme coinfection diagnostic nuance deferred to separate document.
- Published corrigendum exists (Clin Infect Dis 2021 doi 10.1093/cid/ciab275 / PMID 33960362) — check publisher record.
