# Summary

- After the 2017 EDS nosology overhaul (>20 EDS types), this paper organizes terminology for joint hypermobility and related conditions.
- Distinguishes asymptomatic joint hypermobility, secondary musculoskeletal manifestations, syndromic hypermobility, and a proposed group of **hypermobility spectrum disorders (HSD)**.
- HSD is the diagnostic space for patients with *symptomatic* joint hypermobility who do **not** meet criteria for EDS or other defined syndromes.
- Introduces a spectrum concept linking pathogenetically related hypermobility manifestations with and without full syndromic EDS.

# Key data points

- Joint hypermobility is a clinical feature, not a diagnosis by itself.
- Secondary MSK manifestations of hypermobility include pain, instability, soft-tissue injury, and reduced proprioception/control.
- HSD proposed when symptomatic hypermobility is present but criteria for hEDS/other EDS types are not met.
- Classification aims to reduce false lumping of all flexible/painful patients into “EDS” or “fibromyalgia.”

# Caveats

- Abstract-level access in this corpus; full diagnostic tables in paywalled Wiley text.
- HSD is a clinical construct—heterogeneous and not a single etiology.
- Does not explain markedly low BMD for age or documented beriberi.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
