# Summary

- Review integrating ecology, epidemiology, and clinical consequences of **Ixodes-transmitted coinfections**, focusing on *Borrelia burgdorferi* + *Babesia microti*.
- Coinfection is common in the enzootic cycle and contributes to emergence of babesiosis in Lyme-endemic regions.
- In humans, dual infection increases disease severity and duration (building on Krause 1996 and related work).

# Key data points

- *Ixodes* ticks maintain diverse human pathogens; dual carriage is not rare.
- Despite relatively poor ecological fitness of *B. microti* alone, coinfection dynamics help explain geographic emergence with Lyme disease.
- Human coinfection data: greater symptom burden and longer illness.
- Framework generalizable to other vector-borne pathogen pairs.
- Prevention/therapy design should account for co-transmission risk.
