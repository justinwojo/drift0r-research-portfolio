# Summary

- Cohort study examining whether thiazide dose associates with magnitude of urine calcium reduction and whether those reductions associate with fewer symptomatic stone events.
- Finds a dose–response: higher thiazide doses produce larger drops in urine calcium, and greater calcium reductions correlate with fewer symptomatic stone events.
- Offers a mechanistic explanation for why NOSTONE’s modest once-daily HCTZ regimens may have failed: insufficient calciuria reduction relative to historical higher-intensity practice.
- Practical implication: if thiazide is used for hypercalciuria, titrate to urine calcium response (and tolerability), not to a fixed low “blood-pressure-like” dose alone.

# Key data points

- N≈634 participants with thiazide exposure and urine chemistry follow-up (per published report).
- Higher thiazide dosage associated with greater reductions in urine calcium than the modest ~40–50 mg/day drops discussed for NOSTONE HCTZ arms.
- Greater urine calcium reduction negatively correlated with symptomatic stone events.
- Authors suggest monitoring 24h urine calcium on therapy to adjust dose and diet toward an adequate calciuric response.
- Open-access JAMA Network Open publication (CC-BY).

# Caveats

- Observational; residual confounding (adherence, diet Na, selection of higher doses for worse calciuria).
- Stone-event endpoint, not BMD or fracture.
- Does not define the optimal agent (HCTZ vs chlorthalidone/indapamide) for bone-focused care.
