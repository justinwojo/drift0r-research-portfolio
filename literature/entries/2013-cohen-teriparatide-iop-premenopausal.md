# Summary

- Open-label pilot of teriparatide 20 µg daily for 18–24 months in 21 premenopausal women with idiopathic osteoporosis (unexplained fragility fractures or low BMD).
- Primary endpoint: within-subject lumbar spine BMD change.
- Spine BMD +10.8%, total hip +6.2%, femoral neck +7.6% (all p<0.001).
- Serum P1NP doubled by 1 month, peaked ~6 months, returned toward baseline by 18–24 months; CTX also rose (coupled turnover).
- Iliac crest biopsies: increased trabecular bone volume/number, cortical width, and ~71% increase in trabecular stiffness.
- Four nonresponders (19%) had blunted/delayed P1NP rise and markedly lower baseline bone formation rate on histomorphometry.
- Nonresponders had higher baseline IGF-1; suggests osteoblast dysfunction subset within IOP.
- Best available young-adult idiopathic osteoporosis teriparatide experience (sex differs; age and idiopathic framing highly relevant).

# Key data points

- N=21; ages in premenopausal range (younger adult).
- Spine +10.8% over 18–24 months.
- Coupled rise in P1NP and CTX during therapy.
- ~19% nonresponse rate linked to low baseline bone formation.
- Microarchitecture and stiffness improved on biopsy.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Young adult with severe idiopathic-range osteoporosis is the closest age-matched anabolic IOP experience.
- Documents CTX as a responsive turnover marker during teriparatide—relevant to monitoring high-normal CTX 616.
- Nonresponder biology warns that not all severe IOP responds equally; baseline formation status matters.
- Sex is female—extrapolation to men requires Orwoll/Kurland male data (lit-0007/0008).

# Caveats

- Pilot, open-label, no placebo; women only.
- BMD declines after discontinuation reported in follow-up work—may need sequential antiresorptive.
- Nonresponse subset limits universal expectations for Forteo.
- Not a fracture-endpoint study.
