# Summary

- Short clinical review of secondary osteoporosis for practicing clinicians (Australian Prescriber).
- Secondary osteoporosis may be suspected in patients presenting with fragility fracture, especially younger adults.
- Workup for secondary causes indicated for premenopausal women with osteoporosis and men aged <50 with fragility fractures or osteopenia/low BMD.
- Lists major endocrine, GI, renal, drug-induced, and lifestyle contributors.
- Notes that identifying and treating the underlying cause can improve bone outcomes and alter pharmacologic choices.
- Practical laboratory and clinical checklist style useful for case-based research corpora.
- Reinforces Z-score concepts and when low BMD is unexpected for age.
- Accessible open full text via PMC.

# Key data points

- Explicit indication for secondary workup: men <50 with fractures or low BMD.
- Secondary osteoporosis less common overall than primary but concentrated in men and younger patients.
- Causes span hypogonadism, glucocorticoids, malabsorption, hypercalciuria, thyrotoxicosis, etc.
- Management pairs cause-directed therapy with standard osteoporosis measures.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Age 38 male with severe low BMD is the textbook population for this workup mandate.
- Supports continuing evaluation beyond normal calcium, PTH, and vitamin D.
- Aligns hypercalciuria and historical hypogonadism as high-yield secondary targets.
- Fragility-type pars defect fits “suspect secondary osteoporosis” trigger.

# Caveats

- Brief prescriber-oriented article, not a systematic evidence review.
- Australian practice context; drug availability may differ.
- Limited detail on anabolic therapy selection or BTM interpretation.
