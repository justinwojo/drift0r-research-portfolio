# Summary

- International pathology consensus from the 2011 Boston symposium defining how pathologists should diagnose IgG4-RD.
- Diagnosis rests primarily on **morphology**: dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, **storiform fibrosis**, and **obliterative phlebitis**, plus increased IgG4+ plasma cells / IgG4:IgG ratio as secondary supports.
- Explicitly warns that elevated serum IgG4 alone is insufficient and that strict criteria are needed when proposing new organ sites.

# Key data points

- Critical triad: (1) dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate; (2) storiform-pattern fibrosis; (3) obliterative phlebitis.
- Tissue IgG4 counts and IgG4/IgG ratios are important but **secondary** to morphology.
- Terminology scheme for “histologically highly suggestive” vs “probable” disease based on biopsy features.
- Clinicopathologic correlation required; guidelines do not replace clinical judgment.

# Caveats

- Pathologist-focused; limited guidance on isolated mild serum elevations without tissue.
- Organ-specific cell-count cutoffs vary and were refined later.
- Research summary only.
