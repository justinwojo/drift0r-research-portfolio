# Summary

- Prospective **randomized controlled trial** comparing surgical management (posterolateral fusion ± pedicle instrumentation) vs a structured **exercise** program in adults with symptomatic isthmic spondylolisthesis.
- Primary message: **surgery improved function and pain more efficiently** than the exercise program at the study’s primary follow-up horizon.
- Establishes level-I-class comparative evidence that operative fusion can outperform nonoperative exercise alone in selected symptomatic adults—not that everyone needs surgery.
- Companion analyses from the same program address instrumented vs non-instrumented fusion and longer-term outcomes.

# Key data points

- Population: symptomatic adults with radiographically verified isthmic spondylolisthesis (classic low-grade L5–S1 phenotype).
- Randomization arms: exercise program vs posterolateral fusion in situ vs instrumented fusion without reduction (grouped surgically vs conservatively in part 1 analyses).
- Outcome domains: pain and functional scores favoring surgery over exercise.
- Long-term extension literature (Ekman et al.) suggests modest durable advantage of fusion vs 1-year exercise, with some attenuation of early differences.
- Does not require high-grade slip for surgical benefit—low-grade symptomatic disease was the clinical problem.

# Caveats

- Year-2000 technique and rehab protocols; modern MIS TLIF/ALIF/LLIF and multimodal PT differ.
- Does **not** study patients with markedly low BMD (T-scores in −3.7 to −4.5 range) or multi-system illness.
- Exercise arm is not equivalent to contemporary comprehensive conservative care or SCS.
- Part 1 of a series—read with companion papers for instrumentation and long-term data.
- Not medical advice.
