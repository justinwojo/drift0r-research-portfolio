# Summary

- AAAAI Mast Cell Disorders Committee work group report on diagnosing and managing **mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS)**.
- Endorses the three-part consensus framework: (1) episodic multisystem symptoms of mast-cell mediator release; (2) acute increase in a validated mast-cell mediator (classically tryptase using the 20% + 2 ng/mL rule over baseline); (3) response to antimediator therapy.
- Distinguishes primary (clonal, e.g., systemic mastocytosis / monoclonal MCAS), secondary (IgE-mediated, inflammatory), and idiopathic MCAS.
- Elevated **baseline** tryptase alone (as in HαT) is **not** MCAS; baseline elevation warrants evaluation for clonal disease and TPSAB1 copy number, not automatic MCAS labeling.

# Key data points

- Acute tryptase should be drawn 30 min–4 h after a spell and compared with a baseline obtained when asymptomatic (≥24 h after resolution).
- Diagnostic tryptase rise: baseline × 1.2 + 2 ng/mL (Valent consensus operationalized here).
- Other mediators (n-methylhistamine, 11β-PGF2α, LTE4) may support diagnosis when properly timed and assayed.
- KIT D816V testing (blood/bone marrow as indicated) when clonal disease suspected; baseline tryptase >20 ng/mL is a minor SM criterion (WHO)—HαT can confound this.
- Management: H1/H2 blockers, mast-cell stabilizers, leukotriene modifiers, epinephrine for anaphylaxis; omalizumab in selected refractory cases; avoid unproven “mast cell” panels as sole basis for diagnosis.

# Caveats

- Does not fully integrate later ECNM/AIM proposals for “normal” BST range up to ~15 ng/mL including asymptomatic HαT.
- Work group report ≠ formal AAAAI practice parameter with GRADE tables throughout.
- Does not explain osteoporosis, hypogonadism, or IgG4 patterns.
