# Summary

- Retrospective andrology/infertility clinic series of men **<50 years** with testosterone deficiency who underwent baseline DXA (n=399), with a treated follow-up DXA subgroup (n=75; mean ~30 months).
- Baseline low BMD was common: **35.3% osteopenia** and **2.75% osteoporosis** by T-score criteria at spine and/or hip—supporting DXA even in younger hypogonadal men.
- Treatment split: testosterone (n=43) **increased** spine BMD; **clomiphene citrate or anastrozole** (pooled n=21) was associated with **significant spine BMD decrease** (p=0.003). Hip BMD did not change significantly in any arm.
- Authors conclude estrogen-pathway modulators may harm spine BMD despite raising T, while exogenous T improved spine density—directly relevant to SERM/AI strategies in osteoporotic young men.

# Key data points

- Entry: total T <350 ng/dL or free T <1.5 ng/dL; age <50.
- Baseline: osteopenia 141/399 (35.3%); osteoporosis 11/399 (2.75%) at lumbar spine.
- Higher BMI independently associated with higher spine and hip BMD.
- Follow-up DXA mean 30.4 ± 16.2 months.
- T therapy: spine BMD ↑ (p<0.001); CC or anastrozole: spine BMD ↓ (p=0.003); hip NS all groups.
- Treatments were clinical (not randomized); CC and AI were pooled for the BMD-decrease analysis.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 5

**Why:**
- Matches young male hypogonadism + extreme osteoporosis + historical **clomiphene** and intermittent **anastrozole**.
- Provides the scarce human BMD signal that clomiphene is **not automatically bone-neutral** even if it raises T and symptoms improve.
- Aligns directionally with Burnett-Bowie (AI → spine BMD risk) and argues for prioritizing skeletal estradiol sufficiency and DXA monitoring on any estrogen-modulating regimen.

# Caveats

- Retrospective, non-randomized, small treated DXA subset; confounding by indication likely.
- Clomiphene and anastrozole **pooled**—cannot isolate pure CC effect magnitude.
- T-score thresholds in young men are debated (Z-score often preferred clinically).
- Does not prove patient’s bone loss was drug-driven amid multi-hit etiology.
- Not medical advice.
