# Summary

- Review of beriberi as a nutritional complication after gastric resection or bariatric surgery due to thiamine deficiency.
- Explains thiamine’s roles in glucose metabolism, oxygen delivery, cardiac function, neurotransmission, and neuronal survival.
- Emphasizes that patients may harbor subclinical deficiency and that beriberi can be precipitated by acute illness (e.g., sepsis) or poor intake—even years after surgery and even in apparently well-nourished patients.
- Advocates long-term nutritional follow-up and prompt thiamine administration to reduce morbidity/mortality.

# Key data points

- Onset can be days to many years after gastrectomy/bariatric procedures.
- Dry beriberi polyneuropathy and Wernicke encephalopathy are key neurologic forms.
- Acute metabolic demand (illness, reduced intake, carbohydrate loads) can tip compensated deficiency into clinical disease.
- Prompt supplemental thiamine improves outcomes.
- National guideline-aligned follow-up of vitamin/mineral status after upper GI surgery is recommended.

# Caveats

- Focused on gastric/bariatric populations, not spinal cord stimulator surgery.
- Review-level evidence; heterogeneous case literature.
- Does not address benfotiamine specifically.
