# Summary

- Experimental study of exogenous testosterone effects on self- and partner-reported aggression and mood.
- Eugonadal men: T enanthate 200 mg IM weekly × 8 weeks vs saline — **no significant rise in aggression/mood pathology**.
- Hypogonadal men treated with T: **reductions in negative mood** (tension, anger, fatigue) and increased vigor.
- Trait impulsivity predicted aggression more than T levels; challenges simplistic “more T → more aggression” narratives.

# Key data points

- Eugonadal n = 30 randomized; hypogonadal n = 8 treated biweekly T enanthate.
- Cognitive/motor impulsivity predicted self-reported total aggression beyond age and T.
- Hypogonadal mood improved with physiologic replacement rather than becoming aggressive.

**Why:**
- Supports that **restoring T in hypogonadal men can reduce anger/tension** — consistent with patient reports of behavioral dysregulation when low/off therapy and improvement when repleted.

- Does not explain hypersexuality specifically; small hypogonadal arm.

# Caveats

- Tiny hypogonadal sample; short duration; laboratory aggression measures imperfectly map to real-world behavior.
- Supraphysiologic abuse doses differ from replacement.
- Not a study of paradoxical high-drive states at T ~34 ng/dL.
