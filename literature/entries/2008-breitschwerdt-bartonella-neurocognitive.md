# Summary

- Reports isolation or molecular detection of B. henselae or B. vinsonii subsp. berkhoffii in blood from six immunocompetent patients with chronic neurological/neurocognitive syndromes (seizures, ataxia, memory loss, tremors).
- Each patient had substantial animal contact or recent arthropod exposure as a potential risk factor.
- Argues chronic neurological syndromes associated with persistent Bartonella bacteremia are under-characterized and warrant further study.

# Key data points

- n=6 immunocompetent patients—not immunosuppressed classic BA/peliosis population.
- Pathogens: B. henselae and/or B. vinsonii subsp. berkhoffii.
- Clinical features clustered around chronic neurocognitive/neurologic dysfunction rather than acute CSD.
- Authors call for additional studies; not a controlled prevalence study.

# Caveats

- Tiny uncontrolled series; detection methods specialized.
- Risk-factor exposures present in all cases—selection of referred patients.
- Does not address IgM immunoblot or periosteal MRI findings.
- Causal role still unproven.
