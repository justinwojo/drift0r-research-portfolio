# Why this card exists

Third of three cards filed in v0.4.0 against H3's previously empty contradicting list.

# What it addresses

The reported excess fracture risk in stone disease was **confined to the spine**, with no
increase at other sites. That is a limit on how much a stone-and-calcium mechanism can
explain when low readings appear at spine, hip and forearm at once.

# Scope limits (read before citing)

Two corrections were applied to this card's own draft before it was filed, and both matter:

1. **The draft claimed the case was "CT-negative for stones."** That premise is false. The
   urology summary records **one lifetime stone**, passed spontaneously, during a documented
   period of dehydration. A negative CT in 2026 means no *current* stone; it does not place
   this record outside the stone-forming category. The corrected caveat is about **burden,
   not category** — this is the mildest possible end of the cohort, so the comparison
   applies, and applies weakly.
2. **Do not cite this as "stones are harmless to bone."** The vertebral signal the cohort
   reports is a real elevation. What it argues against is a *uniform multi-site* skeletal
   effect, not a skeletal effect.

Historical cohort; stone workup and composition practice of the era differ from current.
Abstract-level only — no numeric result is carried into any claim or public page.
