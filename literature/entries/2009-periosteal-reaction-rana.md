# Summary

- Educational review of **periosteal reaction** as a nonspecific cortical response to insult (infection, trauma, tumor, drugs, arthropathy, venous stasis, metabolic).
- Organizes reactions into **aggressive vs nonaggressive** patterns (solid, single-layer, multilayered/lamellated, spiculated/sunburst, Codman triangle) that narrow but do not alone prove etiology.
- Emphasizes clinical context + distribution (unilateral focal vs multifocal/symmetric) when ranking differentials for long-bone periosteal change.

# Key data points

- Periosteal reaction = new bone formation after periosteal stimulation; not a diagnosis.
- **Nonaggressive** patterns (thin solid, continuous) favor slow processes: healing fracture, chronic osteomyelitis, HOA-type periostosis, venous stasis.
- **Aggressive** patterns favor rapid processes: acute osteomyelitis, Ewing/osteosarcoma, Langerhans, aggressive infection — but infection can look aggressive.
- Multifocal/symmetric periostosis raises HOA, thyroid acropachy, hypervitaminosis A, voriconazole, Caffey (peds), venous insufficiency.
- Focal unilateral long-bone periosteal change: trauma, infection, surface tumor, adjacent soft-tissue inflammation.

# Caveats

- Pattern taxonomy is mostly radiograph/CT-centric; **MRI periosteal edema** is more sensitive and less specific than classic radiographic solid periostosis.
- Case has **mild unilateral** MRI finding without aggressive periosteal new bone on plain film description—lower pretest for aggressive neoplasm.
- Review does not address imaging-negative soft-tissue nodules separately.
