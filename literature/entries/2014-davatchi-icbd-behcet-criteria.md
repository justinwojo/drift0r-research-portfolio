# Summary

- Multinational collaboration derived **International Criteria for Behçet’s Disease (ICBD)** because ISG 1990 criteria had limited sensitivity in some regions.
- ICBD uses a **point system** (oral ulcers, genital ulcers, ocular lesions, skin lesions, vascular, neurologic, pathergy optional point) rather than mandatory oral ulcers + 2 of 4.
- Validation set: sensitivity ~94.8% vs ISG ~85.0%; specificity ~90.5% vs ISG ~96.0%.
- Proposed for both diagnosis guidance and classification in research settings.

# Key data points

- ISG 1990 (classic): recurrent oral ulceration **plus** two of recurrent genital ulcers, eye lesions, skin lesions, positive pathergy.
- ICBD improves sensitivity especially where pathergy testing is inconsistently available or phenotype differs by geography.
- Behçet is a variable-vessel vasculitis with mucocutaneous, ocular, vascular, and neurologic domains—not a primary osteoporosis disease.
- Pathergy adds a point in ICBD when performed; does not define disease alone.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 1

**Why:**
- **Low similarity by design:** Behçet criteria hinge on recurrent oral ± genital ulceration, characteristic uveitis, pathergy, and vasculitis patterns—not documented as leading features in the public Drift0r case.
- Chronic polyarthralgia without synovitis/ulcers does not meet ISG or ICBD.
- Included only as a formal DDx criteria anchor so Behçet can be **explicitly scored and set aside**.

# Caveats

- Classification criteria ≠ gold-standard pathophysiology tests.
- Geographic variation in phenotype and HLA-B*51 association.
- Not medical advice.
