# Summary

- Combines a time-series clinical case series of patients with multi-organ “sick building syndrome” after water-damaged building (WDB) exposure and a double-blind placebo-controlled trial of cholestyramine (CSM).
- Authors hypothesize chronic indoor air exposure in WDBs (fungi, mycotoxins, bacteria, endotoxins, antigens, LPS, microbial VOCs) drives a multi-system syndrome.
- Diagnostic framing used exposure potential, symptoms in ≥5 organ systems, and absence of confounders.
- Represents the **origin literature** later extended into CIRS protocols (VCS testing, binder therapy, HLA claims in subsequent papers).

# Key data points

- Indoor air of WDBs described as a complex biological mixture—not mold alone.
- 28 cases enrolled in time-series assessments at multiple clinical time points.
- CSM studied as sequestering therapy for biotoxin-associated illness in the companion trial design.
- Mechanistic discussion emphasizes inflammatory and neurotoxic pathways (author interpretation).

# Caveats

- Methods and diagnostic criteria are nonstandard relative to mainstream occupational medicine.
- Subsequent CIRS claims (HLA susceptibility, proprietary biomarkers) remain highly contested.
- Small clinical series / author-linked trial—high risk of bias.
- Research catalog only.
