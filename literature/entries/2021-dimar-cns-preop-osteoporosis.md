# Summary

- CNS systematic review and evidence-based guideline on **preoperative osteoporosis assessment** before spine instrumentation.
- **Grade B**: Preoperative DXA (T-score < −2.5), opportunistic CT Hounsfield units (<97.9), or serum vitamin D3 (<20 ng/mL) predict increased risk of osteoporosis-related adverse events after spine surgery.
- **Grade B**: Preoperative **teriparatide** increases BMD, induces earlier/more robust fusion, and may improve select outcomes (lower screw loosening, better fusion rates).
- Insufficient evidence that **bisphosphonates alone** reduce postoperative adverse events in this context.
- Explicitly supports counseling osteoporotic patients about higher risk of nonunion, screw loosening, cage subsidence, and junctional failure—and optimizing bone before elective instrumentation when feasible.

# Key data points

- Adverse events linked to poor bone: lower fusion rate, instrumentation failure (screw loosening, cage subsidence), proximal junctional failure.
- Assessment modalities: DXA T-score, CT HU, 25-OH vitamin D.
- Therapeutic signal strongest for anabolic therapy (**teriparatide**) preoperatively vs antiresorptives alone.
- Recommendation language: clinicians should consider preoperative teriparatide in osteoporotic patients undergoing spinal instrumentation.
- Full guideline text on CNS website under perioperative spine series.

# Caveats

- Evidence base largely older adults/postmenopausal cohorts—not young men with secondary osteoporosis, HαT, hypogonadism, hypercalciuria.
- Teriparatide duration, washout, and combination strategies not fully standardized across included studies.
- Hypercalciuria/kidney-stone risk (patient concern with teriparatide) requires separate metabolic evaluation—not covered in depth here.
- Not medical advice.
