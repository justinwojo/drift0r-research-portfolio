# Summary

- Clinical review defining HαT as an **autosomal-dominant trait** from increased germline *TPSAB1* α-tryptase copies.
- Emphasizes **gene-dose** relationships: more α copies → higher basal tryptase and, among symptomatic carriers, greater symptom burden.
- Frames HαT as a trait that elevates BST and can associate with multisystem complaints, while remaining distinct from clonal mastocytosis.

# Key data points

- Diagnosis requires elevated BST **plus** confirmed *TPSAB1* α copy-number gain (ddPCR-style genotyping historically gold standard).
- Many carriers are minimally symptomatic; phenotype is incomplete and variable.
- Elevated BST in HαT is largely from increased constitutive tryptase production/secretion, not episodic degranulation alone.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 5

**Why:** Directly maps to confirmed HαT with tryptase in the mid-teens. Supports interpreting modest BST elevation as genetic trait rather than de facto MCAS or SM.

# Caveats

- Review, not a new prospective natural-history cohort.
- Symptom lists can be over-applied if every carrier is assumed “diseased.”
- Does not resolve when bone marrow biopsy is mandatory in HαT+ patients with markedly low BMD for age.
