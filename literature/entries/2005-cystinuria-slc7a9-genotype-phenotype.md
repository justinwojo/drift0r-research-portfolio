# Summary

- Large molecular series of cystinuria probands analyzing SLC3A1 and SLC7A9 mutations, including 40 newly reported variants.
- Supports classification into type A (two SLC3A1 alleles), type B (two SLC7A9 alleles), and rare mixed/digenic patterns with partial phenotypes.
- Documents that SLC7A9 heterozygotes frequently show elevated urinary dibasic amino acids (incomplete dominance) with lower stone penetrance than biallelic disease.
- Foundational for interpreting heterozygous SLC7A9 findings as usually a mild carrier/partial phenotype rather than classic recurrent cystine stone disease.

# Key data points

- Mutations identified in 97% of probands (282 alleles, 86.8% detection context as reported in abstract/full series).
- Type B (SLC7A9) heterozygotes: urinary phenotype often abnormal; clinical stone disease less penetrant than biallelic cystinuria.
- Digenic inheritance can produce partial phenotypes in some families.
- Distinguishes severe early recurrent cystine lithiasis (typical biallelic disease) from milder carrier biochemistry.

# Caveats

- Does not explain idiopathic-range hypercalciuria, alkaline-urine CaP risk, or osteoporosis; those are separate axes.
- Individual variants differ; not every SLC7A9 het has the same urinary phenotype.
- Stone composition in the patient’s single stone is not documented as cystine.
