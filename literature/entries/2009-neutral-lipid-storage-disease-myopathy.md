# Summary

- Neutral lipid storage diseases (NLSD) are rare disorders of triglyceride catabolism (*PNPLA2*/ATGL → NLSD with myopathy; *ABHD5*/CGI-58 → Chanarin–Dorfman with ichthyosis).
- NLSD-M: progressive skeletal myopathy, often cardiomyopathy, elevated CK common, lipid droplets in muscle and leukocytes (**Jordan anomaly** on peripheral smear).
- Systemic lipid accumulation can affect liver and other organs; onset may be adult.
- Diagnosis: clinical + smear/muscle biopsy lipid storage + gene testing; management largely supportive/cardiac surveillance.

# Key data points

- CK is frequently elevated in lipid storage myopathies (contrast with pure arthralgia syndromes).
- Weakness is proximal/axial more than migratory joint pain.
- Cardiac involvement drives prognosis in PNPLA2 disease.
- Jordan anomaly is a cheap morphological clue when present.

- **Does not match:** Dominant phenotype is **joint pain/fragility + osteoporosis**, not progressive lipid myopathy with high CK/cardiomyopathy. No ichthyosis. Thiamine-responsive neuropathy, HαT, IgG4, hypercalciuria unexplained. Imaging-neg polyarthralgia with normal CRP is not NLSD’s core.
- **When to revisit:** Unexplained high CK, lipid droplets on biopsy, or cardiomyopathy on echo.

# Caveats

- Rare; pretest probability low without myopathic labs/biopsy clues.
- Many other metabolic myopathies (McArdle, CPT-II, mitochondrial) share exercise intolerance.
