# Summary

- Investigated Bartonella serology among Swedish patients with presumed tick-bite exposure and symptom duration ≥6 months (n=224).
- Seroprevalence 7% for B. henselae and 1% for B. quintana (one dual positive).
- Compared seropositive patients with matched seronegative controls from same cohort: tick bites actually more common in seronegative group; animal contact and symptom profiles similar.
- Most common symptoms overall: fatigue, muscular symptoms, arthralgia, cognitive symptoms—none specifically linked to seropositivity.

# Key data points

- B. henselae seroprevalence 7%—higher than some prior Swedish blood-donor estimates but lower than southern European donors.
- No symptom signature distinguished seropositive vs seronegative persistent-symptom patients.
- Positive serology ≠ explanatory diagnosis for chronic symptom complexes in this design.
- Tick-bite history did not track with seropositivity in the expected direction within this selected cohort.

# Caveats

- Serology only—no enrichment PCR/bacteremia assessment.
- Swedish epidemiology; different vectors/reservoirs than Texas.
- Drift0r reports no known tick bite (still not fully excluding other exposures).
- Does not rule out true infection in individual PCR-confirmed cases.
