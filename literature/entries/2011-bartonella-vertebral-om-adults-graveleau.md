# Summary

- Describes clinical features, diagnosis, treatment, and outcome of **adult** patients with **Bartonella henselae vertebral osteomyelitis**, with literature context.
- Demonstrates that B. henselae bone infection is not limited to children with classic cat-scratch disease.
- Highlights diagnostic reliance on **serology/PCR** because cultures are usually negative.

# Key data points

- Adult immunocompetent hosts can develop Bartonella vertebral osteomyelitis.
- Presentation may include back pain ± fever ± lymphadenopathy; inflammatory markers variable.
- Imaging (MRI) shows osteomyelitis/discitis-like signal; microbiologic yield from standard culture is poor.
- Serology and molecular methods are key; histopathology may show granulomatous inflammation.
- Antibiotic selection often includes agents with intracellular activity; duration individualized; prognosis often favorable when recognized.

# Caveats

- Small case-level evidence; publication bias toward dramatic vertebral presentations.
- Patient’s spinal disease is structural spondylolisthesis/pars defects, not reported as infectious spondylodiscitis.
- Does not address imaging-negative superficial tendon nodules.
