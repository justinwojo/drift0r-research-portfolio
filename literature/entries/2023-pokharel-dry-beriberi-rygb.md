# Summary

- Case of dry beriberi after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass presenting with paresthesia, lower-extremity weakness, and altered mental status approximately one year after surgery.
- Illustrates that post-bariatric thiamine deficiency neuropathy may be delayed, not only immediate postoperative.
- Reviews that bariatric procedures increase risk via reduced intake, vomiting, and/or malabsorption, and that dry beriberi is a recognized neurologic complication.
- Emphasizes consideration of thiamine deficiency in post-bariatric patients with new neuropathy or encephalopathy.

# Key data points

- Presentation: sensory symptoms + weakness ± mental status change after RYGB.
- Timing: months to years post-op possible (here ~1 year).
- Dry beriberi remains on the differential alongside Wernicke encephalopathy after metabolic surgery.
- Thiamine repletion is both diagnostic trial and therapy.
- Long-term nutritional monitoring after RYGB is standard recommendation in broader literature.
