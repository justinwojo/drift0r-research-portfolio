# Summary

- Mayo Clinic practice-based series of 3,300 patients with IgG subclass testing over 2 years; 158 (4.8%) had IgG4 >140 mg/dL.
- Only **29/158 (18.4%)** had definite or possible IgG4-RD.
- Non-RD diagnoses spanned biliary, pancreatic, hepatic, and pulmonary disease plus systemic vasculitis—confirming elevated IgG4 is a nonspecific laboratory finding in clinical practice.

# Key data points

- Elevation cutoff used: IgG4 >140 mg/dL.
- Prevalence of elevation among tested: 4.8%.
- IgG4-RD fraction among elevated: ~18% (definite + possible).
- Non-RD spectrum: biliary tract disease, pancreatitis/cancer workups, liver disease, lung disease, systemic vasculitis.
- Multi-organ IgG4-RD tended toward higher serum levels than single-organ or non-RD (overlap remains).

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4

**Why:** Quantifies that most patients with elevated IgG4 do **not** have IgG4-RD—highly applicable to a ~150–165 mg/dL elevation without organ fibrosis. Supports keeping a wide differential (infection, inflammatory, biliary/pancreatic, vasculitis, idiopathic).

# Caveats

- Testing indication bias (suspected IgG4-RD or immunodeficiency).
- Does not focus on isolated IgG1 deficiency or specific antibody deficiency co-patterns.
- Research catalog only.
