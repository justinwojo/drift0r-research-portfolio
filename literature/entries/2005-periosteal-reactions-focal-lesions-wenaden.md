# Summary

- Review of **periosteal reaction (PR)** patterns associated with **focal bone lesions**, and how PR morphology contributes to radiologic characterization.
- Distinguishes continuous/solid patterns from interrupted/aggressive patterns and links them to growth rate of the underlying process.
- Stresses integrated reading of matrix, zone of transition, soft-tissue mass, and clinical tempo.

# Key data points

- PR is a cortical response that helps grade biological activity of an adjacent or intrinsic bone process.
- Slow lesions → solid/continuous periosteal new bone; rapid lesions → lamellated, spiculated, interrupted patterns, Codman triangle.
- Infection can produce aggressive PR and soft-tissue change, overlapping malignancy.
- Absence of a discrete soft-tissue mass or aggressive PR narrows concern for high-grade surface sarcoma.
- Radiographs remain foundational for PR morphology; MRI better shows soft-tissue and marrow extent.

# Caveats

- Less directly applicable when there is **no focal bone lesion**, only mild periosteal edema.
- Does not address multifocal tendon nodules or IgG4 labs.
- Pre-dates some MRI-periosteum nuances covered in newer reviews (Allen 2023).
