# Summary

- Prospective randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 5-day oral azithromycin vs placebo in laboratory-confirmed typical CSD.
- Primary endpoint: time to 80% reduction in total lymph node volume by blinded 3D ultrasound.
- Within first 30 days, 7/14 azithromycin vs 1/15 placebo achieved 80% node volume reduction (P=0.026); after 30 days groups converged.
- Supports short-course azithromycin for faster node resolution in typical CSD, not for chronic multi-system disease.

# Key data points

- n≈29 randomized active-duty/military dependent population with typical CSD.
- Significant early nodal benefit of azithromycin; no durable between-group difference after 1 month.
- Endpoint was lymphadenopathy volume, not arthralgia, night sweats, or osteomyelitis.
- Placebo arm tended to be older demographically.

# Caveats

- Typical CSD only; not chronic bacteremia, endocarditis, or MSK-predominant disease.
- Small sample; single-center military setting.
- Does not validate long combination regimens used for suspected chronic infection.
- Patient’s strongest response included atovaquone+clindamycin backbone (Babesia-oriented) plus azithromycin.
