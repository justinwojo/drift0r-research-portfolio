# Summary

- Mechanistic review of idiopathic hypercalciuria (IH), the most common metabolic abnormality in calcium kidney stone disease.
- Integrates intestinal hyperabsorption, reduced renal tubular reabsorption, and bone resorption as overlapping contributors rather than clean mutually exclusive subtypes.
- Classic male cutoffs for hypercalciuria (~>300 mg/day) and the tendency to lose calcium from bone on restricted diets are discussed.
- Frames IH as a systems disorder of calcium economy that can produce both stones and negative skeletal balance.

# Key data points

- IH is the commonest metabolic abnormality among calcium stone formers.
- Historical definition often uses ~300 mg/day urine Ca in men (lab/diet-dependent).
- Patients with IH can continue to excrete high urine calcium even on low-calcium diets, implying a non-dietary (bone/renal) source.
- Bone density is commonly reduced in IH; the skeleton can supply a substantial fraction of excreted calcium when intake is limited.
- Distinguishing pure “renal leak” from mixed IH physiology is difficult outside research protocols.

# Caveats

- 2008 mechanistic review; genetics and transporter biology have advanced since.
- Not a treatment trial; does not quantify how much of this patient’s markedly low BMD for age is attributable to IH vs hypogonadism history, immobilization/pain, or other secondary factors. Magnitude gap: mild BMD reduction in cited IH literature ≠ spine T −4.3.
