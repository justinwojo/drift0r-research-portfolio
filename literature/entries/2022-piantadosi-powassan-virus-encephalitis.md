# Summary

- Clinical review of **Powassan virus encephalitis** as an increasingly recognized severe *Ixodes*-transmitted flaviviral infection.
- Stresses that clinical, laboratory, and imaging findings are **nonspecific**; diagnosis depends on ordering POWV-specific testing in patients with compatible exposures and neuroinvasive disease.
- Practical guidance for clinicians evaluating acute encephalitis during tick season in endemic regions.

# Key data points

- Incubation and presentation: fever, headache, altered mental status, focal neuro deficits; CSF typically inflammatory.
- MRI may show nonspecific encephalitis patterns; no pathognomonic radiologic signature.
- Testing: serum/CSF IgM with confirmatory PRNT, and PCR early; send-out to specialized/public health labs often required.
- Supportive care is mainstay; no approved POWV-specific antiviral.
