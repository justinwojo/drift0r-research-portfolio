# Summary

- One-year hospital series of all IgG subclass assays with IgG4 >1.35 g/L; charts reviewed for final diagnoses (polyclonal elevations only).
- Among 59 patients with elevated IgG4, **only ~10%** had IgG4-RD. Largest group had **repeated infections** (~25%); autoimmune disease ~13.5%; also cancer, PID, interstitial lung disease, cystic fibrosis, vasculitis, and undiagnosed cases.
- Mean IgG4 levels and IgG4/IgG ratio were higher in true IgG4-RD than in other causes—but substantial overlap.

# Key data points

- Threshold studied: serum IgG4 >1.35 g/L (135 mg/dL).
- Final diagnoses among elevated IgG4: infections 25%, autoimmune 13.5%, IgG4-RD 10%, miscellaneous (cancer, PID, ILD, CF, histiocytosis, vasculitis) remainder.
- Higher absolute IgG4 and IgG4/IgG ratio favor IgG4-RD but do not rule out mimics.
- Concludes elevation is **not specific**; exclude alternative pathology before diagnosing IgG4-RD.

# Caveats

- Single-center, referral/testing bias (tests ordered for suspected IgG4-RD or immunodeficiency).
- Does not stratify by degree of elevation in the 140–170 mg/dL band specifically.
