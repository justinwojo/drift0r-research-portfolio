# Summary

- Review of **palindromic rheumatism (PR)** as episodic, migratory peri-/articular attacks that may sit on a continuum with rheumatoid arthritis.
- Summarizes clinical pattern (abrupt-onset, migratory, self-limited attacks), imaging of synovitis/tenosynovitis during flares, serology risk stratification, and progression risk to persistent RA.
- Useful reference when a patient’s early course is **migratory** rather than additive fixed polyarthritis.

# Key data points

- PR attacks are typically acute, mono- or oligoarticular, lasting hours to days, with intervening asymptomatic periods; tendons/periarticular tissues often involved.
- Ultrasound/MRI during attacks may show synovitis or **tenosynovitis** even when between-attack exams are quieter.
- ACPA/RF positivity identifies higher risk of evolution to RA; seronegative PR exists and has different implications.
- Not all migratory joint pain is PR/RA-spectrum—infection (e.g., gonococcal, viral), crystal disease, and noninflammatory arthralgia remain on DDx.
- Management and prognosis depend on autoantibody status, attack frequency, and evolution of persistent synovitis.

# Caveats

- Classic PR is an **inflammatory** intermittent phenotype; Drift0r’s persistent noninflammatory labs argue against straightforward PR→RA continuum.
- Does not explain periosteal humeral edema, IgG4↑, osteoporosis, or specialty-lab vector signals.
- Imaging-negative chronic nodules are not the same as flare-only tenosynovitis.
