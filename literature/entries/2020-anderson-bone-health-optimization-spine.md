# Summary

- Practical framework for **bone health optimization (BHO)** before elective spine surgery: identify osteoporosis, evaluate secondary causes, treat, then time surgery.
- Osteoporosis is common and often undiagnosed in preoperative spine cohorts; poor bone predicts mechanical complications and impaired fusion biology.
- Emphasizes multi-modality assessment (DXA, TBS when available, opportunistic CT HU, labs including vitamin D) and medical therapy selection.
- **Teriparatide** highlighted as an agent that can accelerate fusion and reduce hardware complications in osteoporotic spine surgery literature.
- Supports delaying elective instrumentation when bone is inadequate—exactly the “fusion deferred for BMD” strategy.

# Key data points

- Preoperative osteoporosis prevalence substantial in spine surgical populations (study-specific figures; often >30% in screened series).
- Invalid lumbar DXA from degeneration is common—use hip/radius or CT HU when spine DXA unreliable (relevant when L-spine already collapsed/degenerated).
- BHO pathway: screen → diagnose → treat secondary factors → anabolic or antiresorptive therapy → reassess → operate.
- Case vignettes in related BHO literature show teriparatide-driven BMD gains before reconsidering surgery.
- Aligns with CNS 2021 osteoporosis assessment recommendations (lit-0173).

# Caveats

- Narrative/framework review, not a single RCT of BHO clinics vs usual care (though later BHO clinic studies exist).
- Most cited surgical series: older, often female osteoporotic patients.
- Does not solve etiology of young-male secondary osteoporosis.
- Not medical advice.
