# Summary

- Foundational family study of **idiopathic hypercalciuria (IH)** showing familial clustering consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.
- Establishes hypercalciuria as a heritable trait, not merely dietary misbehavior.
- Anchor citation for later twin/heritability estimates (~50% for urinary calcium excretion) and linkage studies (VDR region, SAC/ADCY10 region, etc.).
- Clinical implication: relatives of hypercalciuric stone formers warrant metabolic attention; bone loss can accompany lifelong renal calcium leak/absorptive phenotypes.

# Key data points

- Pedigrees of IH probands demonstrate multi-generational hypercalciuria.
- Inheritance pattern most consistent with autosomal dominant transmission with incomplete penetrance/variable expressivity.
- Separates monogenic hypercalciuric stone syndromes (e.g., Dent, CLCN5, ADHH) from polygenic/common IH.
- Frames IH as a chronic mineral-balance disorder with skeletal as well as renal consequences.

# Caveats

- 1979 methods predate modern genetics; no single IH gene explains most families.
- Does not quantify BMD effects (later urine-calcium–bone-loss literature does).
- Patient also has *SLC7A9* heterozygosity—separate cystine-handling axis from calcium IH.
