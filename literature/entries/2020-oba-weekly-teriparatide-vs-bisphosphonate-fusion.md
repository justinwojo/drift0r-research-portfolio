# Summary

- Multicenter, prospective, **randomized** comparison of weekly teriparatide versus bisphosphonate therapy for bone union over 6 months after multilevel lumbar interbody fusion in osteoporotic patients >50 years.
- Instrumentation with pedicle/iliac/S2AI screws after interbody cages and autograft.
- Evaluates impact of anabolic vs antiresorptive pharmacologic strategy on early fusion biology and graft–endplate bone contact.
- Directly informs the clinical choice many patients face when fusion is planned or deferred for bone quality.

# Key data points

- Population: osteoporotic patients undergoing multilevel LIF—higher pseudarthrosis/hardware-risk group.
- Weekly TPTD formulation (common in Japan) vs BP comparator—formulation differs from daily teriparatide (class discussion) but same PTH(1–34) pathway.
- Outcomes center on radiographic bone union and bone-contact status of grafted bone at ~6 months.
- Supports anabolic advantage narrative seen in Ohtori-era fusion literature and Cheng network meta-analysis.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4–5

**Why:** Fusion has been **deferred** pending bone optimization; patient is weighing rebuild strategies (anabolic first vs antiresorptive) under extreme BMD deficit. This RCT is among the cleaner head-to-head spine-fusion pharmacologic comparisons, complementing existing Ohtori/Cheng cards.

# Caveats

- Age >50 osteoporotic cohort—older than age 38; male severe secondary OP evidence thinner.
- Weekly TPTD regimen ≠ US daily teriparatide labeling; effect direction still informative.
- Hypercalciuria/stone risk modifies anabolic candidacy—pair with teriparatide safety/hypercalciuria literature in corpus.
- Short 6-month radiographic horizon; long-term hardware failure not fully captured.
