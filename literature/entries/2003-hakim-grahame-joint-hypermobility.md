# Summary

- Focused clinical review of the commonly encountered (“benign”) joint hypermobility syndrome (JHS), as distinct from rarer heritable connective-tissue disorders broadly.
- Covers recognition, epidemiology, clinical features, and management approaches used in dedicated hypermobility clinics.
- Emphasizes that recognition itself is therapeutic—many patients cycle through unexplained pain labels until hypermobility is identified.
- Management centers on physiotherapy, joint protection, and addressing secondary pain rather than immunosuppression.

# Key data points

- JHS presents with joint pain, instability, soft-tissue injuries, and sometimes multi-system symptoms without inflammatory arthritis.
- Beighton score and clinical questionnaires (Hakim 5-point) aid recognition.
- Brighton criteria historically used for JHS diagnosis (superseded/reframed by 2017 hEDS/HSD framework for many purposes).
- Noninflammatory labs and normal synovitis workup are expected.

# Caveats

- Terminology outdated relative to 2017 EDS/HSD classification (see lit-0111, lit-0120).
- “Benign” label is often contested by patients with high disability.
- Does not address neuropathic or metabolic contributors to joint failure.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
