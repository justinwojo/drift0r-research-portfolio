# Summary

- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of teriparatide in 437 men with osteoporosis (spine/hip BMD >2 SD below young male mean).
- Arms: placebo, teriparatide 20 µg/day, teriparatide 40 µg/day; all received calcium and vitamin D.
- Median treatment ~11 months (trial stopped early after rat osteosarcoma toxicology findings).
- Bone formation markers rose early, followed by resorption markers—classic anabolic window.
- Lumbar spine BMD +5.9% (20 µg) and +9.0% (40 µg) vs placebo (p<0.001); femoral neck +1.5% and +2.9%.
- No radial BMD gain; whole-body BMC modestly increased.
- Response similar regardless of gonadal status, age, baseline BMD, BMI, smoking, or alcohol.
- Established teriparatide as effective anabolic therapy for male osteoporosis including idiopathic and hypogonadal subgroups.

# Key data points

- N=437 men; median ~11 months therapy.
- Spine BMD: +5.9% (20 µg), +9.0% (40 µg).
- Femoral neck: +1.5% (20 µg), +2.9% (40 µg).
- Formation markers up first, then resorption markers.
- Efficacy independent of hypogonadal vs eugonadal status.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Directly supports teriparatide/Forteo as a candidate for severe male osteoporosis.
- Includes idiopathic and hypogonadal men—both relevant to Drift0r history.
- Upper-normal CTX and high-turnover phenotype may be compatible with monitoring anabolic response (formation markers expected to rise further on TPTD).
- Extreme spine T-scores (−4.3) exceed typical trial entry severity in magnitude but same disease class.

# Caveats

- Trial stopped early; fracture endpoints underpowered (see Kaufman follow-up).
- 40 µg dose not the marketed dose (20 µg is standard).
- Radius did not improve—relevant if forearm is a problem site (−3.3).
- Not specifically a “young adult only” trial; mean age higher than 38.
