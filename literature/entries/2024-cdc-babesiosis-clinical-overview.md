# Summary

- Human babesiosis is an intraerythrocytic protozoal infection (*Babesia microti* predominant in the US) transmitted mainly by *Ixodes* ticks; also via blood transfusion and rarely congenital routes.
- Clinical spectrum: asymptomatic parasitemia → flu-like febrile illness → severe disease with hemolytic anemia, organ failure, death—risk highest in asplenia, advanced age, and immunocompromise.
- Diagnosis: Giemsa-stained blood smear (Maltese cross forms classic but not always seen), **PCR** (sensitive), and serology (epidemiologic/retrospective more than acute stand-alone).
- Geography: Northeast and upper Midwest US are classic endemic zones; expanding range reported.

# Key data points

- Coinfection considerations with Lyme disease and anaplasmosis share *Ixodes* vectors.
- Immunocompromised hosts can have persistent/relapsing parasitemia despite standard therapy (see clinical-care page and IDSA 2020).
- PCR may remain positive after clinical recovery; interpretation requires clinical context.
- Blood product screening policies evolved as transfusion-transmission risk was recognized.
