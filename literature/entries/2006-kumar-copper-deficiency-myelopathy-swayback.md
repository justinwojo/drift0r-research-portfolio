# Summary

- Landmark clinical review of acquired copper deficiency myelopathy based on Mayo Clinic experience plus literature.
- Neurologic syndrome: spastic gait and prominent sensory ataxia resembling subacute combined degeneration of B12 deficiency; axonal neuropathy common.
- Hematologic clues: anemia and neutropenia; marrow can mimic myelodysplasia—but neurology may occur **without** hematologic abnormalities.
- Causes: prior gastric surgery, excessive zinc ingestion, malabsorption; often idiopathic. Hyperzincemia can accompany hypocupremia even without known zinc intake.

# Key data points

- MRI may show dorsal-column T2 hyperintensity (not required for diagnosis).
- Copper and B12 deficiency can coexist—check both.
- Copper repletion promptly corrects anemia/neutropenia; neurologic improvement is slower, often incomplete, and mostly sensory—early treatment prevents progression.
- Diagnostic panel typically: serum copper, ceruloplasmin, zinc, CBC; 24h urine copper sometimes adjunctive.

# Caveats

- Distinct from Wilson disease (copper **overload**)—see lit-0247 and existing lit-0198.
- Overlaps with existing corpus card lit-0186 (Jaiser 2010); this is the earlier Mayo primary source.
