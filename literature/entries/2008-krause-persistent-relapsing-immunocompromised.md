# Summary

- Retrospective case-control study of patients with **persistent/relapsing *B. microti*** despite repeated antibabesial courses, compared with controls who cleared after one standard course.
- **All case patients were immunosuppressed** at acute infection; <10% of controls were.
- Frames mainstream view: prolonged symptomatic relapsing babesiosis is primarily a disease of impaired host immunity (B-cell lymphoma, asplenia, rituximab), not of otherwise immunocompetent hosts with normal labs and no parasitemia.

# Key data points

- Cases n=14 vs controls n=46; southern New England, New York, Wisconsin.
- Case cluster: B-cell lymphoma, asplenia, prior rituximab common.
- Cases had more complications; **3 deaths**.
- Infection cleared in most cases only after **≥6 weeks** continuous antibabesial therapy (often combination/prolonged strategies).
- Controls resolved after single standard course (atovaquone–azithromycin or clindamycin–quinine class regimens).

# Caveats

- Does not prove immunocompetent hosts never have prolonged infection (see Krause 1998 PCR persistence), but symptomatic multi-month relapsing disease with treatment failure clustered in immunocompromised.
- Small case series; treatment regimens heterogeneous.
- Does not evaluate FISH assays.
