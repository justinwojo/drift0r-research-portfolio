# Summary

- Cross-sectional UK primary-care study comparing moderate and **heavy** recreational cannabis smokers with cigarette-smoking controls on DXA, bone turnover, vitamin D, and fracture history.
- Heavy users (≥5000 lifetime smoking episodes) had lower total-hip and spine BMD Z-scores, lower BMI, higher CTX/P1NP, lower 25-OH vitamin D, and roughly **double** fracture rate vs controls.
- Multiple regression: heavy cannabis use remained an independent predictor of hip and spine BMD; mediation analysis suggested the spine effect was partly **indirect via lower BMI**.
- Supports treating heavy cannabis as a plausible co-factor in secondary osteoporosis workups—not a sole explanation for markedly low BMD for age in a young male.

# Key data points

- Heavy users n=144; moderate n=56; cigarette-smoking controls n=114.
- Hip Z-score: heavy −0.20 ± 0.9 vs control +0.2 ± 0.9 (p<0.0005).
- Spine Z-score: heavy −0.5 ± 1.2 vs control 0.0 ± 1.2 (p<0.0005).
- Fracture rate ratio 2.17 (95% CI 1.59–2.95).
- CTX and P1NP higher; 25-OH D lower in heavy users.
- Heavy use explained ~5–6% of hip/spine BMD variance in regression models.

# Caveats

- Observational; residual confounding (nutrition, activity, other substances, socioeconomic factors).
- Lifetime episode thresholds do not map cleanly to modern THC potency or edible/vape patterns.
- Not a cessation trial; does not prove BMD recovers with abstinence.
- Not medical advice.
