# Summary

- Paraneoplastic arthritides are rare remote effects of cancer: migratory non-erosive arthritis, palmar fasciitis–polyarthritis, remitting seronegative symmetric synovitis with pitting edema (RS3PE), hypertrophic osteoarthropathy (HOA), and tumor-induced osteomalacia (TIO; FGF23).
- Clues: explosive late-onset arthritis, poor DMARD response, constitutional symptoms, clubbing/periostitis (HOA), or phosphate wasting (TIO).
- Solid tumors (lung, GI, ovary, hematologic) appear in series; arthritis may precede cancer diagnosis by months.
- Treatment of the tumor often improves the rheumatic syndrome.

# Key data points

- Inflammatory markers are **often elevated** in paraneoplastic arthritis/HOA—normal chronic CRP lowers but does not eliminate TIO/HOA variants.
- TIO presents with hypophosphatemia, osteomalacia, fractures, and FGF23 excess—check phosphate and tubular reabsorption if osteomalacia suspected.
- HOA: clubbing + periosteal new bone + arthropathy; strong lung disease/cancer association.
- Tiny pulmonary nodules ≤3 mm (as in this case’s imaging) are usually incidental but deserve guideline-based surveillance—not automatic paraneoplastic proof.

**Why:**

- **Against:** Onset ~2017 with long course without progressive cancer declaration; **normal CRP/ESR** atypical for many paraneoplastic arthritides; no clubbing described; thiamine deficiency with clear nutritional response; HαT genetically confirmed; hypogonadism treated; phosphate status not highlighted as low in pack.
- **Clinician checklist:** Review phosphate, ALP (high in osteomalacia), FGF23 if phosphate low; age-appropriate cancer screening; do not chase paraneoplasia as sole explanation of B1/HαT/IgG4 cluster without red flags.

# Caveats

- Over-investigation harms; use red-flag thresholds.
- TIO tumors can be tiny mesenchymal tumors needing specialized localization (octreotide/DOTATATE scans).
