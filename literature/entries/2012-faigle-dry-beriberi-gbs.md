# Summary

- Brief report of dry beriberi presenting as a Guillain-Barré–like syndrome as the first clinical manifestation of thiamine deficiency.
- Underscores that peripheral neuropathy from B1 deficiency can dominate the presentation before (or without) classic Wernicke features.
- Supports inclusion of thiamine deficiency in the differential of acute/subacute flaccid or areflexic weakness.
- Complements larger case-report literature on GBS mimics (e.g., Shible 2019).

# Key data points

- Dry beriberi can be the index presentation of thiamine deficiency.
- Phenotype overlaps GBS: progressive weakness, sensory symptoms, reflex changes.
- Recognition matters because thiamine repletion is disease-modifying and low-risk.
- Alcohol history is not required for consideration.
