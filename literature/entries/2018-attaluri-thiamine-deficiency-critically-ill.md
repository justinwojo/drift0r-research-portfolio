# Summary

- Clinical review stressing that thiamine deficiency is under-recognized in critically ill and septic patients.
- Catalogues cardiac (wet beriberi), neurologic (dry beriberi, Wernicke), and mixed presentations.
- Notes sepsis as a high-risk state for deficiency due to increased metabolic demand and common risk cofactors.
- Argues for low threshold to consider and replete B1 when suggestive features appear.

# Key data points

- Patients with sepsis are frequently thiamine-deficient.
- Deficiency can cause heart failure, peripheral neuropathy, and encephalopathy—overlapping common ICU syndromes.
- Risk amplified by poor nutrition, GI surgery, alcoholism, renal replacement, and high catabolism.
- Repletion is inexpensive and can be diagnostically/therapeutically clarifying when deficiency is present.

# Caveats

- Review is hospital/critical-care oriented; outpatient chronic disease extrapolation is imperfect.
- Does not address bone density effects of thiamine deficiency (separate literature).
- Cannot distinguish nutritional vs infection-driven consumption without full dietary/metabolic workup.
- Not medical advice.
