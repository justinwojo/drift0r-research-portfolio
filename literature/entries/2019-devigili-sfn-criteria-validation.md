# Summary

- Reappraisal of 150 previously diagnosed sensory neuropathy patients plus prospective validation in 352 new suspected-sensory-neuropathy subjects.
- Diagnostic framework based on clinical signs, QST, and IENFD; tested which combinations predict durable SFN at 18-month follow-up.
- Core finding: **clinical signs + abnormal QST and/or IENFD** is more reliable than lab abnormalities without signs; sensory *symptoms alone* are poor screens.
- Patients with both abnormal QST and IENFD but no baseline signs all developed clinical signs at follow-up; isolated single-test abnormality did not progress.

# Key data points

- Validation baseline SFN diagnosis in 149/352 (42.4%): two clinical signs + abnormal QST/IENFD 69.1%; abnormal IENFD alone 20.1%; abnormal QST alone 5.4%.
- Among 38 symptom-only (no signs) patients: 89.4% had normal QST and IENFD; 56% recovered fully by 18 months.
- None with a single abnormal test (QST or IENFD) developed the other abnormality or clinical signs at follow-up.
- Practical implication: do not diagnose SFN on symptoms alone; require signs and/or objective structure/function tests.

# Caveats

- Does not include QSART/autonomic battery as a third objective arm (see Thaisetthawatkul 2013).
- Follow-up was 18 months—not lifelong post-nutritional neuropathy.
- Research catalog only.
