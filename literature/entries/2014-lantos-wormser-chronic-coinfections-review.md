# Summary

- Systematic review by Lantos & Wormser evaluating evidence for chronic *Anaplasma*, *Babesia*, *B. duncani*, and *Bartonella* coinfections in patients labeled with “chronic Lyme disease.”
- **Does not find convincing evidence** for treatment-responsive chronic babesiosis in immunocompetent persons **without fever, laboratory abnormalities, and detectable parasitemia**.
- Likewise unconvinced by geographically widespread chronic *B. duncani* illness without objective parasitologic findings, and by simultaneous Lyme–Bartonella infection / tick-borne Bartonella claims as commonly practiced.
- Primary mainstream counterweight to specialty-lab chronic coinfection frameworks.

# Key data points

- Five structured PubMed Boolean searches.
- No convincing evidence for: (1) chronic anaplasmosis; (2) treatment-responsive symptomatic chronic babesiosis in immunocompetent hosts without fever/labs/parasitemia; (3) same for chronic *B. duncani* without objective findings; (4) tick-borne transmission of *Bartonella* as commonly asserted; (5) simultaneous Lyme + Bartonella infection as a validated entity in that literature set.
- Emphasizes need for objective microbiologic evidence before prolonged antimicrobial courses for “chronic coinfections.”

# Caveats

- Review reflects 2014 evidence cutoff; later FISH papers and *B. odocoilei* reports post-date it.
- Authors are leading skeptics of chronic Lyme paradigm—interpret alongside primary data, not as sole authority.
- Patient’s impressive but non-durable antibiotic responses are clinical observations not addressed as proof of infection here.
- Absence of evidence ≠ proof of absence for every individual; still raises diagnostic bar appropriately.
