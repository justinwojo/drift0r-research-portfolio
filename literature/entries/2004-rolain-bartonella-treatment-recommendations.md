# Summary

- Comprehensive treatment recommendations across clinical syndromes caused by B. bacilliformis, B. quintana, and B. henselae (CSD, bacillary angiomatosis, bacteremia, endocarditis, CNS, etc.).
- Emphasizes that in vitro MICs do not always predict clinical response because of intracellular niche and disease-specific pathophysiology.
- For CNS Bartonella infection, doxycycline plus rifampin preferred for CNS penetration; for endocarditis, aminoglycoside-containing regimens historically important.
- Typical CSD in immunocompetent hosts is often self-limited; antibiotics (including azithromycin) may hasten lymphadenopathy resolution more than alter natural history of uncomplicated disease.

# Key data points

- Syndrome-specific regimens: e.g., doxy ± rifampin for CNS; gentamicin-containing strategies for endocarditis (see also Raoult 2003).
- Macrolides (erythromycin, azithromycin) and doxycycline are mainstays for bacillary angiomatosis/peliosis in immunocompromised hosts.
- CSD treatment benefit modest for typical nodes; severe/disseminated disease warrants directed therapy.
- Reviews intracellular lifestyle and limited bactericidal options as reasons combination therapy is used in severe disease.

# Caveats

- 2004 evidence; does not cover later biofilm/intracellular combination experiments (e.g., azlocillin combinations).
- Does not address specialty-lab chronic Bartonella treatment protocols used in some LLMD practices.
- Patient’s best clinical response regimen was atovaquone+clindamycin±azithromycin (Babesia-oriented), not doxy+rifampin or gentamicin.
- Not medical advice; regimens require specialist supervision.
