# Summary

- ISCD 2023 Adult Official Positions define how DXA should be reported by age and sex.
- In men age ≥50 (and postmenopausal women): T-scores preferred; WHO densitometric classification applies.
- In males younger than age 50 (and females before menopause): Z-scores, not T-scores, are preferred.
- Z-score ≤ −2.0 = “below the expected range for age”; Z-score > −2.0 = “within the expected range for age.”
- Osteoporosis cannot be diagnosed in men under age 50 on the basis of BMD alone.
- Clinical diagnosis in younger men requires fragility fractures and/or secondary causes plus low BMD, not T-score ≤ −2.5 in isolation.
- Precision / LSC: each DXA facility should determine its precision error and calculate the least significant change (LSC); the precision error supplied by the manufacturer should not be used as a substitute for facility LSC when interpreting serial BMD change.
- Trabecular bone score (TBS): ISCD states TBS is appropriate in adults aged ≥40 years; male fracture-risk evidence is primarily from ages ≥50. Routine monitoring of TBS change is not recommended as a serial monitoring metric in the same way as BMD LSC.
- Vertebral fracture assessment (VFA): ISCD provides formal indication criteria (e.g., height loss, glucocorticoid use, and related clinical triggers). Young age plus low BMD alone is not, by itself, a complete VFA indication without meeting formal criteria.
- 33% radius may be used in certain circumstances when hip/spine cannot be interpreted.
- Essential interpretive framework for a 38-year-old man with extremely low reported T-scores.
- Peer-reviewed executive summary of the same 2023 Adult PDC positions: Shuhart et al., J Clin Densitom 2023 (doi 10.1016/j.jocd.2023.101435).

# Key data points

- Men <50: report Z-scores preferentially.
- Diagnostic phrase: “below expected range for age” if Z ≤ −2.0.
- No BMD-only osteoporosis diagnosis under age 50 in men.
- WHO T-score criteria apply to men ≥50 and postmenopausal women.
- Facility-derived LSC required for serial BMD change interpretation; manufacturer LSC discouraged as a substitute.
- TBS appropriateness starts at age ≥40; patient ~38 → applicability should be clinician-discussed / often deferred.
- VFA requires formal indication criteria, not low BMD alone in a young adult.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Age 38 → ISCD says Z-scores are the preferred reporting metric, even if T-scores are also printed.
- Lumbar T −4.3 is clinically extreme but formal densitometric “osteoporosis” label under 50 requires clinical context (fractures, secondary causes).
- Atraumatic pars fracture + secondary factors (hypogonadism history, hypercalciuria) supply the clinical diagnosis pathway ISCD expects.
- Forearm −3.3 may be relevant if 33% radius protocol used.

# Caveats

- Positions are consensus standards, not a trial.
- Many clinicians still quote T-scores in young adults for communication; both should be understood.
- Does not grade severity beyond Z ≤ −2.0 language.
- Database choice (male vs female reference) remains a separate interpretive issue.
