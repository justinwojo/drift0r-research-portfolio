# Summary

- North American Spine Society (NASS) evidence-based clinical guideline summary for **adult isthmic spondylolisthesis** (pars interarticularis defect with anterior translation, most often L5–S1).
- Defines disorder, diagnostic approach, and medical/interventional vs surgical options using graded recommendations.
- For low-grade disease, **posterolateral fusion (PLF) and interbody fusion techniques** are supported for clinical improvement when surgery is chosen; interbody techniques tend to show higher radiographic fusion rates than PLF alone in cited evidence.
- Surgery vs pure medical management has historically **insufficient comparative evidence** for a strong one-size recommendation—individualize to symptoms, neuro status, and failure of nonoperative care.
- Imaging: standing radiographs for slip grade; CT for pars morphology; MRI for neural compression and disc pathology.

# Key data points

- Population: adults ≥18 with back and/or leg pain related to isthmic spondylolisthesis.
- Common pathoanatomy: bilateral L5 pars defects → L5–S1 anterolisthesis; disc degeneration at the slip level is frequent in adults and linked to symptom progression.
- Grade of recommendation framework: A/B/C/I (insufficient)—several management questions remain Grade I.
- When operating for low-grade isthmic listhesis, fusion is recommended/suggested for long-term clinical improvement (per full NASS document language).
- Decompression alone is generally not preferred for isthmic disease because the primary pathology is the pars defect / segmental instability, not degenerative facet stenosis alone.

# Caveats

- Summary article, not the full technical report/evidentiary tables; verify specific recommendation grades in the complete NASS PDF.
- Evidence base older than 2016 for many RCTs; newer MIS/interbody series not fully reflected.
- Does not address **severe secondary osteoporosis**, teriparatide pre-fusion optimization, or SCS as a bridge—those are adjacent topics.
- Not medical advice; research notes only.
