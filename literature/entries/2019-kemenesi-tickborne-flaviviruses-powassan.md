# Summary

- Comprehensive review of **tick-borne flaviviruses**, centered on **Powassan virus (POWV)** as a rare but severe cause of encephalitis in North America and the Russian Far East.
- Documents increasing US case detection since ~2005 despite still-low absolute numbers historically (<150 documented cases in many tallies since 1958 discovery).
- Covers vector ecology (*Ixodes* spp., including deer tick virus lineage), pathogenesis, diagnostics (serology, PCR, PRNT), and absence of specific antivirals/vaccines for POWV.

# Key data points

- POWV neuroinvasive disease has high rates of permanent neurologic sequelae (~50% range in many series) and mortality often cited around 10%+.
- Clinical illness is typically acute febrile encephalitis/meningitis—not chronic multi-year polyarthralgia with osteoporosis.
- Diagnosis requires specific POWV testing; routine tick-borne panels often omit it.
- Climate, host, and surveillance changes may expand geographic risk, but endemic foci remain uneven.

# Caveats

- Not a Texas coccidioides/histoplasmosis substitute; different ecology.

- Review date 2019; incidence reporting continues to evolve.
