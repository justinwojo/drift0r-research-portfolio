# Summary

- International Working Group consensus on diagnosing hypophosphatasia (HPP) in children and adults using integrated clinical, laboratory, radiographic, and *ALPL* genetic data.
- Emphasizes that diagnosis is often **substantially delayed** in adults with mild/nonspecific presentations.
- **Persistently low ALP for age and sex**—without another identifiable cause—is the biochemical cornerstone; substrates (e.g., PLP) and genetics support confirmation.
- Proposes major and minor diagnostic criteria; practical rule: two major, or one major + two minor, after low ALP is established.

# Key data points

- Adult HPP can mimic osteoporosis (fractures, pain, low BMD) yet is a mineralization disorder—management differs (bisphosphonates relatively problematic; ERT in selected phenotypes).
- Low ALP must be serial and age/sex-adjusted; single “low-normal” values need context.
- Non-HPP causes of low ALP (antiresorptives, hypothyroidism, malnutrition, Zn/Mg deficiency, etc.) must be excluded.
- Complements editorial literature already in corpus (Inoue 2024) with formal criteria structure.

**Score:** 2

**Why:**
- **Matches as differential only:** Young-adult severe low BMD warrants chart-mining **all historical ALP values**.
- **Gatekeeper:** If ALP has been repeatedly mid/high-normal, classic HPP becomes unlikely under these criteria.
- **Mismatches:** Hypercalciuria + *SLC7A9* het, high-normal CTX 616, multi-system non-mineralization features are not core adult HPP.
- **Actionable:** Explicit ALP audit + PLP/*ALPL* only if lows found—high value, low cost.

# Caveats

- Criteria require low ALP first; do not diagnose HPP from BMD alone.
- Prior antiresorptive use lowers ALP and confounds interpretation.
- Not medical advice; specialist metabolic bone input for borderline biochemistries.
