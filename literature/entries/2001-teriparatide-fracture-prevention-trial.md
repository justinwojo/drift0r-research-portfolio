# Summary

- Landmark double-blind RCT of recombinant PTH(1–34) (teriparatide) 20 or 40 µg daily vs placebo in 1,637 postmenopausal women with prior vertebral fractures.
- Demonstrated large reductions in new vertebral and nonvertebral fragility fractures and substantial gains in lumbar spine and femoral neck BMD.
- Established intermittent PTH as the first approved anabolic osteoporosis therapy and is the efficacy backbone for teriparatide use (class discussion) in high-fracture-risk patients.
- Side-effect profile in the paper emphasized nausea/headache; detailed hypercalciuria/stone analyses are expanded in later safety literature (see lit-0156).

# Key data points

- N=1,637 postmenopausal women with prior vertebral fractures; daily SC PTH 20 µg, 40 µg, or placebo.
- New vertebral fractures: 14% placebo vs 5% (20 µg) and 4% (40 µg); RRs ~0.35 and 0.31.
- New nonvertebral fragility fractures: 6% placebo vs 3% each PTH arm (RR ~0.47).
- Lumbar spine BMD: +9 and +13 percentage points more than placebo (20 and 40 µg).
- Femoral neck BMD: +3 and +6 percentage points more than placebo.
- 40 µg increased BMD more but with more side effects; 20 µg became the registered dose.
- Trial stopped early relative to planned duration because of rat osteosarcoma findings (context for black-box warning).

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 3

**Why:** Strongly relevant as the efficacy rationale for discussing bone-building therapy with a clinician when BMD is markedly low and surgical reconstruction is planned, but population is postmenopausal women with primary osteoporosis—not a young man with IH, hypercalciuria, prior stone, and multi-system secondary bone loss.

# Caveats

- Sex/age mismatch; male osteoporosis data come from separate trials (e.g., Orwoll).
- Excluded the exact mineral risks that complicate this case (hypercalciuria/recent stones—see safety reviews).
- Fracture benefit is population-level; individual response and dual stone–bone management remain clinician judgment.
