# Summary

- Structured review of the differential diagnosis and diagnostic workup for secondary osteoporosis.
- Emphasizes pretest probability: men, premenopausal women, very low Z/T-scores, and fragility fractures without classic risk profiles need deeper evaluation.
- Organizes causes by endocrine, GI, renal, hematologic, rheumatologic, and iatrogenic categories with corresponding tests.
- Complements AACE laboratory checklists and Endocrine Society male OP guidelines with differential-depth narrative.

# Key data points

- Initial labs typically: CBC, chemistry (Ca, P, Cr, ALP, albumin), 25-OH vitamin D, TSH, 24-h urine calcium ± creatinine/sodium; add PTH, celiac serology, SPEP/UPEP, testosterone (men), cortisol testing as indicated.
- ALP interpretation dual-use: high may suggest osteomalacia/Paget/liver; **low** flags HPP differential.
- Hypercalciuria is both a stone risk and a reversible bone-loss pathway (thiazide-responsive in selected patients).
- Workup completeness matters before elective instrumentation/fusion.

# Caveats

- Journal audience includes obstetrics/gynecology; principles generalize to men but male-specific evidence is thinner for some drugs.
- Pair with male OP guidelines (Watts 2012) and hypercalciuria literature for stone–anabolic-therapy tradeoffs (clinician decision).
- Not a monogenic genetics paper—pair with EOOP panel literature.
