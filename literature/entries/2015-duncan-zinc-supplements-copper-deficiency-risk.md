# Summary

- Clinical pathology investigation of copper status in patients prescribed therapeutic zinc supplements.
- High-dose zinc can induce copper deficiency; the diagnosis is often missed until hematologic or irreversible neurologic injury occurs.
- Supports systematic monitoring of copper (and clinical vigilance for myeloneuropathy/cytopenias) when zinc is prescribed long-term or at high dose.
- Bridges single-case zinc literature to real-world prescribing practice.

# Key data points

- Aims: assess how often zinc-treated patients develop biochemical copper deficiency and related complications.
- Clinical stakes: anemia, neutropenia, and myeloneuropathy that may not fully reverse.
- Practical implication: paired copper–zinc monitoring and patient education about total zinc load (prescribed + OTC + denture products).
- Diagnostic approach remains serum copper ± ceruloplasmin with zinc level and exposure history—not Wilson algorithms.
