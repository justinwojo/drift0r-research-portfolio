# Summary

- Expert review of teriparatide (PTH 1–34) safety after registration for postmenopausal and male osteoporosis.
- Addresses hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, hyperuricemia, and the black-box osteosarcoma concern from rat toxicology.
- Key clinical message for this case: registration trials excluded recent stones and baseline hypercalciuria; mild on-treatment rises in urine calcium were common, but excess clinical stone events were not clearly demonstrated in those selected populations.
- Advises a different, more careful approach when baseline hypercalciuria or prior stones exist—define/correct metabolic stone risks first; consider imaging for silent nephrocalcinosis; intensify monitoring if teriparatide is still used.

# Key data points

- Pivotal Fracture Prevention Trial: post-dose hypercalcemia (>10.6 mg/dL at 4–6 h) in ~11% on 20 µg/day; consecutive hypercalcemia ~3%; therapy withdrawal for persistent hypercalcemia rare (~1/541).
- Hypercalciuria definitions referenced: ~4 mg/kg/day or absolute ~300 mg/day upper limits commonly used.
- Baseline 24h urine Ca averaged ~165–188 mg/day in registration cohorts; teriparatide raised urine Ca by ~20–30 mg/day on average vs baseline/placebo at 6–12 months.
- Despite excluding recent stones/hypercalciuria, 7% of women and 18% of men still had baseline urine Ca >300 mg/day; clinical urolithiasis incidence was low and not clearly excess vs placebo in reported analyses.
- Sustained hypercalciuria on repeated follow-up ~3% of participants; <1% needed Ca-intake or dose adjustment for hypercalciuria.
- Author practice: in renal hypercalciuria candidates, obtain noncontrast CT looking for silent nephrocalcinosis before starting; manage stones first.

# Caveats

- 2008 expert review; post-marketing stone epidemiology has continued to evolve.
- Registration populations were mostly older postmenopausal women / osteoporotic men without this patient’s multi-system secondary osteoporosis stack.
- “No excess stones in trials” ≠ “safe without monitoring” when baseline urine Ca is already high (333 mg) and urine pH is alkaline.
