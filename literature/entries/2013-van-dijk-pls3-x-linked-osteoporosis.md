# Summary

- Reports pathogenic *PLS3* (plastin-3) variants in five families with **X-linked osteoporosis and osteoporotic fractures**.
- Plastin-3 bundles F-actin; zebrafish models support a bone-regulatory role.
- Additional rare *PLS3* variant (rs140121121) found in families referred for OI type I workup and associated with ~2× fracture risk among elderly heterozygous women—links monogenic and common osteoporosis risk.
- Phenotype is often **nonspecific low-turnover/childhood-onset OP** without classic OI extraskeletal stigmata.

# Key data points

- Hemizygous males: early-onset osteoporosis, low BMD, vertebral and peripheral fractures.
- Heterozygous females: milder/variable penetrance (consistent with X-linked pattern).
- Frequently misclassified as mild OI or idiopathic juvenile/early osteoporosis before genetics.
- Supports including *PLS3* on bone-fragility panels for young men with severe OP.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 3–4

**Why:**
- **Matches:** 38-year-old male with markedly low BMD; monogenic X-linked OP is a high-yield “don’t miss” in young men when secondary factors feel incomplete (patient’s view that historical low T is **insufficient**).
- **Partial:** No documented childhood fracture cascade in the public pack; multi-system non-bone features still need separate explanations.
- **Actionable research note:** *PLS3* sequencing (or multi-gene bone panel including *PLS3*, *WNT1*, *LRP5*, *COL1A1/2*, *ALPL*) remains reasonable even with hypercalciuria and prior hypogonadism present.

# Caveats

- Phenotype overlaps mild OI and idiopathic early OP—clinical diagnosis alone is unreliable.
- Not every male with severe OP has PLS3 disease; panel testing > single-gene guesses.
- Does not address stone/hypercalciuria axis.
