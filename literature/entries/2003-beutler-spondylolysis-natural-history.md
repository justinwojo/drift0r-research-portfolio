# Summary

- Only long-horizon **prospective natural history** study following spondylolysis/spondylolisthesis from childhood onset through **>45 years** of life (continuation of Fredrickson cohort).
- Documents incidence of pars defects, likelihood of slip with **bilateral** vs unilateral defects, and slow adult progression of low-grade listhesis.
- Adult slip progression correlates with **disc degeneration at the slip level**—mechanically consistent with painful adult isthmic disease even when radiographic grade remains low (Meyerding 1).
- Progression rate slows with each decade; no subject reached a 40% slip in this cohort.
- Healing of defects was essentially limited to **unilateral** lesions early in life; bilateral chronic defects persist.

# Key data points

- Spondylolisthesis occurs in a large fraction of patients with **bilateral** spondylolysis; unilateral defects rarely progress to listhesis.
- Average slip progression higher in youth, then decelerates (reported decade-wise decline in progression).
- Concurrent **L5–S1 disc degeneration** is the key adult factor linked to further slip and symptoms.
- Population was not pain-selected—separates structural prevalence from clinical disability.
- Supports counseling that grade 1 isthmic disease can remain radiographically “mild” while disc failure and foraminal stenosis drive severe symptoms.

# Caveats

- Pre-MRI era enrollment; modern disc/root phenotyping limited.
- Does not address osteoporosis-related bone fragility or atraumatic clinical “pop” events.
- Small cohort; generalizability to multi-system illness limited.
- Not medical advice.
