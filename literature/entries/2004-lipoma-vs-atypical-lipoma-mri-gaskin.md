# Summary

- Consecutive series of **126 fatty soft-tissue masses** evaluated with MRI to separate simple lipomas / lipoma variants from well-differentiated liposarcoma (atypical lipoma).
- Shows MRI is **highly specific** for simple lipoma when imaging criteria are classic, and sensitive for suspicious fatty tumors needing histology.

# Key data points

- MRI was **100% specific** for simple lipoma when a grossly fatty mass had few/no thin septa and minimal/no enhancement or high-T2 nonfat foci.
- Features favoring atypical lipomatous tumor / WDL: thick/nodular septa (often >2 mm), nonadipose nodular components, prominent enhancement, larger/deep lesions.
- MRI is highly sensitive for detecting well-differentiated liposarcoma among fatty masses but positive predictive value is limited by benign lipoma variants with complex features.
- Practical rule: pure fat-signal lesion with thin septa → imaging diagnosis of lipoma; complex fatty mass → biopsy/excision pathway.

# Caveats

- Study enrolls **visible fatty masses**, not imaging-occult nodules.
- Prior lower-back lipoma excision is separate anatomic site/history.
- Very small non-fatty nodules (granuloma, fibrosis, thickened retinaculum, scar) are outside scope.
