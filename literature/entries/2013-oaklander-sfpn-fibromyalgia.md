# Summary

- Tests whether some patients labeled with fibromyalgia actually have small-fiber polyneuropathy (SFPN), an objective peripheral-nerve disease.
- Compared 27 FM patients (2010 ACR criteria + physician diagnosis) with 30 matched controls using symptom scales, exam scores, distal-leg skin biopsy, and autonomic function testing.
- 41% of FM skin biopsies vs 3% of controls met diagnostic criteria for SFPN; neuropathy exam scores were significantly higher in FM.
- Among SFPN-positive FM subjects, blood work suggested possible dysimmune, hepatitis C, or genetic contributors—not diabetes (all GTTs normal).

# Key data points

- SFPN defined by dysfunction/degeneration of small-fiber neurons; has testable biomarkers (PGP9.5 skin biopsy, AFT).
- Diagnostic skin-biopsy SFPN: 41% FM vs 3% controls (P ≤ 0.001 range for key comparisons).
- Abnormal AFT rates similar between groups → FM-associated SFPN interpreted as primarily somatic in this sample.
- Implies a subset of “fibromyalgia” is misclassified organic peripheral neuropathy with treatable causes in some cases.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4

**Why:** High conceptual relevance. Patient had severe burning neuropathic pain (beriberi era), residual allodynia, widespread joint-region pain without inflammatory arthritis, and was at risk of “functional/centralized pain” labeling despite organic disease (documented thiamine deficiency neuropathy). SFPN workup is a concrete “don’t miss” test category when FM is proposed. Not a perfect match: beriberi is large- and small-fiber capable and was treatment-responsive; current dominant complaint is joint fragility more than classic stocking-glove burning.

# Caveats

- Small single-center cohort; not all subsequent studies find the same proportion of SFPN in FM.
- Skin biopsy not known to have been done in this case (verify clinically).
- SFPN explains pain quality and multi-symptom load better than joint mechanical failure/osteoporosis.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
