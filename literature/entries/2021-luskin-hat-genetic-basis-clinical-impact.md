# Summary

- Concise clinical review: HαT is common (**~4–6%**), defined by excess *TPSAB1* α copies, with **sBT often >8 ng/mL**.
- Elevated BST in HαT largely reflects increased **pro-α-tryptase synthesis/secretion**, not ongoing mast-cell activation.
- Only a subset (~one-third in some estimates) of carriers have associated multisystem symptoms; HαT is enriched in SM and idiopathic anaphylaxis and **modifies anaphylaxis severity**.
- Emphasizes accurate genotyping combined with sBT to help **risk-stratify who needs bone marrow evaluation**.

# Key data points

- Diagnostic confirmation: elevated BST + *TPSAB1* CNV testing.
- HαT ≠ SM; co-occurrence is possible and increases mediator-symptom burden when both present.
- Phenotype heterogeneity may involve α/β heterotetramers and co-inherited modifiers.

**Why:** Matches confirmed HαT, tryptase 14.5–17.7 (typical trait range), KIT negative, non-anaphylactic phenotype. Directly relevant to the deferred marrow biopsy decision: HαT explains much of the tryptase signal, lowering pre-test probability of clonal SM *relative to an unexplained BST of the same magnitude*—while not eliminating clinical judgment around markedly low BMD for age.

# Caveats

- Review article synthesizing heterogeneous cohorts.
- Does not provide a rigid absolute tryptase cutoff that rules SM out (especially with atypical bone disease).
- Paywalled full text.
