# Summary

- Review of teriparatide as the primary osteoanabolic option approved for men with osteoporosis at high fracture risk.
- Summarizes Kurland et al. idiopathic male osteoporosis RCT and the larger Orwoll et al. pivotal trial (idiopathic or hypogonadal).
- Notes trial termination of Orwoll study after ~11 months due to rat osteosarcoma toxicology; human osteosarcoma risk considered very low.
- BMD gains at spine and hip in men approximate those in women; fracture data in men are thinner but supportive in follow-up analyses.
- Discusses glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis indications and sequential/combination strategies (e.g., with bisphosphonates—often attenuating anabolic effect if co-started).
- Practical framing for when to choose anabolic vs antiresorptive therapy in men.
- Reinforces that gonadal status does not preclude teriparatide response.
- Useful clinical companion to the primary RCTs (lit-0007, lit-0008).

# Key data points

- Kurland: ~13.5% spine BMD gain over 18 months in idiopathic men.
- Orwoll: +5.9% spine / +1.5% FN at 20 µg over ~11 months.
- Response independent of hypogonadism in Orwoll analysis.
- Antiresorptive co-therapy can blunt teriparatide BMD gains if started together.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Directly supports bone-building therapy discussion (clinician decision only) for severe male osteoporosis.
- Covers both idiopathic and hypogonadal pathways present in the case narrative.
- High fracture-risk framing fits extreme T-scores and progressive hip loss (−4.2%/yr).
- Advises thoughtful sequencing if antiresorptives are later considered after anabolic course.

# Caveats

- Review (secondary literature); relies on small male fracture datasets.
- 2011—predates abaloparatide/romosozumab era and some sequential-therapy refinements.
- Does not resolve young-adult long-term safety questions in detail.
