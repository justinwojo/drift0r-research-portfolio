# Summary

- ISCD Official Positions define how DXA should be acquired, interpreted, and reported in adults, including a critical section for **males younger than 50** and premenopausal women.
- In that population: **Z-scores, not T-scores, are preferred**; Z ≤ −2.0 is “below the expected range for age”; Z > −2.0 is “within the expected range for age.”
- **Osteoporosis cannot be diagnosed in men under age 50 on the basis of BMD alone**—clinical fractures and secondary causes drive the diagnosis.
- WHO T-score ≤ −2.5 osteoporosis definition applies to postmenopausal women and men ≥50 (femoral neck reference standard NHANES III young white female).

# Key data points

- Men <50 / premenopausal women: report Z-scores; language “below expected range for age” if Z ≤ −2.0.
- Diagnosis of osteoporosis in young adults requires more than densitometry (fragility fracture and/or significant secondary osteoporosis).
- Diagnostic sites: lumbar spine, total hip, femoral neck; 33% radius in specific situations.
- Follow-up DXA requires same device/software when possible; use least significant change (LSC) to interpret real change (e.g., −4.2%/yr hip must exceed LSC).
- Pediatric positions are separate; transitional young adults still use adult young-male rules once epiphyses closed.

**Why:** Case is a ~38-year-old man with markedly low BMD often discussed via T-scores. ISCD positions are mandatory context: extreme Z-equivalent bone deficit + clinical fragility still support a clinical osteoporosis diagnosis, but the label must be framed as secondary/young-adult disease—not WHO postmenopausal T-score logic alone. Also frames same-scanner hip loss significance.

# Caveats

- Positions brochure is consensus densitometry guidance, not a pharmacologic treatment guideline.
- Very low BMD in a young man almost always implies secondary disease—pair with Watts 2012 and AACE secondary panels.
- T-scores may still appear on printouts; analysts must not treat them as WHO diagnostic criteria in isolation under age 50.
