# Summary

- Reviews biomarkers used to diagnose **mast cell activation**, contrasting acute/event tryptase rises with emerging **urinary mast cell mediator** panels.
- Core urinary markers: **N-methylhistamine**, **leukotriene E4 (LTE4)**, and **2,3-dinor-11β-prostaglandin F2α**.
- Emphasizes that basal tryptase elevation can reflect **HαT** and therefore does not by itself prove activation; paired baseline vs symptomatic measurements improve interpretation.
- Urinary testing is often more practical (spot/random or timed collections) than catching an acute serum tryptase peak.

# Key data points

- Best-accepted activation biomarker remains an **adequate rise in serum tryptase** during symptoms vs personal baseline.
- Urinary mediators support diagnosis when tryptase timing is missed or inconclusive.
- Normal mediator panels substantially lower confidence in ongoing MCAS-type activation.

# Caveats

- Negative urine mediators do not fully exclude **focal marrow disease** presenting as osteoporosis without systemic mediator storms (low probability but not zero).
- Collection timing, diet, medications, and lab reference ranges affect interpretation.
- Not a bone-disease paper.
