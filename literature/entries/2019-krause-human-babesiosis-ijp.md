# Summary

- Comprehensive 2019 invited review of human babesiosis by Peter Krause covering global species distribution, transmission, clinical disease, laboratory diagnosis, and therapy.
- Updates the NEJM-era narrative with broader geographic detail and modern treatment framing (atovaquone + azithromycin preferred).
- Explicitly notes **persistent asymptomatic parasitemia** after acute disease and **rare relapse in previously healthy adults**, vs prolonged relapsing disease in highly immunocompromised patients.

# Key data points

- Common symptoms: intermittent fever, fatigue, chills, sweats, anorexia, headache, myalgia.
- Less common: arthralgia, emotional lability, neck stiffness, nausea, abdominal pain, **shortness of breath / non-productive cough**, photophobia, weight loss.
- Severe disease: elderly, asplenic, HIV, malignancy, immunosuppressants, comorbidities.
- Diagnostics: smear, PCR, serology with phase-appropriate interpretation.
- Therapy usually 7–10 days; immunocompromised may need prolonged therapy; exchange transfusion for select severe cases.
- Relapse in healthy adults uncommon but reported up to many months after initial illness; prolonged relapsing disease mainly immunocompromised.
