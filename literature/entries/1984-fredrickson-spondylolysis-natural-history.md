# Summary

- Foundational prospective study of the **natural history of spondylolysis and spondylolisthesis**, following a large childhood cohort with serial radiographs.
- Established that pars defects typically arise in **early childhood/adolescence** (stress/fatigue mechanism), often at **L5**, and may be asymptomatic for years.
- **Bilateral** defects predispose to isthmic listhesis; many defects persist lifelong rather than heal.
- Provides the structural background for adult presentations—including sudden clinical “events” on **chronic non-united pars**—without requiring high-energy trauma.
- Later extended by Beutler et al. (2003) to midlife (see lit-0166).

# Key data points

- Incidence of spondylolysis ~4–6% range by skeletal maturity in classic population data (age-dependent rise from early childhood).
- L5 predominance; association with spina bifida occulta/dysplastic posterior elements in some subjects.
- Unilateral lesions more likely to heal; bilateral chronic defects remain and can permit anterior translation of L5 on S1.
- Many radiographic defects are painless—pain drivers in adults often include disc degeneration, instability micro-motion, and root compression rather than grade alone.
- Frames “atraumatic pop” while rising from a chair as a plausible **acute clinical manifestation** of chronic bilateral pars insufficiency ± disc failure, especially when bone quality is poor—not necessarily a brand-new high-energy fracture in virgin bone.

# Caveats

- 1984 imaging (plain radiographs/obliques); CT/MRI phenotypes of disc/root disease not available.
- Does not specifically study osteoporotic insufficiency fractures of the pars in adults.
- Population epidemiology ≠ individual multi-system illness.
