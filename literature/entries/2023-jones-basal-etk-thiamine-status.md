# Summary

- Protocol paper arguing that **ETKAC alone** can be confounded when erythrocyte transketolase enzyme protein availability is limited, so **basal ETK activity** (per gram hemoglobin) should be reported as a complementary functional biomarker.
- Provides detailed method: microplate path length, NADH molar absorption coefficient, hemolysate hemoglobin measurement—aimed at inter-lab harmonization.
- Demonstrates application with comparative samples (women in The Gambia and UK).
- Reinforces the broader principle that **functional assays (ETK/ETKAC) and whole-blood ThDP** are preferred status tools; isolated serum/plasma free thiamine can miss or misrank deficiency—especially when clinical beriberi is present.

# Key data points

- ETKAC = activity coefficient after in vitro thiamine diphosphate stimulation (higher stimulation → greater functional deficiency).
- Basal ETK activity: absolute enzyme activity normalized to hemoglobin—captures low enzyme abundance scenarios where ETKAC interpretation fails.
- Analytical detail: path-length calculation in microplates; NADH extinction coefficient specific to assay conditions.
- Use-case: improve replication and clinical interpretability of functional thiamine assessment.
- Complements—not replaces—LC-MS/MS whole-blood thiamine diphosphate (ThDP) quantification used in many modern labs.

**Score:** 4

**Why:**
- Case hinged on low serum B1 (7 nmol/L; ref ~8–30) plus dramatic clinical response; later whole-blood value also obtained—exactly the serum-vs-functional/status assay interpretation problem.
- Supports arguing that functional status (ETK/ETKAC or erythrocyte/whole-blood ThDP) is more definitive than serum free thiamine alone, and that “near-normal serum” would not exclude deficiency if clinical and functional data conflict.
- Strengthens lab literacy for recurrence monitoring around surgery/illness.

# Caveats

- Methods/protocol focus—not a neuropathy treatment trial.
- No universal clinical cutoffs exported for all hospital labs.
- Alcohol-use and critical-illness populations differ from nonalcoholic multi-system outpatients.
- Not medical advice.
