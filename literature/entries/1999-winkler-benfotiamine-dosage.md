# Summary

- Six-week open clinical trial comparing high- and medium-dose benfotiamine–vitamin B combinations versus benfotiamine monotherapy in painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy.
- 36 patients (ages 40–70) with acceptable glycemic control (HbA1c < 8.0%) randomized into three groups of 12.
- Outcomes: pain (modified visual analogue scale), vibration sensation, and current perception threshold at 5/250/2000 Hz on the peroneal nerve.
- Greatest improvement in the highest-dose benfotiamine arm; benefits often evident by week 3.

# Key data points

- High-dose arm: 4×2 capsules/day ≈ 320 mg benfotiamine/day (Milgamma-N combination).
- Medium-dose combination: 3×1 capsules/day; monotherapy: 150 mg benfotiamine/day.
- Significant improvement in most neuropathy parameters by week 3 (p < 0.01).
- High-dose group significantly better than lower-dose groups (p < 0.01 and p < 0.05).
- Metabolic control unchanged during study.

**Score:** 3

# Caveats

- Small sample; open-label design risks placebo and rater bias.
- Combination products confound pure benfotiamine attribution in two arms.
- Short 6-week horizon vs multi-month axonal recovery.
