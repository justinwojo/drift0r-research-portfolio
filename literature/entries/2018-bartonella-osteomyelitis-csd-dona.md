# Summary

- Case of disseminated cat-scratch disease with **skull osteomyelitis** plus literature review of **51 immunocompetent pediatric** Bartonella osteomyelitis cases.
- Consolidates presentation (fever, osteoarticular pain, lymphadenopathy), preferred diagnostics (serology + MRI), therapy variability, and generally excellent prognosis.
- Argues Bartonella should be considered when lymphadenitis coexists with bone pain/limitation.

# Key data points

- Mean age ~7.8 y; fever 84%, prolonged fever 65%, osteoarticular pain 88%, lymphadenopathy ~65%.
- Bone sites: **vertebral body most common (~52%)**; multifocal disease possible; periosteal thickening/enhancement described on MRI.
- Preferred micro diagnosis: **serology**; MRI preferred for bone/soft-tissue extent; invasive bone biopsy often avoided given good prognosis.
- Median antibiotic duration ~23 days in treated patients; ~12.5% received no antimicrobials yet recovered—treatment remains controversial.
- Authors emphasize investigating bone pain during CSD and avoiding unnecessary invasive bone sampling when diagnosis is otherwise secure.

# Caveats

- Pediatric CSD osteomyelitis ≠ proven model for adult chronic Bartonella MSK illness.
- Patient’s commercial labs did not confirm Bartonella; specialty LDT only—evidence quality differs from serology-defined CSD series.
- Case lacks fever-dominant acute CSD phenotype in recent narrative.
