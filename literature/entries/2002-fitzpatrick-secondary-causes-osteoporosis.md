# Summary

- Comprehensive review of secondary osteoporosis spanning endocrine, GI, renal, marrow, drug-induced, and genetic causes.
- States that 20–30% of postmenopausal women and more than 50% of men with osteoporosis have a secondary cause in some studies.
- Catalogues hypogonadism, hypercalciuria, hyperparathyroidism, celiac/malabsorption, glucocorticoids, and many less common entities.
- Emphasizes that premenopausal women and younger men with low BMD or fractures especially need secondary workup.
- Distinguishes conditions that reduce peak bone mass versus accelerate bone loss.
- Provides a practical framework still used in modern secondary-cause checklists.
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria listed among important secondary contributors to bone loss.
- Supports not stopping workup after “normal calcium/PTH/vitamin D.”

# Key data points

- Secondary cause prevalence substantially higher in men than typical postmenopausal primary osteoporosis.
- Broad differential: endocrine, GI, marrow, drugs, immobilization, idiopathic hypercalciuria.
- Workup indicated for younger adults with osteopenia/osteoporosis or fragility fracture.
- Multiple concurrent secondary factors may coexist.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Male markedly low BMD for age at age 38 is exactly the population where secondary causes dominate.
- Hypercalciuria is explicitly on the differential—matches patient anchor.
- Historical hypogonadism is a classic Fitzpatrick-list endocrine cause.
- Normal Ca/PTH/VitD does not exclude secondary disease (e.g., hypercalciuria, past hypogonadism, other occult causes).

# Caveats

- 2002; some diagnostic tests and prevalence estimates have been refined.
- Not a prospective cohort; synthesis of literature of its era.
- Does not provide modern BTM reference-marker guidance (see Vasikaran lit-0012).
