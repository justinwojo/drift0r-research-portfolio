# Summary

- **PROCESS** multicenter RCT: spinal cord stimulation **plus** conventional medical management (CMM) vs CMM alone in **failed back surgery syndrome** with neuropathic leg pain (± back pain).
- Primary efficacy finding: SCS provided **superior pain relief**, health-related quality of life, and functional capacity vs CMM alone at 6 months (with continued follow-up reporting).
- Established Level I support for SCS as a standard option when chronic neuropathic pain persists after (or in the pathway of) lumbar surgery/chronic management.
- Complications typical of implantable neuromodulation (hardware issues, infection, lead problems) occurred; therapy is not risk-free.
- Patient selection and successful trial stimulation historically used as gatekeepers to permanent implant.

# Key data points

- Design: prospective, randomized, controlled, multicenter; ~100 patients with FBSS neuropathic pain.
- Primary/secondary outcomes: ≥50% pain relief rates, QOL, function, opioid use trajectories (study-defined endpoints).
- SCS + CMM outperformed CMM alone on key pain and function metrics at primary analysis horizon.
- Crossover allowed in longer-term analyses (common in neuromodulation trials)—interpret ITT carefully.
- Population is **post-surgical FBSS**, not unoperated isthmic spondylolisthesis; mechanism of use (neuropathic leg ± axial pain) is still clinically adjacent.

# Caveats

- Conventional paresthesia-based SCS technology of mid-2000s.
- FBSS definition assumes prior spine surgery; Drift0r has **not** completed fusion (SCS as bridge/alternative).
- Does not address multi-joint systemic “crash” weeks after implant.
- Industry sponsorship common in neuromodulation RCTs—assess bias risk.
