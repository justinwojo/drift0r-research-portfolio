# Summary

- Classic Whipple disease is a rare chronic multi-system infection by *Tropheryma whipplei* (Actinobacteria), historically fatal untreated.
- Classic tetrad pattern: chronic diarrhea/malabsorption, weight loss, migratory seronegative arthralgia/arthritis (often preceding GI disease by years), and later cardiac/neurologic involvement.
- Diagnosis evolved from PAS-positive macrophages in duodenal biopsy to **PCR** (tissue, fluids), immunohistochemistry, and culture in reference labs.
- Treatment is prolonged antibiotics (historical penicillin/streptomycin induction then TMP-SMX maintenance; doxycycline + hydroxychloroquine strategies also used in modern practice—see specialty guidance); CNS disease needs CNS-penetrant regimens.

# Key data points

- Arthritis/arthralgia is often the **first** clinical phase and can dominate for years without diarrhea—key “don’t miss” for seronegative MSK presentations.
- Immune dysregulation (not simple promiscuous exposure) underlies who develops classic disease despite environmental ubiquity of the organism.
- Relapse risk, especially neurologic, mandates long therapy and follow-up.
- Localized *T. whipplei* endocarditis, encephalitis, and asymptomatic carriage expand the clinical spectrum beyond textbook Whipple.
