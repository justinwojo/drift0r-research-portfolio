# Summary

- Evaluated adults with psychosis vs non-psychotic controls for Bartonella spp. DNA using multiple PCR modalities (qPCR, dPCR, ddPCR).
- Higher proportion of adults with psychosis had bloodstream Bartonella DNA (43.2%) than controls (14.3%, p=0.021).
- Species identified in subset: B. henselae most common, also B. vinsonii subsp. berkhoffii, B. quintana, B. alsatica, B. rochalimae; some co-infections.
- Frames prior reports linking Bartonella bacteremia to neuropsychiatric symptoms including PANS and schizophrenia.

# Key data points

- 116 patients and controls evaluated; 43.2% psychosis vs 14.3% controls DNA-positive.
- Species resolved for 18/31 bacteremic participants; B. henselae 11/18.
- Coinfection with different Bartonella spp. in 3 psychosis patients.
- Does not prove Bartonella causes psychosis or that antibiotics treat psychosis.

# Caveats

- Association study; reverse causation, confounders, and lab specificity questions remain.
- Patient anchors are MSK/periosteal/Ig subclass and specialty serology—not psychosis primary diagnosis.
- Commercial molecular methods differ from patient-reported PCR/FISH negative results.
- Authors include Galaxy Diagnostics leadership (COI).
