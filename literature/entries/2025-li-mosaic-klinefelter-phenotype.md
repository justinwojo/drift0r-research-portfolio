# Summary

- Mosaic Klinefelter syndrome (e.g., 46,XY/47,XXY and related mosaics) is a less common KS subtype that often lacks the full classic phenotype.
- Because clinical signs can be mild or occult, mosaic KS is easily missed unless karyotype/FISH is performed for infertility or hypogonadism workups.
- Study evaluates prevalence, sperm parameters, hormones, and genetic findings among mosaic KS patients in a southern China clinical series.
- Reinforces that **normal-appearing virilization does not exclude mosaicism**, and that fertility outcomes can differ from non-mosaic 47,XXY.

# Key data points

- Mosaic KS ≈10% of KS overall in classic epidemiologic estimates; true rates depend on ascertainment and cytogenetic resolution.
- Phenotype often milder than non-mosaic 47,XXY: larger mean testicular volumes, more variable T/gonadotropin patterns, occasionally preserved spermatogenesis sufficient for ejaculated sperm or easier TESE recovery.
- Standard 20–30 cell karyotype may miss very low-level mosaicism; FISH or higher cell counts increase sensitivity when suspicion remains.
- Diagnostic path still starts with clinical + endocrine context, not shotgun karyotyping of every low-T man.

# Caveats

- Regional single-network series; prevalence not globally generalizable.
- “Milder” is statistical—some mosaic individuals are still classically affected.
- 2025 paper; long-term outcome literature still thinner than for non-mosaic KS.
