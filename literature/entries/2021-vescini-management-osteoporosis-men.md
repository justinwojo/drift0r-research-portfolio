# Summary

- Contemporary narrative review of epidemiology, pathophysiology, diagnosis, and treatment of osteoporosis in men.
- Reiterates higher secondary-cause burden in men and need for structured laboratory evaluation.
- Discusses densitometric diagnosis controversies and alignment with major society guidelines.
- Summarizes lifestyle measures and pharmacologic options: bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide/anabolics.
- Addresses hypogonadism, glucocorticoids, and other endocrine contributors.
- Notes underdiagnosis/undertreatment and higher post-fracture mortality in men.
- Integrates Italian/European and international guideline perspectives.
- Useful 2020s update bridging older Endocrine Society CPG and newer anabolic practice.

# Key data points

- Secondary osteoporosis frequent in men (often cited up to ~50–60%).
- Treatment goal: fracture risk reduction; BMD is intermediate endpoint.
- Anabolic agents preferred in very high-risk scenarios in broader osteoporosis algorithms.
- Comprehensive secondary workup remains standard of care.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Supports full secondary evaluation in a young man with extreme BMD loss.
- Treatment section informs options after drivers (hypercalciuria, past hypogonadism) are mapped.
- Aligns with multi-factor pathogenesis rather than single-cause models.
- Reinforces that male osteoporosis management is evidence-informed but still extrapolative for some agents.

# Caveats

- Narrative review; variable depth on young men <50.
- Not a formal clinical practice guideline.
- Fracture RCTs in men remain fewer than in women.
