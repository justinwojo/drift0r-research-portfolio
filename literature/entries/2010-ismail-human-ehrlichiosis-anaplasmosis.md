# Summary

- Reviews microbiology, epidemiology, clinical features, pathogenesis, diagnosis, and treatment of **human monocytic ehrlichiosis (HME)** and **human granulocytic anaplasmosis (HGA)**.
- Presents both as **acute febrile tick-borne diseases** that can be life-threatening, with cytopenias, hepatitis, and occasionally severe multi-organ failure.
- Laboratory diagnosis: morulae on blood smear (insensitive), PCR in acute illness, and paired serology; doxycycline is the treatment of choice.

# Key data points

- *Ehrlichia chaffeensis* (Lone Star tick, south-central/southeastern US—**Texas-relevant**) vs *Anaplasma phagocytophilum* (*Ixodes*, Northeast/Upper Midwest/Pacific Northwest pattern).
- Typical course is acute days-to-weeks, not years of culture-negative polyarthralgia with normal inflammatory markers.
- **Chronic infection claims** in immunocompetent patients remain poorly supported in mainstream literature; persistent detection is mainly discussed in immunosuppressed or animal models/rare case contexts.
- Coinfection with other tick-borne pathogens can occur where vectors overlap.

- Does not adjudicate every specialty-lab “chronic” label.
