# Summary

- Multimodality review of **IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD)** imaging across pancreatic and extrapancreatic sites, including soft tissue, with emphasis on **functional imaging (FDG PET/CT)** and common mimics.
- Provides pattern recognition for mass-forming fibroinflammatory lesions and guidance on when imaging supports vs challenges an IgG4-RD diagnosis.

# Key data points

- IgG4-RD is a multiorgan fibroinflammatory disease; classic targets include pancreas, salivary/lacrimal glands, biliary tree, retroperitoneum/aorta, kidneys, nodes, and less often soft tissue.
- Lesions often appear as infiltrative or mass-like soft tissue with enhancement; MRI may show T2-hypointense fibrotic components; FDG avidity is common in active disease.
- **Lymphadenopathy** is frequent; soft-tissue/subcutaneous or intramuscular masses are uncommon but reported and can mimic sarcoma.
- Imaging alone is not diagnostic—needs clinicopathologic correlation (IgG4+ plasma cells, storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis) and exclusion of mimics (lymphoma, multicentric Castleman, infection, other autoimmune).
- Serum IgG4 elevation is supportive but neither sensitive nor specific; imaging distribution helps phenotype classification.

- Imaging-negative palpable nodules are a poor match for mass-forming IgG4 soft-tissue disease (which is usually visible).
- Mild periosteal edema is not a classic IgG4-RD hallmark.
