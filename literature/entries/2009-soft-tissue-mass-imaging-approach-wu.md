# Summary

- Systematic approach to imaging **soft-tissue tumors and tumorlike lesions**, emphasizing modality selection (US first for many superficial lumps; MRI for characterization/extent).
- Reviews features that allow confident benign diagnoses versus indeterminate lesions requiring biopsy.
- Contextualizes the common clinical problem of a **palpable abnormality** and what imaging is expected to show.

# Key data points

- Superficial palpable masses are often first evaluated with **targeted ultrasound**; MRI is preferred for deep, large, or indeterminate lesions.
- Many “masses” are tumorlike: ganglion, bursitis, tenosynovitis, hematoma, accessory muscle, scar, granuloma, rheumatoid nodule, etc.
- Lesions with classic imaging signatures (e.g., simple lipoma, ganglion) can be diagnosed noninvasively; indeterminate solid lesions need tissue.
- If high-quality US/MRI are truly negative, differential shifts toward **very small, isoechoic/isointense, flat, or non-mass-like** processes (focal tendon thickening, panniculitis, neuropathic perception, transient nodules) rather than occult large neoplasm.
- Clinical–imaging discordance should prompt re-review of the imaged site/marker placement rather than assuming “MRI always sees everything.”

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 4

**Why:** Core methods paper for the signature problem of **~½ dozen palpable left forearm tendon-area lumps that remain imaging-negative on MRI/US/CT**. Supports that missing a large neoplasm after multi-modality imaging is unlikely, and that non-mass-like or tiny lesion biology (or site mismatch) is more plausible.

# Caveats

- Review cannot identify the specific histology of imaging-occult nodules.
- Does not evaluate systemic drivers (IgG4, Bartonella, migratory arthralgia).
- Surgeon refusal of exploratory biopsy in imaging-negative anatomy is a clinical risk judgment outside the paper’s scope.
