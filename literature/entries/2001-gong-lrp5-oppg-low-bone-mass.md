# Summary

- Positional cloning study identifying *LRP5* as the gene for autosomal recessive **osteoporosis-pseudoglioma syndrome (OPPG)**—severe childhood osteoporosis plus vitreoretinal pathology.
- Establishes LRP5 as a core Wnt co-receptor required for normal bone accrual.
- Obligate heterozygous carriers (parents of affected children) show **reduced bone mass**, foreshadowing later reports of heterozygous LRP5-related primary osteoporosis without eye disease.
- Histology: deficient trabecular volume with relatively normal surface osteoblast/osteoclast appearance—formation/accrual defect rather than pure high-turnover destruction.

# Key data points

- OPPG: very low BMD, childhood fractures/deformation, ocular involvement ranging from mild PHPV to phthisis bulbi.
- No primary collagen-synthesis defect distinguishing OPPG from classic OI.
- Positions LRP5 on the low-bone-mass end of a allelic spectrum later completed by high-bone-mass gain-of-function alleles.
- Mechanistic anchor for sclerostin/DKK1-pathway therapeutics (romosozumab lineage).

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 2–3

**Why:**
- **Matches (spectrum concept):** Extreme young-adult low BMD invites *LRP5* (and other Wnt-pathway genes) on a bone-fragility panel; heterozygous LOF can present as “idiopathic” early osteoporosis without blindness.
- **Mismatches as classic OPPG:** No reported congenital/infancy visual failure; multi-system non-skeletal syndrome features dominate the case narrative.
- **Research value:** Supports interpreting LRP5 low-mass alleles as plausible monogenic **contributors**, not as an explanation for hypercalciuria/*SLC7A9*/infection/mast-cell axes.

# Caveats

- Biallelic OPPG is clinically obvious in childhood; adult differential is mainly heterozygous LRP5 primary OP (see later Korvala/Collet literature).
- Eye history and exam still useful when *LRP5* variants are found.
- Paywalled full text for some readers; abstract + citation record remain reliable.
