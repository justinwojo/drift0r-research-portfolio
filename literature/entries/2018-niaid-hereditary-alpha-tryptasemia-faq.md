# Summary

- Hereditary alpha-tryptasemia (HαT) is a common **autosomal-dominant genetic trait** from extra germline *TPSAB1* copies encoding α-tryptase—not a rare Mendelian “disease” in the classical sense.
- Defines elevated basal serum tryptase with variable associated symptoms; many carriers are oligosymptomatic.
- NIAID frames HαT as a **biochemical/genetic trait** that can modify anaphylaxis risk and multisystem complaints without equating to clonal mastocytosis or MCAS by itself.
- Diagnosis requires tryptase measurement plus specialized copy-number genotyping (e.g., ddPCR)—standard sequencing panels often miss CNV.

# Key data points

- Population frequency ~4–6% in studied cohorts; accounts for a large share of unexplained basal tryptase elevation.
- Gene-dose relationship: more α-encoding *TPSAB1* copies → higher basal tryptase (and often more symptoms in early family studies).
- Management is symptom-directed; no trait-specific “cure.”
- Distinguishes elevated tryptase from degranulation syndromes and from WHO systemic mastocytosis criteria workups.
