# Summary

- HαT is an autosomal-dominant trait from increased germline *TPSAB1* copies encoding α-tryptase.
- Defines elevated basal serum tryptase with variable multisystem complaints; many carriers are oligosymptomatic.
- Basal tryptase and symptom severity show a **gene-dose** relationship with α-encoding *TPSAB1* copy number.
- Clarifies that elevated tryptase in HαT reflects increased pro-tryptase synthesis/secretion, not automatically mast-cell degranulation disease.

# Key data points

- Genetic basis: extra α-tryptase-encoding *TPSAB1* copies on one allele (common copy-number variant trait).
- Clinical associations reported in early series: cutaneous, GI, autonomic, connective-tissue, and anaphylaxis-modifying features—later refined by larger studies.
- Gene-dose: more α copies → higher basal tryptase and often more symptoms.
- Diagnostic approach: tryptase level + genotyping (ddPCR preferred for absolute copy number).
- Management is symptom-directed; no disease-specific cure for the trait itself.

# Caveats

- HαT is a *modifier*/trait, not a unifying diagnosis for markedly low BMD or beriberi.
- Symptom associations from early family studies have been only partly replicated; avoid over-attributing every symptom to HαT.
- Does not replace evaluation for clonal mastocytosis when clinically indicated.
- Not medical advice.
