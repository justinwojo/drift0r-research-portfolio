# Summary

- Combines a literature review of fluoroquinolone (FQ)–related **nightmares** with active-comparator restricted **FAERS** disproportionality analyses (2004Q1–2023Q4).
- Case reports/trials suggest nightmares as an under-recognized FQ neuropsychiatric effect; trial prevalence estimates ranged roughly **0.01–8%** where reported.
- Versus azithromycin and TMP-SMX, ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin/moxifloxacin showed **~6- to 10-fold** higher reporting odds for the MedDRA term “nightmare,” robust across frequentist/Bayesian metrics and adjusted models.
- Frames antibiotics—not only psychotropics—as plausible contributors to nightmare-dense sleep complaints during infection treatment courses.

# Key data points

- FQs analyzed: ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin.
- Comparators: azithromycin; trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole.
- ROR vs azithromycin ≈ 6.18 (95% CI 4.14–9.23); vs TMP-SMX ≈ 10.38 (95% CI 4.92–21.89).
- Literature arm: 7 studies identified with nightmare data.
- Reports often consumer-driven—typical FAERS limitation.
- Does not include PSG/REM architecture endpoints; mechanism inferred via known FQ GABA/CNS effects.

# Caveats

- Spontaneous reporting bias; stimulated reporting; confounding by indication/infection.
- Nightmares ≠ full sleep-architecture (N3/REM) mapping.
- Limited primary literature; many signals from case reports.
- Not medical advice; does not recommend stopping indicated antibiotics.
