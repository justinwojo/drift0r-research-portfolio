# Summary

- Narrative review emphasizing that men fracture later than women but have roughly twice the 1-year fatality after hip fracture.
- High-risk groups: prior fragility fracture, oral glucocorticoids, androgen deprivation therapy for prostate cancer.
- Beyond high-risk groups, many secondary causes and risk factors require systematic history, exam, limited labs, and DXA of spine and hip.
- Secondary osteoporosis is common in men and must be sought before labeling disease “primary.”
- Discusses evolving normative DXA databases (single female reference debate) and implications for T-score interpretation.
- Treatment choices for men largely extrapolate fracture reduction from women; approved agents include bisphosphonates, denosumab, teriparatide.
- Underdiagnosis and undertreatment of male osteoporosis remain major care gaps.
- Useful bridge between Endocrine Society guideline (lit-0001) and clinical secondary-cause workups.

# Key data points

- Men fracture ~10 years later than women on average.
- Post-hip-fracture mortality substantially higher in men than women.
- Secondary causes and medications (GC, ADT, PPI, SSRIs, enzyme-inducing AEDs, etc.) are frequent contributors.
- DXA of spine and hip is the core densitometric tool.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Frames young/middle-aged male severe bone loss as a secondary-first diagnostic problem.
- Aligns with multi-system search (endocrine, GI, renal calcium wasting, meds).
- Supports pharmacologic consideration once secondary contributors are mapped.
- Reinforces that fracture history + extreme low BMD warrant full evaluation even under age 50.

# Caveats

- Narrative (not systematic) review; evidence grading limited.
- Pre-dates some newer anabolics and densitometry refinements.
- Less detail on idiopathic osteoporosis histomorphometry or young-adult teriparatide response.
