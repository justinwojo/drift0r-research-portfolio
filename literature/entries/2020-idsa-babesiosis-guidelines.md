# Summary

- IDSA evidence-based guideline on diagnosis and management of human babesiosis (*Babesia microti* and related species).
- Diagnosis centers on **Giemsa-stained blood smear** and/or **Babesia PCR** in the appropriate clinical setting (fever, hemolysis, endemic exposure, asplenia, immunosuppression); serology supports but does not alone prove active infection.
- First-line therapy for most immunocompetent adults: **atovaquone + azithromycin**; alternative **clindamycin + quinine** (or regimens including clindamycin) for severe disease; exchange transfusion in life-threatening high-parasitemia cases.
- Critical for this case: specialty-lab FISH positivity without standard-lab confirmation is **not** equivalent to guideline-defined active babesiosis—yet the patient’s best antibiotic response was a babesiosis-active regimen, which remains an epidemiologic/therapeutic clue requiring rigorous retesting.

# Key data points

- Preferred diagnostic tests: microscopic identification of parasites on blood smear and/or species-specific PCR.
- Antibody testing indicates exposure; high or rising IgG can support diagnosis but acute infection needs smear/PCR.
- Mild–moderate disease: atovaquone 750 mg PO BID + azithromycin (day 1 load then daily) typically 7–10 days; longer if immunocompromised.
- Severe disease: IV clindamycin + quinine; consider red-cell exchange if parasitemia high or organ failure.
- Immunocompromised patients may need prolonged therapy and can have persistent/relapsing infection—monitor smears/PCR.

# Caveats

- Case lacks classic fever/hemolysis documentation in the evidence pack; atypical chronic MSK presentations are outside the core evidence base.
- FISH assays used by some specialty labs are not the IDSA preferred methods; absence of smear/PCR keeps diagnosis unconfirmed.
- Antibiotic response is not pathognomonic (anti-inflammatory and microbiome effects possible).
- Bartonella is outside this guideline’s scope.
