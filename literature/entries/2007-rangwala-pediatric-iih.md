# Summary

- Comprehensive review of idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH) in children, updating understanding since earlier 1990s summaries.
- Adolescents often resemble adult IIH (female, obese); younger children include more boys and non-obese patients.
- Associated triggers in young children include recombinant growth hormone and all-trans-retinoic acid among others.
- Most improve medically; progressive visual loss may need optic nerve sheath fenestration or shunting.
- Proposes pediatric-specific diagnostic criteria with age-stratified opening-pressure cutoffs.

# Key data points

- Proposed CSF opening pressure: >180 mm H₂O if age <8 with papilledema; >250 mm H₂O if age ≥8 (or <8 without papilledema)—historical proposal, later refined by other groups.
- MRI/MRV recommended to exclude secondary causes (venous sinus thrombosis, mass).
- Visual morbidity is the primary long-term organ threat; headache may persist.
- Young-child IIH may be a different disorder biologically than adolescent/adult IIH.

# Caveats

- 2007 review; adult consensus guidelines (e.g., Mollan/EHF 2018) and IIHTT data supersede management details.
- Opening-pressure thresholds have been revised in later pediatric literature.
- “Childhood IIH” label in a history needs source documentation (papilledema, LP, imaging).
- Research catalog only.
