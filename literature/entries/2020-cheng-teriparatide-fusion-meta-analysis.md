# Summary

- Systematic review and (network) meta-analysis of comparative studies of **teriparatide** and **bisphosphonates** on **spinal fusion outcomes**.
- Main synthesis: **teriparatide significantly improves fusion rates**; bisphosphonates do not demonstrate equivalent fusion-rate benefit.
- Supports prioritizing anabolic therapy when the clinical goal is both systemic BMD gain **and** higher odds of solid arthrodesis.
- Provides quantitative backing for BHO programs and CNS Grade B language on preoperative teriparatide.

# Key data points

- Included comparative clinical studies of anti-osteoporosis agents around spinal fusion.
- Endpoint focus: radiographic fusion/union rates; secondary outcomes often include screw loosening and clinical scores when reported.
- Directional result consistent with Ohtori 2012/2013 primary studies: anabolic > antiresorptive for fusion biology.
- Open-access PLOS ONE publication facilitates independent verification of forest plots and inclusion criteria.
- Does not eliminate residual nonunion risk—optimizes probability, not certainty.

# Caveats

- Underlying studies heterogeneous (dose, duration, surgical technique, sex/age).
- Network MA inherits limitations of sparse head-to-head trials.
- Young-male secondary osteoporosis underrepresented.
- Does not address hypercalciuria/stone risk tradeoff with teriparatide.
- Not medical advice.
