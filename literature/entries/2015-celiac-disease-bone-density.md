# Summary

- Untreated celiac disease (CD) is a well-established cause of low BMD/osteoporosis via malabsorption (Ca, vitamin D), secondary hyperparathyroidism, inflammation, and low BMI/hypogonadism pathways.
- Many newly diagnosed adults already have osteopenia/osteoporosis; some present with bone disease and minimal GI complaints (“occult” CD).
- Gluten-free diet improves BMD in many studies, especially early after diagnosis; recovery is incomplete in some adults.
- Standard serology (tTG-IgA + total IgA) is first-line; **seronegative CD** exists (often IgA deficiency or gluten already restricted) and may need duodenal biopsy ± HLA-DQ2/8.

# Key data points

- Low BMD reported in large fractions of untreated adult CD at diagnosis (study-dependent; often tens of percent osteoporotic).
- Nutrient deficiencies can include iron, folate, B12, fat-soluble vitamins; thiamine deficiency is less classic than in pure alcoholism/beriberi contexts but malabsorption can contribute to multi-nutrient loss.
- Seropositivity associates with lower BMD vs controls in population work (e.g., tTG/EMA studies).
- Biopsy remains reference standard when suspicion is high despite negative serology.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 2

**Why:**
- **Matches (partial):** Markedly low BMD for age is exactly the bone phenotype that prompts CD screening. Historical thiamine deficiency raises a general **malabsorption/nutrient** question even if beriberi had other drivers. Multi-system fatigue/pain syndromes sometimes hide CD.
- **Against:** Patient celiac **serology negative** (per research brief)—substantially lowers probability but does not mathematically zero seronegative CD if IgA sufficient and diet unrestricted. Noninflammatory polyarthralgia with normal CRP is not classic enteropathic arthritis. Hypercalciuria (*SLC7A9*) is opposite the usual hypocalciuria/secondary hyperparathyroidism story of malabsorption. HαT and IgG4 pattern unexplained.
- **Practical:** If not already done with total IgA, document tTG-IgA + IgA level; only pursue endoscopy if high residual suspicion (unexplained iron deficiency, ongoing malabsorption signs, strong family history).

# Caveats

- Negative serology on gluten-free diet is uninterpretable—confirm dietary status.
- HLA-DQ2/8 has high NPV but poor PPV.
- Bone recovery literature is heterogeneous (DXA sites, GFD adherence, age).
