# Summary

- Prospective community-based study of 46 *B. microti*–infected persons in Connecticut/Block Island followed with serial smears, PCR, serology, and symptom questionnaires for up to 27 months.
- Demonstrates that **silent or mild infection may leave PCR-detectable DNA for months**, and rarely can recrudesce after long intervals.
- Specific therapy shortens duration of detectable DNA vs no therapy.

# Key data points

- Untreated/silent infection (n=24): mean DNA persistence **~82 days**.
- Treated acute illness with clindamycin + quinine (n=22): mean DNA persistence **~16 days** (*P*=0.03).
- DNA detectable >3 months: 25% untreated vs 0% treated (*P*=0.02).
- Among untreated subjects, longer DNA persistence correlated with longer symptom duration (mean 114 vs 15 days).
- One subject had recrudescent disease after **two years**.
- Side effects common with clindamycin–quinine (9/22).

# Caveats

- Persistence here is defined by **PCR/smear-detectable** organism DNA, not FISH LDT alone.
- Community cohort of known exposure/endemic infection—not multi-year undiagnosed illness with negative commercial panels.
- Does not validate treating PCR-negative patients based on specialty FISH alone.
- Recrudescence after years was rare, not the rule, in immunocompetent hosts.
