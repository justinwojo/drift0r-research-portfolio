# Summary

- Clinical study of bone density changes in men with hypercalciuria and osteoporosis treated with thiazide diuretics, compared with calcium–vitamin D supplementation in osteoporotic men without hypercalciuria.
- Over ~18 months, annual BMD changes at lumbar spine, femoral neck, and total hip were small and similar between groups.
- Thiazides produced a large reduction in urinary calcium excretion (~46% from baseline).
- Authors conclude thiazides are effective for lowering calciuria (and thus potentially stone risk) but did not demonstrate superior bone-mass gains vs supplementation controls in this design.

# Key data points

- Population: hypercalciuric osteoporotic men on thiazides vs osteoporotic men without hypercalciuria on Ca/Vit D.
- Duration: ~18-month treatment observation.
- Annual BMD changes (examples from abstract): lumbar spine ~0.6±2.5% (thiazide) vs ~0.0±3% (supplementation); femoral neck and total hip similarly small and non-differential.
- Urinary calcium fell ~45.9% in the thiazide group (P=0.0015).
- Interpretation: anticalciuric efficacy confirmed; anabolic-level BMD recovery not shown.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 5

**Why:** Rare male-specific hypercalciuria + osteoporosis thiazide BMD study—sex and dual pathology match. Supports discussing thiazide with a clinician to cut urine Ca (relevant to stones/anabolic-therapy risk (clinician decision)) while tempering expectations that thiazide alone will rebuild marked T-scores.

# Caveats

- Not a randomized comparison of thiazide vs no thiazide within hypercalciuric men; control arm differs by hypercalciuria status.
- Small absolute BMD changes; underpowered for fracture endpoints.
- Does not address combination strategies (thiazide + anabolic, or thiazide + Na restriction titration).
- Older imaging/eras of osteoporosis care; absolute values not transferable to 2025–2026 DXA serials.
