# Summary

- Comprehensive review of osteogenesis imperfecta spanning classic *COL1A1/*COL1A2* dominant disease and expanding recessive genotypes.
- Emphasizes the clinical continuum from perinatal lethal to **mild forms that can present in adolescents/adults as premature osteoporosis** with few extraskeletal clues.
- Mild (type I) disease: quantitative collagen reduction, near-normal stature, blue sclerae variable, fractures often childhood/ambulation-related with post-pubertal decrease—yet adult low BMD and later fractures occur.
- Management: multidisciplinary; antiresorptives in selected patients; emerging anabolic and genetic-therapy research.

# Key data points

- ~85–90% of classic OI is dominant type I collagen gene variants; remaining cases involve collagen-processing, mineralization, and signaling genes (overlap with EOOP panels).
- Mild OI is under-ascertained; family history may be incomplete; de novo variants common.
- Adult issues: hearing loss, basilar invagination risk in severe types, scoliosis, pregnancy counseling, fracture care, and bone-density interpretation.
- Differential includes HPP, idiopathic juvenile/early OP, non-accidental injury (peds), and EDS-spectrum connective-tissue disorders (different genes).

# Caveats

- Negative EDS evaluation ≠ negative OI evaluation.
- Genetic counseling before testing; variants of uncertain significance need expert interpretation.
- Does not explain hypercalciuria/*SLC7A9* or multi-system non-collagen features.
