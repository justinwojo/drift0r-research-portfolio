# Summary

- First definitive link between germline **TPSAB1 α-tryptase copy-number gains** (duplications/triplications) and dominantly inherited elevated basal serum tryptase (BST).
- Studied 35 families plus additional cohorts; elevated BST exclusively associated with extra α-tryptase–encoding *TPSAB1* sequence in the cohorts examined.
- Associated multisystem phenotype in ascertained families: flushing/pruritus, dysautonomia, functional GI symptoms, chronic pain, and **connective tissue features including joint hypermobility**.
- Gene-dose relationship: alleles with three α copies → higher BST and more symptoms than two α copies.

# Key data points

- Population prevalence of elevated BST ~**4–6%**; HαT is the major genetic explanation.
- Autosomal dominant inheritance of extra *TPSAB1* α copies.
- Elevated tryptase here reflects increased constitutive α-tryptase production/secretion, not necessarily mast-cell clonality or acute activation.
- Symptom complexes reported both in familial and additional elevated-BST cohorts.

**Why:** Patient is genetically **HαT-positive** with modestly elevated tryptase (14.5–17.7 mcg/L), **KIT-negative**, without classic MCAS/allergies—exactly the non-clonal genetic hypertryptasemia this paper defined. Connective-tissue/joint phenotype in HαT cohorts is also relevant to his polyarthralgia/fragility frame (not proof of causality).

# Caveats

- Original families were **ascertainment-biased** toward symptomatic multisystem disease; many community HαT carriers are mild or asymptomatic.
- Does not establish that HαT causes markedly low BMD for age or explain non-mast-cell findings (hypogonadism, thiamine deficiency, IgG4 pattern, etc.).
- Bone marrow histology/KIT status not the focus of this genetics paper.
