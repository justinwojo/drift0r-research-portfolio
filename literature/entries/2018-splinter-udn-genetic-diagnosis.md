# Summary

- Reports structure and early outcomes of the multi-site **NIH Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN)** over ~20 months of referrals.
- Eligibility required an undiagnosed condition despite thorough prior evaluation by a health care provider; network scaled beyond the original NIH UDP with clinical sites, sequencing cores, metabolomics, and model-organism support.
- Among applicants who completed full evaluation, **diagnosis rate was 35%** (132/382); most diagnoses came from exome/genome sequencing, with a minority from clinical review alone.
- Diagnoses changed management meaningfully: therapy recommendations (~21%), further diagnostic testing (~37%), and variant-specific genetic counseling (~36%); 31 new syndromes defined.

# Key data points

- Referrals n = 1519; accepted n = 601 (**~40% acceptance**).
- Of accepted patients, 32% had prior exome sequencing.
- Applicant symptom domains: neurologic 40%, musculoskeletal 10%, immunologic 7%, GI 7%, rheumatologic 6%.
- Complete evaluation n = 382 → diagnoses n = 132 (35%); 15 (11%) clinical review alone; 98 (74%) by exome/genome.
- Care impact among diagnosed: therapy change 21%; testing change 37%; counseling 36%.

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 5

**Why:**
- Case is explicitly multi-system without unifying label after specialty workup—the exact UDN use case.
- Musculoskeletal + immunologic symptom mix maps to non-trivial fractions of UDN applicants.
- Provides realistic expectations: selective acceptance, ~1 in 3 diagnosed even after intensive evaluation, genomics is powerful but not guaranteed.
- Anchors “how to apply” thinking: objective multi-system phenotype, exhaustive prior workup documentation, and willingness to pursue deep phenotyping + sequencing.

# Caveats

- Early network experience (2014–era design); methods (RNA-seq, long-read, AI phenotyping) have advanced.
- Pediatric-heavy historic mix; adult multi-system inflammatory/infectious stacks may differ in yield.
- Acceptance is competitive; local academic undiagnosed clinics may be more accessible first steps.
- Not medical advice; diagnosis rate is not a promise for any individual.
