# Summary

- Review of cystinuria as an inherited aminoaciduria causing cystine stones via defective proximal tubular reabsorption of cystine and dibasic amino acids.
- Genes: SLC3A1 (rBAT; type A) and SLC7A9 (b0,+AT; type B); inheritance classically autosomal recessive with incomplete dominance for many SLC7A9 alleles.
- Summarizes that heterozygotes—especially SLC7A9—can have elevated urine cystine and, less often, stones, while biallelic disease causes early, recurrent cystine lithiasis.
- Management framework (high fluid, alkalinization, thiol drugs) is for true cystine stone disease, not isolated hypercalciuria.

# Key data points

- Cystine stones: ~1–2% of adult stones; higher share of pediatric stones.
- SLC7A9 heterozygotes: increased urinary cystine excretion in a large majority of carriers in genotype series; stone formation reported in a minority (literature ranges often cited ~2–18% depending on series/definition).
- Type A heterozygotes (SLC3A1) more often biochemically silent than type B.
- Treatment pillars for active cystinuria: urine volume ≥3 L/day, urine pH ~7.0–7.5 for cystine solubility, thiol-binding agents when conservative measures fail—not first-line for hypercalciuric Ca stones.

# Caveats

- Alkalinization for cystine can raise CaP supersaturation—relevant if misapplied in a hypercalciuric alkaline-urine patient.
- Review-level heterozygote risk estimates vary by cohort and ascertainment.
- Qualitative cystine negative on high-volume urine weakens active cystine stone risk.
