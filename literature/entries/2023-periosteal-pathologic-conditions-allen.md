# Summary

- Comprehensive imaging pathophysiology review of the **periosteum** and periosteal disease spectrum.
- Explains why MRI detects **periosteal edema/elevation/enhancement** earlier and more often than radiography shows solid new bone.
- Maps morphology and distribution of periosteal reaction to categories: physiologic, traumatic, infectious, neoplastic, metabolic/drug-related, HOA-spectrum.

# Key data points

- Normal periosteum is a living cambium + fibrous layer; reaction patterns reflect rate and nature of stimulus.
- On MRI, periosteal reaction may appear as linear high T2 signal elevating periosteum, soft-tissue edema abutting cortex, and post-contrast periosteal enhancement—even when radiographs are near-normal.
- Infection and trauma commonly produce **unilateral** periosteal and adjacent soft-tissue change; systemic entities more often **bilateral/multifocal**.
- Distribution (diaphysis vs metaphysis/epiphysis; single bone vs polyostotic) is as important as morphology.
- Differentiates physiologic/healing periosteal reaction from aggressive infection/neoplasm using combination of clinical tempo + marrow signal + soft-tissue collection.

# Caveats

- Does not specifically evaluate **imaging-negative tendon-area nodules**.
- Adult immunocompetent mild unilateral periosteal edema has a long DDx; review is pattern-based, not predictive for a single etiology.
- Case lacks reported aggressive periosteal matrix or large collection.
