# Summary

- Surveillance study of 913 patients with cat-scratch disease (CSD) defined by compatible clinical disease plus confirmatory B. henselae serology or PCR.
- Musculoskeletal manifestations (MMs) occurred in 96/913 (10.5%): myalgia 5.8%, arthropathy (arthralgia/arthritis) 5.5%, tendinitis 0.8%, neuralgia 0.4%, osteomyelitis 0.2%.
- Arthropathy often involved medium/large joints; 7 patients had symptoms ≥1 year and 5 developed chronic disease.
- Osteomyelitis—the classic textbook MSK CSD lesion—was the rarest MM; prolonged arthropathy was more common.

# Key data points

- MM prevalence 10.5% of confirmed CSD (not rare).
- Median age with MM 31.5 y vs 15.0 y without MM; age >20 y strongly associated (RR ~5 for any MM; RR ~11 for arthropathy).
- Myalgia median duration 4 weeks (range 1–26); arthropathy median 5.5 weeks (range 1–240).
- Female sex and erythema nodosum associated with arthropathy in multivariate models.
- Osteomyelitis only 2/913 cases—rarest MM despite high clinical awareness.

# Caveats

- Israeli surveillance cohort—epidemiology may differ from US specialty-lab populations.
- Diagnosis relied on serology/PCR within a CSD framework; not enrichment-culture bacteremia series.
- Does not address specialty-lab IgM+/PCR− discordance or non-CSD chronic bartonellosis debates.
- No imaging protocol for periosteal edema vs osteomyelitis.
