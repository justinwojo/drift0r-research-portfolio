# Summary

- Reports methods and early network-wide outcomes of the **NIH Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN)** for patients who remain undiagnosed after extensive evaluation.
- Among 382 patients with complete evaluation in the analyzed period, **132 (35%)** received a diagnosis; many diagnoses altered management, and new disease-gene relationships were discovered.
- Model combines rigorous record review, deep clinical phenotyping, family-based exome/genome sequencing, model-organism studies, and multidisciplinary case conferences.

# Key data points

- UDN launched 2014 as a multi-site expansion of the NIH UDP concept.
- Diagnosis yield ~1 in 3 of fully evaluated accepted participants—not of all applicants.
- Diagnoses include rare Mendelian diseases, phenotypic expansions, and occasional non-genetic answers.
- Downstream effects: therapy changes, ending diagnostic odysseys, reproductive counseling, and research gene discovery.
- Application is selective; many applicants are not accepted (see also Walley 2018 / Mulvihill 2024 on applicant characteristics).

# Caveats

- Acceptance rates are low; local undiagnosed disease clinics may be more accessible first steps.
- 35% yield is not a promise for any individual.
- 2018 data; later network reports show continued but similar-order diagnosis rates with newer omics.
- Not medical advice.
