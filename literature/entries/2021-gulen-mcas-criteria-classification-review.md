# Summary

- Argues that **consensus MCAS criteria** (typical episodic multi-organ symptoms + documented mediator rise + response to targeted therapy) should be applied rigorously.
- Warns that **nonspecific alternative criteria** lead to widespread **misdiagnosis of MCAS**.
- Distinguishes primary (clonal), secondary, and idiopathic MCAS and emphasizes validation/specificity of biomarkers.

# Key data points

- Event-related tryptase increase formula remains the preferred biochemical anchor when available.
- Chronic stable basal tryptase elevation (as in HαT) is **not** the same as an activation event.
- Urinary metabolites (N-methylhistamine, LTE4, 11β-PGF2α / 2,3-dinor-11β-PGF2α) can support activation but still require clinical context.
