# Summary

- Adult-onset Still’s disease (AOSD) is a systemic autoinflammatory illness: high spiking fever, evanescent salmon-colored rash, arthralgia/arthritis, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, serositis, and marked acute-phase response.
- Diagnosis is clinical (e.g., Yamaguchi criteria) after exclusion of infection, malignancy, and other rheumatic disease.
- Extremely high serum ferritin (often glycosylated fraction low) and **elevated CRP/ESR** are hallmark laboratory patterns; leukocytosis is common.
- Arthritis can become chronic and destructive in a subset; IL-1/IL-6 targeted therapy is used in refractory disease.

# Key data points

- Most modern series report **elevated ESR and CRP in the large majority** of active AOSD (often >80% for each).
- Quotidian fever and typical rash are major criteria; without them, AOSD is hard to sustain.
- Synovitis and systemic inflammation are expected—not “pain only with normal CRP forever.”
- Macrophage activation syndrome is a feared complication.

# Caveats

- Criteria require exclusion of infection/malignancy first.
- Chronic AOSD phenotypes still typically show inflammatory lab activity during flares.
- Do not stretch Yamaguchi criteria to fit pain syndromes without fever/rash/acute-phase response.
