# Summary

- Contemporary **systematic review + network meta-analysis** evaluating efficacy of conventional and **novel SCS** modalities versus medical management for chronic back and leg pain.
- Synthesizes modern evidence beyond mid-2000s tonic SCS trials (PROCESS/North era), including advanced waveforms used in current practice.
- Overall direction: SCS associated with greater pain reduction than medical management alone across pooled analyses, with variation by waveform and trial quality.
- Useful for setting realistic expectations after permanent implant: meaningful average benefit, substantial non-responders, and outcome heterogeneity.

# Key data points

- Population: chronic back and/or leg pain cohorts treated with SCS vs CMM/comparators in RCTs eligible for network synthesis.
- Includes conventional and novel SCS paradigms (e.g., high-frequency, burst—study-defined).
- Primary outcomes typically ≥50% pain reduction and continuous pain score change at mid-term follow-up (e.g., ~6 months in related SCS evidence syntheses).
- Network methods allow indirect comparisons when head-to-head trials are sparse.
- Confirms SCS as an evidence-supported option while highlighting residual uncertainty and device-era differences.

# Caveats

- Network MA assumptions (transitivity) can fail when trial populations differ (FBSS vs nonsurgical chronic pain).
- Industry-funded trials dominate neuromodulation literature.
- Does **not** characterize multi-joint systemic flares weeks after implant.
- Not medical advice.
