# Summary

- Narrative review of partial androgen deficiency of aging males (PADAM) and neuropsychiatric/behavioral symptoms.
- Classic low-T behavioral cluster: fatigue, **decreased libido**, depressive mood, low motivation, anxiety, **irritability**, insomnia, impaired concentration/memory, reduced dominance/performance.
- Overlap with major depression; discusses mixed evidence linking T levels to depression and antidepressant effects of androgen replacement.
- Frames androgens as modulators of sexuality, aggression, emotion, and personality — heavily modified by social context.

# Key data points

- Symptom list is the standard clinical teaching set for hypogonadal mood/behavior (not a quantitative cohort).
- Emphasizes inconsistency in human data relating T to aggression/personality.
- Psychological PADAM features can mimic primary psychiatric disease — differential diagnosis issue.

**Why:**
- **Partial match:** irritability/aggression and misattribution to primary psychiatric disease appear in both literature and patient history (SSD later reversed; “manic” episode resolved with T normalization).
- **Major mismatch:** classic literature predicts **low** libido with low T; patient reports **paradoxical hypersexuality/hyperarousal when T is low** and high-T-like symptoms — opposite of PADAM stereotype.
- Still useful as the “expected phenotype” against which the paradox is defined.

# Caveats

- Aging-male focus; narrative, not systematic.
- Does not document true paradoxical hypersexuality at profound hypogonadism.
- Cannot substitute for psychiatric evaluation of mood/aggression.
