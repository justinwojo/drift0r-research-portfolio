# Summary

- Retrospective study of 101 patients with Bartonella endocarditis diagnosed by high-titer IFA (≥1:800), PCR, and/or culture of blood/serum/valve tissue (1995–2001).
- Outcomes: 12 deaths and 2 relapses; recovery associated with aminoglycoside use.
- Patients receiving aminoglycosides more likely to fully recover (P=0.02); ≥14 days aminoglycoside associated with better survival vs shorter courses (P=0.02).
- Concludes effective therapy should include an aminoglycoside for a minimum of 2 weeks—foundation for later endocarditis guidelines (doxy + gentamicin/rifampin strategies).

# Key data points

- n=101 Bartonella endocarditis cases—largest early outcome series.
- Mortality 12%; relapse 2%.
- Aminoglycoside exposure and ≥14-day duration statistically linked to better outcomes.
- Illustrates culture-negative endocarditis as a major Bartonella clinical niche.

# Caveats

- Endocarditis population—not MSK/polyarthralgia cohort.
- Retrospective; antibiotic choices not randomized.
- Does not speak to IGeneX IgM or periosteal edema presentations.
- Gentamicin toxicity considerations limit extrapolation to outpatient chronic regimens.
