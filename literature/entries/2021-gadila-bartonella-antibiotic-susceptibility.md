# Summary

- Tested ceftriaxone, doxycycline, gentamicin, azithromycin, ampicillin, and azlocillin against Bartonella under different growth conditions.
- All drugs had MICs <0.5 µg/mL in BAPGM enrichment media but failed to inhibit growth in standard insect media; azlocillin most potent (MIC 0.01 µg/mL).
- Under intracellular growth conditions, no single antibiotic was efficacious; azlocillin + azithromycin combination inhibited intracellular growth.
- Supports the concept that culture microenvironment and intracellular niche drastically alter apparent antibiotic efficacy—relevant to chronic infection treatment debates.

# Key data points

- Standard-of-care agents (doxy, azithro, gentamicin, etc.) appear active extracellularly under enrichment conditions yet fail intracellularly as monotherapies in this model.
- Azlocillin+azithromycin only effective intracellular combination among those tested here.
- Authors note gentamicin+doxycycline remains best regimen for confirmed endocarditis clinically.
- Bridges lab susceptibility paradoxes long noted by Rolain/Raoult groups.

# Caveats

- In vitro only; azlocillin combinations are experimental, not standard care.
- Does not validate treating serology-only patients with multi-drug regimens.
- Patient’s regimen centered on atovaquone/clinda (anti-Babesia) rather than doxy/rifampin/gentamicin.
- Not clinical evidence of chronic human MSK bartonellosis.
