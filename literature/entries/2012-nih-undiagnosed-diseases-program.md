# Summary

- Reports structure, methods, and early outcomes of the **NIH Undiagnosed Diseases Program (UDP)** for patients who remain without diagnosis after extensive standard workups.
- Model: rigorous record triage → inpatient/outpatient deep phenotyping → selective SNP array / exome / genome with family controls → multidisciplinary interpretation.
- Early cohort: of 160 admitted participants, **~24%** received a diagnosis (clinical, biochemical, pathologic, or molecular); included rare/ultra-rare diseases and two newly recognized disorders.
- Methodological anchor for analysts: multi-system undiagnosed adults need systematic phenotype ontologies, reverse phenotyping after genomic hits, and acceptance that many remain unsolved—not a failure of effort alone.

# Key data points

- Application funnel is highly selective (thousands of records → hundreds accepted → fewer admitted).
- ~53% of early admissions had neurologic disease dominance; adults and children both included.
- Diagnoses arose from clinical insight, biochemistry, pathology, SNP arrays (CNVs/homozygosity), and exome filtering—not genomics alone.
- Large runs of homozygosity more common in affected participants than controls in the array substudy.
- UDP later scaled into the multi-site Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN) with similar philosophy.

# Caveats

- 2012 early experience; modern UDN uses updated sequencing, RNA-seq, and model organisms.
- Diagnosis rate ~1 in 4 admitted is not a promise for any single patient.
- Access to UDP/UDN is limited and application-based; local academic undiagnosed clinics may be more feasible first steps.
- Not medical advice; does not prioritize infection vs autoinflammatory vs endocrine stacks for this phenotype.
