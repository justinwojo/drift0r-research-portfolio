# Summary

- Retrospective comparison of **151 consecutive Not Accepted** vs **50 randomly selected Accepted** UDN applications to identify factors associated with network acceptance and eventual diagnosis.
- Core distinction: undiagnosed diseases programs prioritize applicants with **objective findings** warranting further investigation over applicants dominated by **subjective symptoms** (pain, fatigue) without hard signs/labs/imaging.
- Not Accepted applicants were older, reported shorter illness duration, had fewer objective findings / more subjective symptoms, and were more often referred by primary care after many failed specialty consults.
- Practical “how to apply” implication: package the referral around objective multi-system evidence (labs, imaging, biopsy, genetics already done), not symptom intensity alone.

# Key data points

- Design: chart/portal review of application materials and referral letters; primary outcome Accepted vs Not Accepted (and diagnosis if accepted).
- Not Accepted group: significantly fewer objective findings, more subjective symptoms, older age, shorter reported illness period, higher primary-care referral fraction.
- Specialty consultation frequency prior to application was similar between Accepted and Not Accepted—**more consults alone do not equal acceptance**.
- Authors argue that distinguishing objective undiagnosed disease from primarily functional/subjective syndromes helps triage who benefits from intensive diagnostic odysseys vs symptom-focused pathways.
- ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02450851 context for UDN.

# Caveats

- Single-network application review; criteria and capacity change over time.
- “Subjective vs objective” is a triage heuristic, not a moral judgment that pain is unreal.
- Acceptance ≠ diagnosis; many accepted patients remain unsolved.
- Not medical advice; does not replace current UDN portal instructions.
