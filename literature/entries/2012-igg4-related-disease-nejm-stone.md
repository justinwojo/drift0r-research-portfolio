# Summary

- Foundational clinical review defining **IgG4-related disease** as a multiorgan fibroinflammatory condition unified by shared pathology.
- Clarifies relationship between **serum IgG4 elevation**, tissue IgG4-positive plasma cells, and organ-threatening fibrosis/mass lesions.
- Sets expectations for diagnosis: clinical + serologic + radiologic + histopathologic synthesis—not a single lab cutoff.

# Key data points

- Core pathology triad (where available): dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, **storiform fibrosis**, obliterative phlebitis, with increased IgG4+ plasma cells.
- Serum IgG4 is elevated in many but not all patients; elevated serum IgG4 also occurs in many non-IgG4-RD conditions.
- Typical organs: pancreas (autoimmune pancreatitis type 1), salivary/lacrimal glands, biliary tree, orbits, retroperitoneum, kidney, lung, nodes, aorta.
- Soft-tissue and MSK involvement can occur but are less common presentations than glandular/visceral disease.
- Glucocorticoids are classically effective in inflammatory phases; relapse is common; biopsy often required for durable classification.

# Caveats

- Serum IgG4 ~2× ULN is only modestly elevated relative to many active IgG4-RD cohorts (often higher), and IgG1 is low—pattern is atypical.
- Imaging-negative forearm nodules and mild periosteal edema are not core NEJM-described IgG4-RD manifestations.
- Not medical proof for or against “IgG4-opathy” without tissue.
