# Summary

- Clinical study showing that low *baseline* pneumococcal polysaccharide antibody (pPA) titers—especially poor titers to serotype 3—associate strongly with subsequent SAD diagnosis.
- Low baseline titers also associated with increased respiratory infections (including chronic rhinosinusitis) and allergy diagnoses.
- Supports using low pre-vaccine titers as a risk signal that warrants formal vaccine-response testing.
- Bridges lab abnormality → clinical infection phenotype.

# Key data points

- Low baseline-pPA and low serotype-3 titers highly associated with SAD.
- Clinical correlates: higher incidence of respiratory infections including CRS; allergy diagnoses more frequent.
- Practical implication: baseline panels are not useless; very low baselines predict impaired vaccine responders.
- Open-access full text available for method details and statistics.

# Caveats

- Association study; low titers predict SAD risk, they do not equal SAD.
- Cohort clinical setting may enrich for allergic/ENT populations.
- Does not address bone disease, cytokines, or HαT.
- Not medical advice.
