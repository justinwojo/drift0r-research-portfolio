# Summary

- 18-month randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of PTH(1-34) in 23 men with idiopathic osteoporosis (ages 30–68; mean ~50).
- Many men with idiopathic osteoporosis have low-turnover histomorphometry; anabolic therapy is mechanistically attractive.
- PTH 400 IU daily (~teriparatide-range) plus calcium/vitamin D vs placebo vehicle.
- Lumbar spine BMD increased 13.5% with PTH; controls unchanged (p<0.001).
- Mean lumbar T-score improved from approximately −3.5 toward −2.4.
- Femoral neck BMD +2.9% (p<0.05); 1/3 distal radius stable.
- Markers of formation and resorption rose substantially (e.g., osteocalcin +230%, urinary NTX +375% by 12 months).
- First clear demonstration that intermittent PTH can reverse severe idiopathic male osteoporosis densitometrically.

# Key data points

- N=23 (10 PTH, 13 control); 18 months.
- Spine BMD +13.5% with PTH.
- Baseline mean lumbar T-score ~−3.5 in treated men.
- Age range includes 30s—closer to Drift0r age than many trials.
- Low-turnover idiopathic phenotype emphasized at baseline.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- Age band and severe spine osteoporosis closely parallel case (T to −4.3 vs trial mean −3.5).
- Supports anabolic (teriparatide-class) therapy for idiopathic male osteoporosis after secondary causes addressed.
- Radius stability mirrors limited cortical/forearm gains seen with PTH anabolics.
- Caveat: Drift0r has upper-normal/high CTX (not classic low-turnover); still may respond, but phenotype differs from classic Kurland low-turnover IOP.

# Caveats

- Very small sample; not powered for fractures.
- PTH dose formulation differs from commercial teriparatide 20 µg.
- Idiopathic male osteoporosis is heterogeneous (low- vs high-turnover subsets).
- Requires exclusion of secondary causes before “idiopathic” label.
