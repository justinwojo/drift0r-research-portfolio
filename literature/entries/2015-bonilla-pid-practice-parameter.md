# Summary

- Joint AAAAI/ACAAI practice parameter covering diagnosis and management of primary immunodeficiencies, including antibody deficiencies with **normal total immunoglobulins**.
- Defines evaluation of specific antibody responses (protein and polysaccharide antigens), IgG subclass measurement caveats, and when IgG replacement is considered.
- Central reference for specific antibody deficiency (SAD) and selective IgG subclass deficiency in adults and children.

# Key data points

- **Specific antibody deficiency**: impaired antibody response to polysaccharide and/or protein antigens with normal (or near-normal) total IgG/A/M and otherwise intact immunity.
- Pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV) serotype responses and Hib/tetanus/diphtheria protein responses are standard functional assessments.
- IgG subclass deficiency: persistently low one or more subclasses with normal total IgG; clinical significance requires infection phenotype and/or impaired specific antibodies—not lab values alone.
- IgG1 comprises the majority of total IgG; isolated IgG1 deficiency with normal total IgG is uncommon but recognized; combine with functional antibody testing.
- Management may include antibiotic prophylaxis, vaccination strategies, and selective IgG replacement based on severity.

# Caveats

- 2015 document; serotype interpretation thresholds and conjugate vs polysaccharide algorithms have nuances updated in later literature (e.g., Perez 2017).
- Protective cutoffs vary by lab and serotype panel; patient values should be re-checked against contemporaneous assay standards.
- Not medical advice; research framing only.
