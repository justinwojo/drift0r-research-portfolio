# Summary

- IOF-IFCC Bone Marker Standards Working Group position paper on clinical use of bone turnover markers (BTMs).
- Recommends serum PINP (formation) and serum CTX (resorption) as international reference markers for observational and interventional studies.
- High BTMs may predict fracture risk independently of BMD in postmenopausal women; evidence still needs strengthening for routine prediction.
- BTMs provide pharmacodynamic monitoring of treatment response (antiresorptive suppression; anabolic rise).
- Clinical monitoring limited by preanalytical variability, assay differences, and quality control needs.
- Standardization agenda: same markers, standardized assays, shared reference experience.
- Establishes the scientific basis for interpreting CTX in research and practice.
- Foundational citation whenever CTX is used as a case anchor.

# Key data points

- Reference resorption marker: serum CTX (s-CTX).
- Reference formation marker: serum PINP (s-PINP).
- High BTMs associated with higher fracture risk independent of BMD (best data in postmenopausal women).
- Treatment monitoring is a major practical use case.

# Similarity to Drift0r case

- CTX 616 at upper normal is interpretable within the IOF reference-marker framework.
- Upper-normal/high resorption supports a high-turnover component to ongoing bone loss (−4.2%/yr hip).
- Provides rationale to pair CTX with PINP when evaluating anabolic vs antiresorptive strategy.
- Serial CTX/PINP useful if teriparatide or antiresorptive therapy is started.

# Caveats

- Strongest fracture-prediction data are in older postmenopausal women, not young men.
- Single CTX value needs context: fasting status, circadian rhythm, recent fracture, assay, reference interval.
- Upper-normal is not the same as frankly elevated; clinical judgment required.
- Does not diagnose a specific secondary cause.
