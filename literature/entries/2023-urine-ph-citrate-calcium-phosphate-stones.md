# Summary

- Contemporary analysis of urinary parameters—especially pH and citrate—as predictors of calcium phosphate (CaP) stone formation.
- Reinforces that urine pH is a dominant determinant of whether calcium stones are phosphate-rich vs oxalate-dominant.
- Citrate is both an inhibitor of crystallization and a modifier of acid–base/pH milieu; interpretation is context-dependent.
- Directly applicable when counseling a hypercalciuric patient with alkaline-leaning urine about composition-specific prevention (and about risks of further alkalinization).

# Key data points

- Higher urine pH associates with higher likelihood of CaP as the stone phase.
- Citrate and pH interact in models of CaP risk; low citrate can compound risk even when pH is not extreme.
- Supports stone-analysis + 24h urine pairing rather than assuming all calcium stones are CaOx.
- Clinical implication: strategies that raise urine pH (alkali, some diets) may reduce CaOx/uric acid risk but increase CaP supersaturation if calcium excretion remains high.

# Caveats

- Patient’s actual stone composition (if ever analyzed) is not recorded in the public case pack.
- Single 24h pH is not as informative as mean pH from multiple collections or stone mineral analysis.
- Heterozygous cystinuria is a separate crystal risk axis (cystine) not modeled here.
