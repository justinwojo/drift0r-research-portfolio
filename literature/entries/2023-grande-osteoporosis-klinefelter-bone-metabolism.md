# Summary

- Updated open-access review of low bone mass in KS: osteoporosis prevalence ≈6–15% and osteopenia ≈25–48% across studies.
- Reduced bone mass is already described from adolescence and may involve both decreased formation and increased resorption.
- Low testosterone is clearly involved but incomplete—eugonadal or adequately replaced KS men can still have impaired BMD.
- Reviews diagnostic monitoring (DXA), lifestyle, vitamin D/calcium, TRT timing, and limited data on bone-specific agents in KS.

# Key data points

- Peak bone mass acquisition during adolescence/young adulthood is a critical vulnerable window if hypogonadism is untreated.
- Mechanisms beyond T: estrogen via aromatization, INSL3, muscle–bone unit, vitamin D/PTH axis, possible chromosomal dosage effects.
- Late-started TRT is less likely to normalize adult BMD than earlier intervention in some observational series.
- Practical care: baseline and interval DXA, fracture risk assessment, treat vitamin D deficiency, optimize T, consider antiresorptive/anabolic therapy when fracture risk high—specialist judgment.

# Caveats

- Prevalence ranges vary by age, TRT exposure, and densitometry definitions (T- vs Z-score use in young men).
- Few KS-specific RCTs of osteoporosis drugs.
