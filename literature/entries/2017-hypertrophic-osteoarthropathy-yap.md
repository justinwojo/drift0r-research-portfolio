# Summary

- Review of **hypertrophic osteoarthropathy (HOA)**: digital clubbing, tubular-bone **periostosis**, and synovial effusions.
- Distinguishes primary HOA (pachydermoperiostosis) from **secondary HOA**, most often pulmonary/malignancy-associated (formerly hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy).
- Details imaging across radiography, CT, MRI, and bone scintigraphy and differentiates HOA from other multifocal periostitis (thyroid acropachy, hypervitaminosis A, venous insufficiency, voriconazole periostitis, progressive diaphyseal dysplasia, lymphoma).

# Key data points

- Secondary HOA strongly associated with **non-small cell lung cancer** and other intrathoracic disease; also GI tumors, cyanotic heart disease, cirrhosis, IBD, chronic infection.
- Periostosis typically **bilateral, symmetric**, diaphyseal/metaphyseal of long bones; may progress to multilayered and epiphyseal involvement.
- Bone scan classically shows “tram-track” / double-stripe cortical uptake.
- Clubbing + periostosis + arthritis-like joint symptoms form the clinical triad; incomplete forms exist.
- Management hinges on treating the underlying secondary cause; periostosis can regress after successful treatment of driver disease.

# Caveats

- Incomplete/secondary HOA can lack full triad—cannot fully exclude atypical HOA without dedicated long-bone survey if clinical suspicion rises.
- Tiny pulmonary nodules ≤3 mm are not typical drivers of secondary HOA.
- Does not explain imaging-negative forearm tendon nodules or IgG4/IgG1 skew.
