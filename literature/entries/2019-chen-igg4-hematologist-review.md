# Summary

- Hematology-oriented review of IgG4-RD presentations: lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia, and organ mass lesions that mimic lymphoma or plasma-cell neoplasms.
- Stresses multi-modal diagnosis and the limits of serum IgG4 as a standalone test.
- Useful for framing elevated IgG4 discovered on immunodeficiency or myeloma-style workups.

# Key data points

- ~70% of IgG4-RD patients have elevated serum IgG4; reported diagnostic sensitivity often ~83–97% but **specificity substantially lower**.
- Polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia and IgG4-rich nodes can mimic hematologic malignancy—clonality studies matter (patient SPEP/FLC polyclonal).
- Mild elevations occur in allergic disease, chronic infection, autoimmune disease, and malignancy.
- Pathologic confirmation (storiform fibrosis, obliterative phlebitis, IgG4 counts) remains gold-standard support when feasible.
