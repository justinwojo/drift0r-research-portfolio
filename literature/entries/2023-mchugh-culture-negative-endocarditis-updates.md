# Summary

- Reviews epidemiology, microbiology, diagnosis, and treatment of **blood culture–negative infective endocarditis (BCNE)** in the era of molecular diagnostics and greater surgical access.
- Categorizes BCNE into: (1) sterilized cultures after antibiotics, (2) fastidious organisms poorly recovered on standard media, and (3) **true intracellular pathogens** (*Bartonella*, *Coxiella*, Tropheryma, etc.) plus noninfectious mimics (NBTE).
- Emphasizes structured testing: history/epidemiology → prolonged/specialized cultures → serology → targeted and shotgun metagenomic sequencing of blood/valve tissue.

# Key data points

- *Bartonella* and *C. burnetii* remain leading true BCNE pathogens in many series when systematically tested.
- Serology is often the fastest practical screen; valve PCR/NGS raises yield when tissue is available.
- Nonbacterial thrombotic endocarditis must stay on the differential.
- Treatment is pathogen-directed once identified; empiric coverage debates are specialist-level.

# Caveats

- Endocarditis-focused; MSK nodules/periostitis are outside scope.
- Metagenomics access and interpretation vary by center.
- Positive specialty LDT blood tests without objective endovascular or tissue disease remain a separate problem.
