# Summary

- Retrospective comparison of oral clomiphene citrate (CC; typically 50 mg every other day) vs testosterone gel replacement therapy (TGRT) for hypogonadism (T <300 ng/dL) or male infertility with low T.
- Both raised serum T into the mid-normal range with similar mean post-treatment T (~573 ng/dL CC vs ~553 ng/dL TGRT).
- CC improved ADAM symptom scores; monthly drug cost substantially lower for CC than branded gels in the study era.
- Supports CC as a biochemical and clinical option, especially when fertility or lower cost matter.

# Key data points

- n = 104 (65 CC, 39 TGRT); mean age 42 (CC) vs 57 (TGRT).
- Mean follow-up ~23 mo (CC) vs ~46 mo (TGRT).
- CC 50 mg EOD; gels 5 g daily (AndroGel/Testim 1%).
- ADAM score: pretreatment 4.9 → 2.1 on CC (p < 0.05).
- Cost (study era): CC ~$83/mo vs gels ~$265–270/mo.

**Why:**

# Caveats

- Nonrandomized; age imbalance between arms; selection bias likely.
- Off-label use of CC in men; long-term safety not definitive.
- Does not address multi-system crash after abrupt stop, gynecomastia management, or bone outcomes in detail.
