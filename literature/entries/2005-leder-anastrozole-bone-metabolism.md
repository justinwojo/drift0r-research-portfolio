# Summary

- Randomized, placebo-controlled 12-week trial of anastrozole in elderly mildly hypogonadal men assessing **bone turnover markers** and total-body BMD after aromatase inhibition.
- Anastrozole raised testosterone substantially and lowered estradiol modestly at both 1 mg daily and 1 mg twice weekly.
- Over **12 weeks**, markers of resorption and formation did **not** worsen vs placebo; total-body BMD unchanged—authors suggested extended AI therapy might not harm bone.
- Subsequent longer BMD data (Burnett-Bowie 2009; Kacker 2014 observational) contradict optimistic extrapolation from short-term markers alone—this paper is the short-horizon comparator, not a long-term safety clearance.

# Key data points

- n = 37 men ages 62–74; baseline T <350 ng/dL.
- Arms: anastrozole 1 mg daily (n=12), 1 mg twice weekly (n=11), placebo (n=14) for 12 weeks.
- Bioavailable T roughly doubled on daily AI (99→207 ng/dL); estradiol fell (e.g., 26→17 pg/mL daily arm).
- Serum NTx, urinary DPD, osteocalcin, P1NP, OPG stable; total-body BMD unchanged at 12 weeks.
- Mechanism framing: simultaneous ↑T and modest ↓E2 create opposing skeletal stimuli—net effect time-dependent.

# Caveats

- 12 weeks is too short for DXA-detectable cortical/trabecular change at clinically decisive sites.
- Authors’ hopeful long-term inference was later challenged by Burnett-Bowie spine BMD results.
- Dosing studied (daily or 2×/week) differs from patient historical 1 mg **weekly**.
- Not medical advice.
