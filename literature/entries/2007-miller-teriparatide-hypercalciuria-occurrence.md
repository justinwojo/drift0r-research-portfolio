# Summary

- Prespecified safety analysis of 24-hour urinary calcium excretion for up to 12 months of teriparatide (TPTD) in two RCTs (1637 postmenopausal women; 437 men; total n=2074).
- All participants received calcium 1000 mg/day + vitamin D 400–1200 IU/day and were randomized to placebo, TPTD 20 µg/day, or TPTD 40 µg/day.
- Urinary calcium rose in all groups vs baseline at most time points; TPTD 20 µg increased urine calcium by up to ~32 mg/day vs concurrent placebo in women (P<0.05).
- Only 7 participants (0.3%) discontinued for repeated hypercalciuria (>300 mg/day)—3 placebo, 4 TPTD.
- Authors concluded magnitude of change is unlikely clinically relevant for **most** patients and does not warrant routine urine calcium monitoring for most—but this excludes high-baseline hypercalciuria phenotypes.

# Key data points

- Pooled osteoporosis/low-bone-mass RCTs; urine Ca at baseline, 1, 6, 12 months.
- Mean on-treatment urine Ca increase with TPTD 20 µg vs placebo up to ~32 mg/day (study 1).
- Discontinuation for sustained hypercalciuria rare (0.3%).
- Registration populations largely excluded active/recent stone disease and extreme baseline hypercalciuria.

# Caveats

- “Unlikely clinically relevant for most” ≠ safe without monitoring when baseline urine Ca is already above 300 mg/day.
- Alkaline urine / CaP stone risk phenotype needs separate metabolic management (thiazide, citrate, diet) if anabolic therapy proceeds.
- Companion expert safety reviews (Miller 2008) recommend more caution in stone formers than the abstract conclusion implies.
- Research catalog only—not a treatment recommendation.
