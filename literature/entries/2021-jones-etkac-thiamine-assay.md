# Summary

- Protocol-focused paper describing the erythrocyte transketolase activity coefficient (ETKAC) assay for thiamine status assessment.
- ETK is a thiamine-diphosphate–dependent enzyme; the activity coefficient after in vitro TDP stimulation estimates functional thiamine adequacy.
- Positions ETKAC among available biomarkers alongside direct measurement of whole-blood/erythrocyte thiamine diphosphate.
- Emphasizes need for standardized, reliable biomarkers to diagnose thiamine deficiency in clinical and population settings.

# Key data points

- ETKAC = functional assay reflecting TDP saturation of erythrocyte transketolase.
- Higher stimulation (activity coefficient) after adding TDP indicates greater functional deficiency.
- Complements—not always replaces—direct ThDP quantification by HPLC/LC-MS/MS.
- Preanalytical handling and method standardization are critical for comparability.
- Useful when direct ThDP assays are unavailable.

# Caveats

- Protocol/methods paper, not a clinical neuropathy series.
- Does not set universal clinical cutoffs for all labs.
- Modern practice increasingly favors direct whole-blood TDP LC-MS/MS over ETK.
