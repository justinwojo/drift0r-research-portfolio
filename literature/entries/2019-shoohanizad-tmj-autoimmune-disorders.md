# Summary

- Narrative review of **temporomandibular disorders (TMD/TMJ)** occurring in the setting of autoimmune systemic disease, with focus on nonsurgical management options.
- TMJ has unique anatomy (disc, loading pattern, growth history) so autoimmune joint disease may present differently than typical peripheral arthritis—and can be an early or concurrent feature of RA, SLE, and systemic sclerosis.
- Emphasizes early recognition of TMJ autoimmune involvement to avoid irreversible structural damage; medical therapy (steroids, DMARDs including methotrexate, NSAIDs, combinations) is first-line before invasive reconstruction.
- Clinical takeaway for sudden-onset TMJ pain/dysfunction: expand beyond dental occlusal explanations when systemic joint or inflammatory context is present.

# Key data points

- Literature window reviewed roughly 2010–2019 (Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus).
- Autoimmune contexts discussed: rheumatoid arthritis, lupus erythematosus, systemic sclerosis (among others).
- TMJ considered to require disease-specific management strategies relative to other joints.
- Collagen-induced arthritis models referenced as experimental platforms for RA–TMJ research.
- Medical options summarized: oral steroids, conventional DMARDs, NSAIDs, methotrexate (~7.5 mg cited in source context), combination regimens.
- Interdisciplinary care (medicine + oral/maxillofacial + physical therapy) recommended.

# Caveats

- Narrative review; not a new epidemiologic incidence study of sudden-onset TMJ as first manifestation.
- Many management details derived from peripheral arthritis paradigms.
- Does not prove autoimmune TMJ in seronegative, noninflammatory polyarthralgia phenotypes.
- Not medical advice.
