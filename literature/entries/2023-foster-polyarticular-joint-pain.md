# Summary

- Defines polyarticular joint pain as involvement of five or more joints; can be inflammatory or noninflammatory.
- Most common chronic causes: osteoarthritis (noninflammatory) and rheumatoid arthritis (inflammatory; ≥0.25% adults worldwide).
- Initial evaluation centers on inflammation signs, pain location, duration, systemic symptoms, and pathogen exposures.
- Redness, warmth, or swelling suggests synovitis; RF and CCP help when RA is a concern; conventional radiography remains first-line imaging, with MRI/US for selected inflammatory questions.

# Key data points

- Systematic exam pattern (distribution + synovitis) narrows DDx more than any single lab.
- Labs, aspiration, and imaging confirm a *suspected* diagnosis rather than replace clinical pattern recognition.
- RF and anti-CCP useful when inflammatory RA is on the table; negative serologies do not exclude all inflammatory disease but lower likelihood of classic seropositive RA.
- MRI is sensitive for erosive/inflammatory change; plain films still standard initial imaging for RA evaluation.
- Point-of-care MSK ultrasound can support inflammatory arthritis when synovitis is subtle.

# Caveats

- Still primary-care scope; limited depth on hypermobility, neuropathic pain, hypogonadism, beriberi, or contested infectious serologies.
- “Noninflammatory” does not mean “benign” or “functional” in a multi-system progressive case.
- Does not address TMJ-dominant flares or extreme joint fragility under trivial load.
- Not medical advice; educational research note only.
