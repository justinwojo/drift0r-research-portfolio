# Summary

- Endocrine Society guideline on adult growth hormone deficiency (AGHD): diagnosis, who to test, and treatment considerations.
- IGF-1 is supportive but **often normal in AGHD**; low IGF-1 is more suggestive when multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies or known structural/genetic disease exist.
- Confirmation usually requires GH stimulation testing unless clear exceptions (e.g., organic panhypopituitarism patterns).
- GH therapy may improve body composition, exercise capacity, skeletal integrity, and QoL in appropriately selected severe GHD; individualize dosing and risk/benefit.

# Key data points

- Testing indicated for structural hypothalamic–pituitary disease, surgery/radiation, head trauma/SAH, or other pituitary deficiencies.
- Isolated low-normal IGF-1 is **not** diagnostic of GHD.
- Benefits most likely in more severe GHD; risks generally low with proper monitoring (glucose, IGF-1, fluid retention, etc.).
- Childhood-onset vs adult-onset pathways differ for retesting.
