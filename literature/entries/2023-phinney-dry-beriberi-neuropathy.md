# Summary

- Case report of thiamine deficiency presenting with dry beriberi peripheral neuropathy and torsades de pointes.
- Reinforces that dry beriberi is a sensory-motor polyneuropathy that can mimic other acute neuropathies, while cardiac involvement may coexist even when the “dry” label is used clinically.
- Stresses including thiamine deficiency in broad differentials for unexplained neuropathy and critical illness arrhythmias.
- Treatment centers on thiamine repletion and supportive care.

# Key data points

- Dry beriberi: peripheral neuropathy (often painful/paresthetic, distal).
- Wet beriberi: high-output heart failure; mixed forms occur.
- Torsades de pointes highlights life-threatening cardiac risk beyond classic wet beriberi edema/failure framing.
- Thiamine deficiency remains under-recognized in non-alcoholic patients.
- Early repletion can reverse or limit injury.
