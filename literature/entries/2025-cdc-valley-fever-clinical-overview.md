# Summary

- Valley fever is infection with *Coccidioides immitis* (typically California) or *C. posadasii* (outside California), acquired by inhaling arthroconidia from disturbed arid soil.
- ~40% of infections are symptomatic (fatigue, cough, dyspnea, headache, night sweats, myalgias, rash) 1–3 weeks after exposure; most primary pulmonary disease is self-limited.
- ~5–10% develop complications/chronic pulmonary disease; **dissemination ~1%** overall but higher in risk groups—bones/joints, soft tissue, and meninges are key extrapulmonary sites.
- Diagnosis mainly serology (EIA IgM/IgG, immunodiffusion, complement fixation); culture/microscopy/PCR adjuncts. Treatment often fluconazole or amphotericin for severe/disseminated disease (see IDSA).

# Key data points

- Endemic: southwestern US (highly endemic southern AZ, CA San Joaquin Valley), parts of Mexico, Central/South America; dust-exposing occupations raise risk.
- Severe/disseminated risk factors: African American or Filipino ancestry, HIV/AIDS, transplant, diabetes, pregnancy, immunosuppressive drugs (including some TNF inhibitors).
- Serology cornerstones: EIA (sensitive), ID (early IgM), CF (IgG; severity/titer trends), LFA rapid antibody test.
- Disseminated osteoarticular disease can be chronic and destructive—biopsy sometimes required.

# Caveats

- CDC overview is operational; IDSA CPG remains treatment backbone.
