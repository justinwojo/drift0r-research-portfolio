# Summary

- 24-month double-blind RCT of oral benfotiamine 300 mg/day vs placebo in type 1 diabetes.
- Primary interest: peripheral nerve function and soluble inflammatory markers.
- Authors report no significant long-term effects on peripheral nerve function or inflammatory markers at this dose/duration in the studied cohort.
- Serves as a caution against over-generalizing short-term symptomatic benefits of benfotiamine in diabetic neuropathy to durable electrophysiologic recovery.

# Key data points

- Dose: 300 mg/day (lower than Drift0r’s 1000 mg and lower than BENDIP high-dose arm).
- Duration: 24 months (much longer than 3–6 week symptom trials); 67 randomized, 59 completed.
- Whole-blood thiamine and thiamine diphosphate rose markedly on benfotiamine (both p < 0.001 vs placebo).
- Population: type 1 diabetes; not absolute thiamine-deficiency beriberi.
- Outcome: no significant differences in peripheral nerve function or soluble inflammatory biomarkers vs placebo.
- Contrasts with shorter symptom-score trials (Stracke 2008; Winkler 1999).

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 2

**Why:** Relevant as a dose/duration comparator for long-term oral benfotiamine safety and nerve outcomes, but population and dose mismatch Drift0r’s deficiency-state recovery narrative. Drift0r’s rapid and complete recovery on 1000 mg in documented low B1 is a different biologic setting than stable diabetic neuropathy at 300 mg.

# Caveats

- Negative result may reflect dose, baseline deficiency status, or endpoint choice—not proof benfotiamine is inert in B1 deficiency.
- Type 1 diabetes cohort only.
- Does not measure repletion of profound thiamine deficiency.
