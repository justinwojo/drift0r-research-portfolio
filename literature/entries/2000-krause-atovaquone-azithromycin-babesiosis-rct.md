# Summary

- Prospective randomized trial (n=58) comparing **atovaquone + azithromycin** vs **clindamycin + quinine** for non-life-threatening *B. microti* babesiosis in southern New England endemic areas.
- Both regimens effective; atovaquone + azithromycin far better tolerated.
- Foundation for preferred oral regimen used in IDSA guidance and in many clinical courses (including regimens containing atovaquone ± azithromycin/clindamycin).

# Key data points

- Arms: atovaquone 750 mg q12h + azithromycin (500 mg day 1, then 250 mg daily) × 7 days (n=40) vs clindamycin 600 mg q8h + quinine 650 mg q8h × 7 days (n=18).
- Adverse effects: **15%** (atovaquone/azithromycin) vs **72%** (clindamycin/quinine); *P*<0.001.
- Common AEs: diarrhea/rash with atovaquone–azithromycin; tinnitus, diarrhea, decreased hearing with clindamycin–quinine.
- Symptoms and parasitemia resolved with both regimens in this non-severe population.
- Note: patient’s regimens used **atovaquone + clindamycin** (± azithromycin)—a real-world combination related to, but not identical to, the RCT arms (clindamycin usually paired with quinine historically; atovaquone usually with azithromycin).

# Caveats

- Non-blinded; smaller quinine arm.
- Does not prove infection in FISH-positive PCR-negative patients.
- Symptom relief from azithromycin/clindamycin can also reflect anti-inflammatory or other antimicrobial effects (Bartonella, etc.).
- Duration studied was short (7 days); patient’s courses were multi-week.
