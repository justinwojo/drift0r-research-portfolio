# Summary

- Retrospective comparative cohort: 96 patients with diverse IgG subclass deficiencies (IgGSDs) vs 270 CVID patients.
- IgGSDs had fewer recurrent lower respiratory infections and bronchiectasis than CVID, but more recurrent mucocutaneous HSV reactivations; autoimmunity patterns differed (arthritis more, cytopenias less).
- Subclass-specific signals: herpes zoster more in IgG3SD; arthritis more in IgG1+3SD; **IgG2+4SD closest to CVID** (LRTI, splenomegaly, ITP, lower switched memory B cells).
- Argues diagnosing specific IgGSD subtypes has differential clinical significance—not a single lump diagnosis.

# Key data points

- IgGSD ≠ mild CVID automatically; infection geography and autoimmunity differ.
- IgG2+4SD is the phenotype most resembling CVID and may warrant closer surveillance.
- Supports disorder-specific follow-up and prophylaxis strategies rather than one-size subclass labeling.
- Large modern comparator cohort relative to older subclass case series.

- Retrospective specialty-center cohort—referral bias.
- Patient’s IgG4↑/IgG1↓ dual pattern is not the paper’s primary bins.
- Functional antibody/vaccine challenge data remain essential for individual decisions.
- Research catalog only.
