# Summary

- Magnesium is a critical cofactor (≥300 enzymatic reactions), stabilizes ATP, and supports neuromuscular, cardiac, metabolic, and bone physiology (~60% of body Mg in bone).
- Hypomagnesemia arises from low intake, GI losses/malabsorption, renal wasting (diuretics, alcohol, genetic), and drugs (notably long-term **PPIs**, some antibiotics/chemo).
- Clinical features: cramps, tremor, tetany, seizures, arrhythmias, and secondary **hypokalemia/hypocalcemia** that may be refractory until Mg is repleted.
- Management: oral salts for mild–moderate deficit; IV MgSO4 for severe/symptomatic disease; fix underlying drivers; monitor for hypermagnesemia in CKD.

# Key data points

- Serum Mg is a poor total-body store marker; ionized Mg and 24-h urine Mg help phenotype renal vs GI losses.
- Concurrent electrolyte disorders (K+, Ca2+) are the rule, not the exception.
- Bone relevance: chronic low Mg associates with osteoporosis risk and impaired PTH/vitamin D axis signaling in physiologic literature cited by the review.
- Hypermagnesemia is mainly iatrogenic or renal-failure related (antacids/laxatives/IV Mg).
