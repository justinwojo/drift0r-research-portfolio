# Summary

- Expert consensus (orthopedic/neuro spine surgery, endocrinology, rheumatology) on identifying and treating poor bone health **before elective spinal reconstruction**.
- Recommends formal bone health evaluation for all patients ≥65 and for younger patients with risk factors (glucocorticoids, prior fracture, limited mobility, known low BMD, etc.).
- Prefers anabolic agents as first-line pharmacologic optimization when not contraindicated because they build bone (vs antiresorptives that primarily preserve).
- Suggests ≥2 months preoperative and ≥8 months postoperative medical therapy windows in the consensus algorithm—highly relevant to timing of any future L5–S1 fusion.

# Key data points

- Bone health should be considered in **every** elective spinal reconstruction candidate.
- DXA hip preferred for availability; opportunistic CT Hounsfield units of vertebrae can flag poor bone quality when DXA pending.
- Untreated osteoporosis associates with instrumentation failure, proximal junctional kyphosis, pseudarthrosis, and revision.
- Anabolic therapy (teriparatide/abaloparatide; romosozumab considered in practice) prioritized pre-op when appropriate; antiresorptives still used based on specialist judgment.
- Coordinate spine surgeon + bone specialist; delay elective reconstruction when bone is critically compromised and optimization is feasible.

# Caveats

- Level 5 expert consensus (Delphi), not RCT-based GRADE throughout.
- Evidence for exact 2-month pre / 8-month post durations is consensus, not definitive trial data.
- SCS already placed; systemic joint flare post-implant is outside this document’s scope.
- Male, multi-system secondary osteoporosis still needs disease-specific workup in parallel.
