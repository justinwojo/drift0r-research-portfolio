# Summary

- Retrospective review of patients with documented hypocupremia and hematologic abnormalities, highlighting how often copper deficiency is missed.
- Classic blood findings: anemia (variable MCV), neutropenia, occasionally thrombocytopenia; marrow may show vacuolization of precursors and ring sideroblasts—**MDS mimic**.
- Neurologic copper deficiency myeloneuropathy frequently coexists but hematologic disease can dominate the presentation.
- Correction of copper deficiency reverses hematologic abnormalities; failure to recognize the cause leads to inappropriate MDS pathways.

# Key data points

- Copper deficiency is an established but under-recognized reversible cause of cytopenias.
- Zinc excess, gastric surgery, and malabsorption are recurring etiologies in series (see also lit-0244–lit-0246).
- Practical lab set: serum copper + ceruloplasmin + zinc + CBC ± bone marrow if already performed for “MDS.”
- Hematologic recovery after repletion is expected; use response as diagnostic confirmation when pretest probability is high.

# Caveats

- Referral-center retrospective series—spectrum bias toward more severe hematologic presentations.
- Normal CBC does not exclude neurologic copper deficiency (see lit-0242).
