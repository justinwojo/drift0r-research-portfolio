# Summary

- Clinical review of **secondary causes of osteoporosis** with emphasis on when and how to expand the laboratory evaluation beyond “basic” bone labs.
- Covers endocrine (hyperparathyroidism, hyperthyroidism, hypogonadism, Cushing, vitamin D), GI malabsorption/celiac, renal hypercalciuria, marrow disorders, medications (glucocorticoids, aromatase inhibitors, etc.), and lifestyle factors.
- Aligns with AACE-style practice: young men and premenopausal women with low BMD or fragility fractures warrant **systematic secondary workup**, not reflexive primary-OP labeling.
- Useful checklist companion to guideline documents already in the corpus.

# Key data points

- Secondary osteoporosis prevalence is highest in men and younger patients—exactly the Drift0r demographic.
- 24-hour urine calcium is a high-yield test for idiopathic hypercalciuria as a bone-loss driver.
- Sex steroids in men, celiac serology when indicated, SPEP/UPEP when indicated, and TSH/PTH/25-OH D are core panel elements.
- Treating the secondary cause can slow loss and modify drug sequencing (anabolic vs antiresorptive).

# Similarity to Drift0r case (0–5)

**Score:** 5

**Why:** Age-38 man with T-scores to −4.3, ongoing hip loss, CTX upper-normal, documented hypercalciuria, historical hypogonadism, and fusion deferred—textbook indication for exhaustive secondary evaluation and incomplete “primary OP” labeling.

# Caveats

- Review/narrative; checklist details should be cross-checked with current AACE/ES guidelines (lit-0217, lit-0001).
- Completing a checklist does not exclude monogenic EOOP—pair with genetic panel thinking (Collet, Laine, van Dijk).
- Not medical advice.
