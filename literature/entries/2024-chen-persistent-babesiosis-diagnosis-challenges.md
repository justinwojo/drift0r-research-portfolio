# Summary

- Reviews epidemiology, mechanisms, and management challenges of **persistent and relapsing human babesiosis** with **low-grade parasitemia**, including immunocompromised and occasional non-immunosuppressed reports.
- Argues diagnosis is difficult when parasites are scarce: routine smear and even PCR can miss intermittent low-level infection, prompting need for **repeated, prolonged monitoring** when clinical suspicion is high.
- Discusses anti-babesial therapy challenges and incomplete clearance in selected hosts—without endorsing nonstandard specialty assays as gold standards.

# Key data points

- Confirmed human babesiosis diagnosis in mainstream practice rests on **blood smear and/or PCR** (IDSA-aligned), with serology for exposure context.
- Low-grade/persistent parasitemia is best documented in asplenia, B-cell immunodeficiency, rituximab, and similar states—still reported rarely outside those settings.
- Intermittent sampling can yield false negatives; a single negative test does not always exclude infection when pretest probability is high.
- **Commercial FISH LDTs** (e.g., IGeneX-type) are **not** established as IDSA-recommended confirmatory tests; independent multi-center validation and adjudication of FISH+/PCR− results remain limited (see also Shah 2020 COI-heavy method paper already in corpus).
