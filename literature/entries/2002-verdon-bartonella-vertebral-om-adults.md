# Summary

- Two adult cases of *Bartonella henselae* **vertebral osteomyelitis**—one patient HIV-infected—expanding the entity beyond classic pediatric cat-scratch osteomyelitis.
- Diagnosis established by **direct identification of the organism** in one case and **seroconversion** in the other; both recovered within about three months.
- Emphasizes contiguous soft-tissue inflammation with vertebral bone infection as a recognizable pattern.

# Key data points

- Adult presentation of B. henselae osteomyelitis is uncommon but documented.
- Routine bacterial cultures often fail; serology and/or molecular testing of tissue/aspirate are decisive.
- Contiguous soft-tissue abscess/inflammation is frequent with vertebral involvement in the broader Bartonella osteomyelitis literature.
- Clinical recovery can occur with (and sometimes without aggressive) antimicrobial courses—treatment remains individualized.

# Caveats

- n=2 case report, not a series or trial.
- Vertebral focus ≠ forearm soft-tissue nodules.
- HIV-associated case differs immunologically from presumed immunocompetent host.
- Does not validate specialty IgM-only Bartonella diagnostics.
