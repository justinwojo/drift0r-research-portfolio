# Summary

- Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH; pseudotumor cerebri) is elevated ICP without mass lesion, typically with papilledema, in a demographic historically enriched for young women with obesity—but men and non-obese patients occur.
- Cardinal features: headache, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus, papilledema; neck/back/radicular pain can accompany high CSF pressure.
- Long-term morbidity is often **chronic headache even after ICP improves**, plus risk of permanent visual field loss if untreated.
- Modified Dandy criteria + ophthalmology assessment guide diagnosis; acetazolamide and weight management are mainstays in typical IIH (specialist care).

# Key data points

- Up to large minorities report chronic headache at 1 year despite ICP normalization in observational work.
- Male sex is a risk marker for more severe visual outcomes in some series.
- Back/neck pain is recognized but IIH does not cause generalized polyarthralgia or osteoporosis.
- Secondary intracranial hypertension (venous sinus thrombosis, drugs, endocrine) must be excluded.

- Steroids, tetracyclines, vitamin A derivatives are secondary-IIH associations in general literature.
