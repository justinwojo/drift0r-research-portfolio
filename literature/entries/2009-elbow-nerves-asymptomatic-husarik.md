# Summary

- Prospective MRI evaluation of elbow nerves in **60 asymptomatic volunteers**.
- Quantifies how often increased fluid-sensitive signal and anatomic variants appear in people **without** clinical neuropathy.
- Establishes key pitfall: **ulnar-nerve T2 hyperintensity at the cubital tunnel is common in asymptomatic elbows**.

# Key data points

- Increased SI on fluid-sensitive sequences in the **ulnar nerve in 60% (36/60)** of asymptomatic subjects.
- Increased SI **never** observed in median or radial nerves in this cohort (under same protocol).
- Anconeus epitrochlearis in 23%; hypertrophic leash of Henry in 15%; ulnar subluxation in 2%.
- Provides normal caliber ranges; isolated signal change without enlargement is especially nonspecific.
- Direct implication: do not diagnose cubital tunnel syndrome from T2 hyperintensity alone.

# Caveats

- Asymptomatic volunteers ≠ patients with forearm pain/nodules; still, base rate of signal change is high.
- Does not address tendon-region palpable lumps or periosteal humeral edema.
- Higher-field/neurography protocols can change signal conspicuity.
