# Summary

- Contemporary reappraisal of clinical associations claimed for hereditary α-tryptasemia (HαT) since its description.
- Early framing treated HαT as a syndromic disorder with connective-tissue abnormalities, autonomic dysfunction, and mast-cell activation features; later work shows HαT is a **common genetic trait** (~5–7% Western populations) and a **modifier of mast cell–mediated reactions**.
- More recent evidence weakens previously held associations with congenital hypermobility and POTS; strengthens links to clonal and nonclonal mast-cell disorders and anaphylaxis severity (tryptase genotype as biomarker).
- Frames HαT at the high end of a spectrum of α-tryptase expression rather than a rare Mendelian “syndrome” alone.

# Key data points

- HαT = extra α-encoding *TPSAB1* copy number → higher basal serum tryptase.
- Heterotetrameric tryptases with unique functional activity explain mechanistic interest beyond simple biomarker status.
- Previously reported connective-tissue and dysautonomia links are **not consistently supported** in newer controlled analyses.
- Stronger evidence for modifying severity of mast-cell reactions (including venom anaphylaxis) and co-occurrence with mast-cell disease phenotypes.

# Caveats

- Appraisal/narrative synthesis—not a new population genetics study.
- Does not explain markedly low BMD for age, hypercalciuria/SLC7A9, IgG1 low/IgG4 high, or thiamine deficiency.
- Symptom attribution remains individual; genotype ≠ syndrome.
