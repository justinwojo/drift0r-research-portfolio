# Summary

- Pharmacokinetic study of single oral thiamine hydrochloride doses of 100 mg, 500 mg, and 1500 mg in healthy subjects.
- Demonstrates that high oral doses produce substantial increases in blood thiamine, with nonlinear absorption characteristics at pharmacologic doses.
- Contextualizes IV thiamine dosing used for deficiency syndromes (often 100 mg daily up to 500 mg TID) versus oral alternatives.
- Supports feasibility of high-dose oral repletion when IV access is unavailable or for step-down therapy.

# Key data points

- Doses studied: 100 / 500 / 1500 mg oral thiamine HCl.
- Active transport dominates at nutritional doses; passive diffusion contributes at high pharmacologic doses.
- Blood levels rise with dose; absorption efficiency is not strictly proportional at high doses.
- Clinical deficiency treatment traditionally starts IV in severe neurologic disease.
- Oral high-dose regimens are used in some metabolic enzyme-defect settings.

**Score:** 2

# Caveats

- Healthy volunteers, not deficient patients.
- Thiamine hydrochloride ≠ benfotiamine (different absorption profile; benfotiamine typically more bioavailable).
- No clinical neuropathy outcomes.
