# Summary

- Cross-sectional comparison of regional BMD and determinants in 70 adult men with KS versus 71 age-matched healthy controls.
- KS patients had universally lower BMD at spine, hip, and forearm; frank osteoporosis (very low Z-scores) was more notable at the forearm than at axial sites.
- Multivariate predictors of BMD included muscle strength, history of ever receiving testosterone, age at diagnosis, SHBG, and bone turnover markers—**not concurrent androgen levels**.
- 25-OH vitamin D was lower in KS; secondary hyperparathyroidism signals were present—dietary/sun exposure differences may contribute.

# Key data points

- Design: university clinical research center; DXA + biochemical bone markers, vitamin D metabolites, PTH, sex hormones, growth factors, anthropometry, muscle strength.
- KS BMD reduced at all measured regions vs controls; more KS men had forearm Z-score < −2 (15 vs 2).
- Muscle strength (biceps and quadriceps) lower in KS and independently associated with BMD.
- Serum testosterone itself was not a direct independent BMD predictor in multivariate models; prior TRT history and earlier diagnosis were.

**Score:** 2–3

**Why:**
- Reinforces that hypogonadal/KS-related bone disease is multifactorial (muscle, vitamin D, timing of treatment)—relevant to severe OP workup.
- Does **not** diagnose KS in the case; it calibrates expectations if karyotype were positive (BMD reduction common; T number alone incomplete).
- Case’s severity of the low BMD may exceed typical KS cohort averages described here (“frank osteoporosis not common”), favoring additional secondary causes.

# Caveats

- Cross-sectional; cannot prove causality of predictors.
- Treated and untreated KS mixed; TRT formulations/duration heterogeneous.
- Generalizability limited to diagnosed KS (selection bias toward more symptomatic men).
